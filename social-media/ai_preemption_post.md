He says AI policy "cannot be the 'Wild West,' with 50 states writing 50 different sets of rules." Last year he voted to make it zero states, for ten years. The Senate struck that provision 99 to 1.

On Sept 23 he posted that "Congress has put hundreds of hours of bipartisan work" into AI and that "America needs one clear federal framework." Here is the record.

CLAIM: "50 states writing 50 different sets of rules" cannot stand; "I work on AI in AI policy every day."
VERDICT: DOCUMENTED PATTERN

→ May 22, 2025: he voted Yea on the House budget bill whose section 43201(c) said no state "may enforce, during the 10-year period beginning on the date of the enactment of this Act, any law or regulation" on AI. New York's AI safety law would have been unenforceable. The Senate removed it July 1, 2025, 99 to 1.
→ AI bills he has cosponsored this Congress, by title: none. Last Congress: two, both joined March 5, 2024, neither passed.
→ His 397 press releases since 2023 mention AI four times, all in passing. None announces an AI position or bill.
→ The 2024 bipartisan House AI task force had 24 members. He was not one.
→ His committee held one AI-titled hearing in 2026. The House itself was in session 377 hours through Aug 31 and voted on 65 days, the fewest in a midterm year since 1994.

The fair reading: a bipartisan framework bill from Reps. Obernolte and Trahan exists, and its preemption language was narrowed after criticism. A patchwork of state laws is a concern both parties share. Staff hours can't be counted, so "hundreds of hours" is uncheckable rather than false.

The pattern: this is the eighth time since December he has backed federal override of a state or local rule, five of them aimed at New York: gas hookups, supplement sales, AI, data privacy, the ten-year AI ban, a New York City bus ordinance, AM radio rules, and now "one clear federal framework." On Sept 4 he wrote of the city's members wanting local control: "Rules for thee, not for me."
