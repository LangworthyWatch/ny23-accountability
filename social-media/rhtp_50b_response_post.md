Rep. Langworthy says his tax law "created a $50 billion Rural Health Transformation Program to strengthen NY23 hospitals." The same law cut federal Medicaid by $911 billion, about $137 billion of it in rural areas. The fund makes up about 37% of the rural cut.

This is his sixth announcement of this fund since December. Here's the rest of the picture, from KFF's analysis of the Congressional Budget Office's numbers.

CLAIM: "Our Working Families Tax Cuts created a $50 billion Rural Health Transformation Program."
VERDICT: MISSING CONTEXT

What holds up:
→ The fund is real: $50 billion over five years, through fiscal 2030.
→ New York's rules for its first round give priority to maternity care, as his post says.
→ Nine of the state's Sept 4 awards serve NY-23 counties, including St. James, Jones Memorial and Westfield Memorial hospitals.

What the post leaves out:
→ The Senate added the fund "just prior to passage," according to KFF. He voted for the final law.
→ The same law cut federal Medicaid by $911 billion. KFF puts the rural share at about $137 billion, so the fund offsets about 37%.
→ The fund ends in fiscal 2030. KFF finds 64% of the law's Medicaid cuts land after that.
→ NY-23 has eight hospitals on the Fiscal Policy Institute's at-risk list, the most of any district in New York.

The fair reading: the fund does help rural hospitals, and the money is reaching the district. But it replaces about a third of what the same law takes out of rural Medicaid, and it runs out before most of the cuts arrive.

A $50 billion fund inside a $911 billion Medicaid cut.
