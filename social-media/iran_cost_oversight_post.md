A nonpartisan federal agency just put a number on the Iran war: $38 billion, and the meter is still running.

The Congressional Budget Office published it September 15.

THE COST
→ $38 billion through August 1, and that is the Defense Department alone
→ Another $2 to $3 billion for every month it continues
→ $13.1 billion of it replaces missile interceptors. CBO says the U.S. has used between half and two thirds of that stockpile since June 2025, and rebuilding it will take at least five years

WHAT IT LOOKS LIKE HERE
CBO found the war raised the price of gasoline, diesel and jet fuel, and that because the price of almost every product reflects shipping costs, it pushed up the price of most goods.
→ Diesel in our region: $4.10 a gallon the week before the war, $6.31 on September 14
→ New York gasoline: $2.89 to $4.34 over the same stretch
CBO attributes part of that increase to the war, not all of it. Fuel prices move for many reasons.

THE VOTES
→ The House has voted 8 times to end this operation
→ He voted No 8 times
→ 3 of those resolutions passed the House anyway, the most recent on September 15, by 220 to 204

WHAT HE HAS SAID ABOUT IT
CBO could not get the Pentagon to answer basic questions. Its words: "DoD did not respond to CBO's requests for information."

We searched every press release his office has issued since the war began on February 28. There are 35. Not one of them mentions the war, its cost, or the votes to end it. He made no remarks in the September 14 floor debate either. His office has put out Iran statements before, including one on U.S. airstrikes on Iran in June 2025.

On September 10 he said: "I have oversight over the entire federal government in that respect."

One more line worth reading twice: CBO's $38 billion does not include future veterans' health care and disability compensation. That bill comes later.

To be fair about it: this was close to a party line vote, and all seven New York Republicans voted the same way he did. What is his alone is the silence.
