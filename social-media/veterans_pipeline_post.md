The Pentagon's public death count for the Iran war went down last week, from 18 to 14. One of the names that came off belonged to Sgt. Angel Rampersad, a New Yorker.

Gov. Hochul: "Changing a website does not rewrite the truth of her sacrifice."

The Pentagon calls it a technical fault. What is not in dispute: the administration told Congress on May 1 that the operation was "terminated." Strikes since are being called "overseas operations." Rep. Thomas Massie, a Republican, calls it an "absurd ruse" and says 90+ days without authorization breaks the law. The same relabeling also decides which war a casualty gets counted under.

Nick Langworthy sits on the House Oversight Committee, in the majority. That committee investigates federal agencies. He could request a hearing. We found none, and no statement.

The rest of the record:

→ 5 votes to keep an unauthorized Iran war going, plus 2 on Lebanon. Two of the Iran measures passed the House anyway. One failed on a 212 to 212 tie.

→ More than 10,000 veterans lost their homes after VA ended its mortgage rescue program in May 2025. The mortgage industry warned a House committee two months earlier: "Foreclosure. Period." Congress authorized a replacement that July. VA did not open it until June 15, 2026. The Mortgage Bankers Association puts VA foreclosures at a nine-year high.

→ Sleep apnea pays a flat 50% today when a CPAP is required. Under the bill he voted 7 times to protect, future claims drop to 10% when treatment leaves incomplete relief, and 0% when symptoms are gone. Existing ratings are safe. The people who pay are deployed right now.

→ 1,102 fewer VBA claims examiners, 67,849 claims already past 125 days.

→ VA research staffing down 5.1% in the FY2027 request. Research is how the next PACT Act ever gets proven.

What cuts the other way: he voted to RAISE toxic exposure funding, and for the bill that put VA research above last year. He has no PACT Act votes, that law passed before he took office. And no vote on the mortgage program either, it passed both chambers by voice vote.

Don't take our word for it. Every figure traces to a roll call, an IG report, or a VA filing.
