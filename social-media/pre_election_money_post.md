In the two weeks before early voting, 13 cents of every dollar his campaign raised came from the district. Half came from PACs. A third came through AIPAC's PAC, from donors in Westchester and Manhattan.

VERDICT: DOCUMENTED PATTERN

→ The $53,220 he raised October 1 to 14: $26,000 from PACs (SpaceX, UPS, the CPAs' association, BASF, AbbVie, the NRA Victory Fund). $16,600 from 18 donors whose gifts are marked "earmarked through" AIPAC's PAC. $6,850 from six donors with addresses in the district. $3,770 from everyone else.
→ Where it went: a $551,000 TV buy on October 9, a $22,900 poll two days before early voting, and $25,000 to the Nassau County Republican Committee on Long Island, the largest payment to any county party in his cycle. His own Erie County committee got $11,500 in two years. The filing says only "political contribution."
→ October 15 to 29, 48-hour notices added $79,500: $42,000 from PACs and firms, $5,500 from three lobbyists, $6,000 from three district donors.
→ The cycle: $2.30 million, 53.8 percent from PACs, at most 17 cents per dollar from the district. Through AIPAC's PAC all cycle: 36 donors, $42,400, none in the district, about 4 percent of his individual money. The PAC itself gave the $10,000 maximum.

All of it is legal and disclosed. Earmarking through a PAC is how AIPAC's members give; $152,913 of his money came through WinRed the same way. October TV buys are what the money is for. We do not know what the Nassau payment was for, and we do not guess.

The pattern we documented on October 16, carried to the end.
