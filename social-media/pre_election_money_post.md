$551,000 on television. $25,000 to Nassau County's Republican Party. $6,850 from donors in the district. His campaign's last filings before the election, read line by line.

VERDICT: DOCUMENTED PATTERN

→ October 1 to 14, he raised $53,220 and spent $597,785. Cash on hand fell for the first time this cycle, to $1,994,177.
→ Of the $25,700 in itemized individual money, $16,600 came from 18 donors in Westchester and New York City whose gifts are marked "earmarked through" AIPAC's PAC. Six contributions came from addresses in the district, totaling $6,850.
→ PACs gave $26,000: SpaceX, UPS and the accountants' association at $5,000 each, then BASF, AbbVie, Vertex and the NRA Victory Fund.
→ The spending: a $551,000 TV buy on October 9, his first of the cycle. A $22,900 poll two days before early voting. And $25,000 to the Nassau County Republican Committee on Long Island, the largest payment to any county party in his cycle. His own Erie County committee got $11,500 in two years. The filing lists no purpose beyond "political contribution."
→ October 15 to 29, 48-hour notices added $79,500: $42,000 from PACs and firms, three donors from the district, three who list their job as lobbyist.
→ The cycle: $2.30 million, 53.8 percent from PACs, at most 17 cents per dollar from the district. Across the whole cycle, 36 donors gave $42,400 through AIPAC's PAC, none of them in the district, about 4 percent of his individual money; the PAC itself gave the $10,000 maximum.

What is ordinary here: all of it is legal and disclosed. Earmarking through a PAC is how AIPAC's members give, and $152,913 of his money came through WinRed the same way. A TV buy in October is what the money is for. Transfers to the NRCC ($125,000) and 33 other candidates ($142,000) are what safe-seat members do. We do not know what the Nassau payment was for, and we do not guess.

The pattern we documented on October 16, carried to the end: most of the money from PACs, a sliver from the district, and in the last two weeks, one check to Long Island.
