Rep. Langworthy says "we took swift and decisive action against 50 high-risk Medicaid providers." The numbers match CMS. The action was taken by CMS, HHS's inspector general and the states, not his committee.

CLAIM: "As a member of the Energy and Commerce Committee... we took swift and decisive action against 50 high-risk Medicaid providers, halting over $203 million in potentially improper payments."
VERDICT: MISLEADING

What checks out:
→ The figures come from a July 28 CMS announcement about its Medicaid Fraud War Room: 50 providers, about $203.3 million, in just under 90 days.

What the post leaves out:
→ Who acted: CMS says the War Room brings together CMS, HHS's Office of Inspector General, state Medicaid agencies and federal law enforcement. The inspector general issued 42 exclusion notices. States took 15 actions.
→ What his committee did: oversight. Its leaders sent letters to ten states, including New York, and its Oversight and Investigations Subcommittee held three fraud hearings this year. He isn't on that subcommittee, and the hearing rosters don't list him at any of the three.
→ What the $203 million measures: what those 50 providers were paid since January 1, 2025, starting nearly 16 months before the War Room launched. CMS didn't say how much was actually withheld or recovered.
→ Where the cases stand: most actions were notices of intent to exclude, which providers had 30 days to contest. CMS hasn't said how many became final.

The fair reading: the enforcement is real, and so is the committee's oversight. He has his own bipartisan Medicaid provider-screening bill from 2025, still in committee. "Halting" mirrors CMS's own "stopped." And "we" can be read broadly, though the post opens "As a member of the Energy and Commerce Committee."

His committee asked questions. The executive branch and the states took the action.
