More than 10,000 veterans have lost their homes to foreclosure since VA shut down its mortgage rescue program in May 2025. It is the highest pace of VA loan foreclosures in a decade. Another 90,000 are behind on payments or already in the foreclosure process.

The part that is hard to read: the industry said out loud what would happen, before it happened.

At a House Veterans' Affairs hearing in March 2025, the Mortgage Bankers Association was asked what would follow if VA scrapped the program. The answer: "Foreclosure. Period. That's really where it's gonna come to."

Two months later VA ended it anyway.

→ May 1, 2025: VASP ends. No replacement exists.
→ July 30, 2025: Congress authorizes a replacement. It becomes law.
→ June 15, 2026: VA finally opens it, ten months after the law passed.
→ Nov. 28, 2026: the deadline for servicers to finish adopting it, so for some veterans the gap still has not closed.

Thirteen months with no backstop. The Mortgage Bankers Association now puts VA foreclosures at their highest level since 2017.

What we are not saying: that the shutdown caused all 10,000. NPR reports it is unclear how many could have been saved by the program. Industry insiders told them some of those veterans had enough disability pay or other income to have kept their homes.

And in fairness, this did not start with this administration. The mess began in 2022 when a pandemic relief program was ended abruptly, trapping veterans who were then told they could not resume normal payments without repaying everything at once. The rescue program was the fix for that. It was cancelled about a year after it started working.

There is also no Langworthy vote to point to here. The replacement passed both chambers by voice vote, so no member has a recorded position on it. What we did not find was any statement from him about any of it.

Don't take our word for it. VA's own circular ending the program is public.
