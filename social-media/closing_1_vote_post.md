One vote, cast twice. $911 billion out of Medicaid. 450,000 New Yorkers off coverage. A hospital in his district on the at-risk list.

Early voting is open. Over eleven months we have checked 176 of his statements against the record. This week we are posting what that record adds up to, one piece a day. Start with the vote.

Rep. Langworthy voted for H.R. 1, the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, on May 22, 2025 (Roll 145, Yea) and again on July 3, 2025 (Roll 190, Aye). By the Congressional Budget Office's accounting, the law cut federal Medicaid spending by $911 billion and SNAP and related programs by $206 billion.

What that meant here:

→ 450,000 New Yorkers lost Essential Plan coverage on July 1, 2026, by the state's own count, when the law ended the program's expansion. Between May 3 and June 29 he posted 30 times about the beagles. About this: zero.
→ Schuyler Hospital in Montour Falls, 25 beds, was flagged on a published at-risk list for the Medicaid cuts. He has not mentioned it.
→ Three nursing homes in Chautauqua County closed or announced closure. Heritage Village in Gerry was operating below the federal RN staffing floor that Section 71111 of the same law blocked from enforcement until 2034. No public statement on any of the three.
→ The $76.2 million rural health fund he posted about three times in one week is one line in the same law: less than one-hundredth of one percent of the Medicaid cut. He called it a "historic investment."
→ His Oversight Committee has held no hearing on where that fund's money went.

The fair reading: the rural fund is real money, and the law's defenders argue the Medicaid changes target eligibility and work requirements rather than care. The at-risk list and the coverage count are what they are.
