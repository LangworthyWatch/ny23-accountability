The Post-Journal endorsed him. It also said his opponent holds town halls and he does not. The graphic cut that part.

The Jamestown paper's October 24 editorial is being passed around as a graphic with one paragraph of praise. Here is what the same editorial says in the paragraphs the graphic skipped:

→ Aaron Gies "is to be commended" for "numerous town hall meetings from Erie to Tioga County." The gatherings were "well attended" and "greatly appreciated by those seeking an alternative to the incumbent."
→ "For his part, U.S. Rep. Nick Langworthy has not been as amenable to that process."
→ The same editor, in his own column in May 2025: "Since being elected, he said he was not going to hold town hall events." And: "Having control of the message is more important than allowing an unruly crowd to steal the show."

The paragraph the graphic does quote credits him with the Chautauqua Lake study and Barcelona Harbor dredging. The appropriations records:

→ The lake study money, $500,000, was requested by him and by Senators Schumer and Gillibrand. The disclosure table marks it as originating in the Senate. The study was authorized in 2018, before he was elected. The money to finish it is a pending request.
→ The harbor was dredged twice. The 2023 dredging ran on $1.15 million the senators secured in 2022, before he took office, with no House request on the record. The 2025 dredging ran on a $7.5 million earmark he requested jointly with both senators, announced jointly.
→ Any member of the House can request this kind of funding for projects in the district. He did, and he voted for the bill. That is the job.

The fair reading: the endorsement is the paper's to give, he did request both earmarks, and he showed up at the harbor. The fentanyl bill the editorial also mentions checks out: he cosponsored it and voted for it twice.

The paper's own words, the disclosure tables and the Corps records are on the entry. Both papers are owned by Ogden Newspapers of Wheeling, West Virginia; no payment from his campaign or office to either paper appears in federal records.
