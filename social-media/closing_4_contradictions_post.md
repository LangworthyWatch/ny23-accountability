He sits on the committee that decides what the House is allowed to vote on. Three times, he voted to stop the vote.

Thirteen members of the House Rules Committee decide which bills and amendments reach the floor. He is the only New Yorker on it. Here is what he did with the seat.

→ EPSTEIN FILES. He wrote to constituents, twice, that "I have never opposed the release of the documents." In July 2025 he voted in that committee to block the amendment that would have forced their release. He never signed the petition that finally forced the vote.
→ TARIFFS. A Jamestown furniture plant closed and named tariffs in its filing. Three times he voted in that committee to keep the House from voting on ending the tariffs. When the vote finally came in February, he voted to keep them.
→ VETERANS. A veterans bill paired a popular benefit with cuts to future disability ratings that the VFW and DAV opposed. He voted in that committee against letting the House pass the benefit without the cuts.

Committee members are expected to vote with their leadership on these procedural questions. That is the point. The seat decides what gets a vote, and this is how he used it.

The committee's own reports record each vote by name. They are linked on the entry.
