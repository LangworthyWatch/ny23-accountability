He holds New York's only seat on the committee that decides what the House gets to vote on. Three times, he used it to keep the vote from happening.

Day four of the record: what he said, then how he voted. All from committee reports, the House Clerk and his own letters to constituents.

EPSTEIN FILES
→ Said, in letters to constituents in September 2025 and March 2026: "I have never opposed the release of the documents."
→ Voted, July 17, 2025, in the Rules Committee: No on the binding amendment to force the Justice Department to release them. The next day he voted to advance a non-binding resolution instead.
→ Never signed the discharge petition that finally forced the vote. It reached 218 signatures on November 12, 2025 without him.

RUSSIA
→ Said, February 24, 2022: "Every moment that goes by without severe consequences is a threat to the entire globe."
→ Voted Nay on the $60 billion Ukraine supplemental (April 20, 2024). Voted Yea on the 2026 sanctions law (September 16, 2026), which carries a presidential waiver. On October 16 the President used the waiver to set the new sanctions aside.

TARIFFS
→ Toured a Jamestown manufacturer to praise "American made." A Jamestown plant's insolvency filing named tariffs among the causes of its closure.
→ Rules Committee record votes 37, 54 and 55: Nay on letting the House vote to end the tariffs. On the floor, February 11, 2026 (Roll 65): Nay on ending the Canada tariff emergency. It passed 219 to 211 without him.

VETERANS
→ H.R. 9237 paired a popular benefit with cuts to future sleep-apnea and tinnitus ratings that the VFW and DAV opposed.
→ June 23, 2026, Rules Committee: No on letting the House vote for the benefit without the cuts. Then voted to close the rule.

The fair reading: Rules Committee members are expected to vote with leadership on procedure, and these were procedural votes about whether the House may vote, not up-or-down votes on the policy. That is exactly the point. The seat decides what gets voted on. He has it, and this is what he did with it.
