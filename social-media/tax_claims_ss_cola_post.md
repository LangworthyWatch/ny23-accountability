He says his tax law "eliminated taxes on Social Security for middle class seniors." The law never changed the rule that decides how much of your benefit is taxable. The Tax Policy Center estimates about half of Social Security recipients will still pay some income tax on their benefits.

We checked seven tax claims from his Oct 6 debate and his official page this week. Some hold up. The one aimed at seniors does not.

CLAIM: The tax law "eliminated taxes on Social Security for middle class seniors."
VERDICT: FALSE
→ The section of the tax code that taxes benefits "was not changed," per the Congressional Research Service. The law added a $6,000 deduction for most taxpayers 65 and over, for 2025 through 2028. Beneficiaries under 65 (about 1 in 5) are not eligible for it themselves.

CLAIM: "Our Working Families Tax Cuts deliver $6,000 more relief."
VERDICT: MISLEADING
→ $6,000 is the deduction, not the money saved. For a single senior in the 10 to 22 percent brackets, it is worth $600 to $1,320 a year. If you owe no income tax, it is worth $0.

CLAIM: "A 3.6% COLA means $75 more a month for retirees."
VERDICT: TRUE
→ That matches Social Security's Oct 14 announcement. The adjustment is automatic, tied to inflation.

CLAIM: "We doubled the child tax credit." (debate)
VERDICT: MISLEADING
→ The 2017 tax law doubled it, before he took office. The law he voted for raised it from $2,000 to $2,200 and made it permanent.

CLAIM: "No tax on tips, no tax on overtime."
VERDICT: MISLEADING
→ Both are capped deductions that end after 2028. For overtime, only the extra "half" of time-and-a-half counts.

CLAIM: "Tax refund checks went up 11% year over year." (debate)
VERDICT: TRUE
→ IRS: the average refund was up 11.5% through May 8.

The fair reading: the law did cut taxes for many seniors and families, and several of his numbers are right. But "eliminated taxes on Social Security" is not what the law does, and seniors planning next year's taxes should know the difference.

Don't take our word for it. Check the law, the IRS and his own words, all cited in the entry.
