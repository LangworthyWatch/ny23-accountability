This time, the wording holds up. Rep. Langworthy says he's "proud to have helped secure" FEMA grants for Olean firefighters' radios and Ellicottville's air packs. "Helped" is accurate, with some context.

CLAIM: "I'm proud to have helped secure a $70,714 FEMA Federal Emergency Management Agency grant for 15 lifesaving radios for Olean firefighters."
CLAIM: "I'm proud to have helped secure a $210,000 federal grant that delivered 18 state-of-the-art air packs to the Ellicottville Fire Department."
VERDICT: MOSTLY TRUE

What holds up:
→ Both are real FEMA Assistance to Firefighters Grants, and the amounts match the records.
→ He voted for the spending laws that funded both grant rounds, in 2024 and 2025.
→ His office has hosted FEMA grant-writing seminars for local fire departments.

What the posts leave out:
→ Who picked the winners: the departments wrote the applications, and FEMA scored them by computer and by panels of fire-service reviewers before making awards. The process gives members of Congress no role in choosing.
→ The Ellicottville grant's award period began in September 2025. The post doesn't say when it was awarded.
→ Olean also moved to apply for a fire engine this year. We found no announced award for it.

The fair reading: these weren't earmarks, and he didn't claim they were. "Helped secure" describes a real, if indirect, role. Compare "I secured," which he has used for money that wasn't law yet.

Credit where it's due: the wording matches the record.
