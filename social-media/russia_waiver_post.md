In 2022, Nick Langworthy said "every moment that goes by without severe consequences" for Russia's invasion "is a threat to the entire globe." Last month he voted for a Russia sanctions law that lets the President waive its sanctions. On October 16, the President did.

What he said:
→ Feb 2022, as NY GOP chairman: "Biden's weakness and ineptitude have led us to this moment."
→ March 2023, his official page: "American weakness invites aggression... Russia & China are watching us."
→ April 2024: "Putin is a thug and a butcher, and Ukraine is right to defend itself."

How he voted:
→ Nay on the 2023 and 2024 Ukraine aid bills and the 2026 Ukraine Support Act.
→ In 2023 and 2024, Aye on 7 of 11 amendments to cut or bar Ukraine aid. In 2025 and 2026, No on all three.
→ Yea on the 2026 sanctions law. Section 115 lets the President waive its sanctions by certifying it's in the national interest. Unlike other parts of the law, Congress can't vote to block it.
→ At the bill's Rules Committee meeting, a motion to allow a vote on narrowing that waiver failed 3-7. He recorded no votes at that meeting.

What happened this month:
→ On Oct 16 the President waived the law's tariffs and nearly all of its other sanctions provisions.
→ Republicans objected within a day: Reps. Mike Lawler (R-N.Y.) and Don Bacon, and Sens. Thom Tillis, John Cornyn, Ted Cruz and Dan Sullivan. Tillis called it "beyond irresponsible."
→ Reuters, citing anonymous officials, reported that a White House order limited Ukraine's satellite access. The administration denies it.
→ His office hasn't issued a press release on Russia or Ukraine since June 2024.

The fair reading: he says he opposed the 2024 aid bill over non-military spending and a forgivable loan, and that he backs Ukraine's defense. His 2022 statement came as party chairman. He may have addressed the waiver in an interview we didn't review.

In 2022, every moment without severe consequences was a threat. In 2026, a fellow New York Republican spoke up. We found no word from Langworthy.
