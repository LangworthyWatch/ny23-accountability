In 2022, Nick Langworthy said "every moment that goes by without severe consequences" for Russia's invasion "is a threat to the entire globe." Last month he voted for a Russia sanctions law that lets the President waive its sanctions. On October 16, the President did.

What he said:
→ Feb 2022, as NY GOP chairman: "Biden's weakness and ineptitude have led us to this moment."
→ March 2023, his official page: "American weakness invites aggression... Russia & China are watching us."
→ April 2024: "Putin is a thug and a butcher, and Ukraine is right to defend itself."

How he voted:
→ Nay on the 2023 and 2024 Ukraine aid bills, and on the 2026 Ukraine Support Act.
→ In 2023 and 2024, Aye on 7 of 11 amendments to cut or bar Ukraine aid. In 2025 and 2026, No on all three.
→ Yea on the 2026 sanctions law. Its Section 115 lets the President waive its sanctions by certifying to Congress it's in the national interest. Unlike other parts of the law, Congress can't vote to block it.
→ At the Rules Committee meeting on the bill, a motion to allow a vote on narrowing that waiver failed 3-7. He recorded no votes at that meeting.

What happened this month:
→ On Oct 16 the President waived the law's tariffs and nearly all of its other sanctions provisions.
→ Reuters, citing anonymous officials, reported that a White House order limited Ukraine's satellite access. The administration denies it. Four GOP senators asked the Pentagon's inspector general to investigate.
→ Since June 2024, his office hasn't issued a press release on Russia or Ukraine, and we found no statement from him on the waiver.

The fair reading: he says he opposed the 2024 aid bill over non-military spending and a forgivable loan, and that he backs Ukraine's defense. The 2022 statement came as a party chairman criticizing a president of the other party. He may have addressed it in an interview we didn't review.

In 2022, every moment without severe consequences was a threat. In 2026, the sanctions he voted for were waived, and we found no word from him.
