"Serving on the Energy and Commerce Committee means I can deliver for NY23 taxpayers," Rep. Langworthy posted Oct 15, listing three energy bills. None of them is law, and one hasn't been introduced.

CLAIM: The POWER Act "speeds up pipeline and power plant construction."
STATUS: Not introduced
→ It's a discussion draft released by the committee chairman in July. It has no bill number. It would fold in the other two bills on his list.

CLAIM: The GRID Power Act "prioritizes reliable power plants."
STATUS: Passed the House, not the Senate
→ He cosponsored it. It passed the House 216-206 in September 2025 and hasn't passed the Senate.

CLAIM: The State Energy Accountability Act "exposes the cost of green mandates."
STATUS: No floor vote
→ His bill. The committee approved it 27-20 in June 2025. It has been waiting for a House vote for more than a year.

VERDICT: MISSING CONTEXT. His descriptions of the bills are broadly accurate, but "deliver" leaves out that none has delivered anything yet.

We also looked at his whole record on the committee since January 2025:

→ He moves his bills through committee more than most: 6 of his 20 committee bills (30%) reached a full-committee vote, about three times the rate for Republican-sponsored bills.
→ One of his committee bills has passed the House. None of his 50 bills and resolutions has become law.
→ He cosponsored 87 committee bills; 6 became law, including a Democrat's hydropower bill.
→ He missed 22 of 221 recorded committee votes (10%). That's about average for the committee's Republicans (median 11%).
→ His quantum computing bill passed committee 36-0, and two of his bills are co-led with Democrats.

The fair reading: getting bills through committee is real work, and the Senate isn't his to control. But the bills he says show he can "deliver for NY23 taxpayers" are still waiting.
