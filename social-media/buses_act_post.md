His caption says "My BUSES Act ends this Big Brother bounty hunt." The video he attached to it has him urging colleagues to adopt the amendment that lets the program continue.

On Sept 3 Rep. Langworthy posted a clip from the Energy and Commerce markup of his BUSES Act with this caption: "The radical left wants bus drivers spied on and fined for idling while they help disabled children board or while they conduct basic safety checks... Some cowards pocket almost $1 million... My BUSES Act ends this Big Brother bounty hunt."

We checked it against the committee's own recording of the same debate, the bill text, and New York City's rules.

→ New York City already exempts school buses that are loading passengers, running wheelchair lifts, or keeping kids warm or cool. Ocasio-Cortez said so in the clip he posted: penalizing drivers for helping a kid in a wheelchair "is a fairy tale."
→ Rep. Scott Peters (D-CA) offered an amendment narrowing the bill to pre-trip inspections, loading passengers with disabilities, and temperature control, and letting cities amend their programs "to comply" instead of ending them. Langworthy: "I want to thank Mr. Peters... I urge my colleagues to vote in favor of this amendment." Adopted by voice vote. That moment is in his own clip, at 3:50.
→ Even as introduced, the bill's bounty ban reached only reports on over-the-road buses. The program's top earner ($895,737 since 2019, per the New York Post) made it filming trucks.
→ Ocasio-Cortez's amendment failed 23 to 25 with his vote against. The bill was reported 26 to 22, the closest vote of the ten bills he called "great" in a separate post that never mentions one is his.

What is true: the exchange was real, the dollar figure is real, and a pre-trip engine check has no clear exemption in city rules. That gap is exactly what the amendment he backed was written to close.

VERDICT: Misleading
