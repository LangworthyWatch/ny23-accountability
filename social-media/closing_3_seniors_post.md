"No tax on Social Security." He has told seniors that four times. It is not true, and he was told so a year ago.

CLAIM: His tax law "eliminated taxes on Social Security for middle class seniors."
VERDICT: FALSE

→ What the law did: a new $6,000 deduction for people 65 and over, through 2028. It shrinks as income rises and is worth about $600 a year to a senior in the lowest bracket. Worth nothing to a senior who already owes no tax.
→ What it did not do: change the rules for taxing Social Security. If your benefits were taxed before, they still are. The deduction may lower the bill. It does not end the tax.
→ When a 76-year-old asked him about it at a town hall in September, he told the man three times it was a "tax credit." It is not a credit either.
→ We rated the claim misleading last December. He repeated it at the debate on October 6 and on TV on October 14.

He also said at the debate that "we doubled the child tax credit." The credit was doubled in 2017, five years before he took office. His law raised it from $2,000 to $2,200.

A real, temporary deduction for most seniors. Not the end of a tax. Not a credit. The entry has the law's text and every date.
