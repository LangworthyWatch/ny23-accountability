"No tax on Social Security." He has said it four times. The law never changed how benefits are taxed.

Day three of the record: the claim aimed at seniors.

CLAIM: The tax law "eliminated taxes on Social Security for middle class seniors."
VERDICT: FALSE (Oct 15 entry)

What he said, and when:
→ July 11, 2025: "88% of seniors will no longer pay federal tax on their Social Security benefits." Rated MISLEADING in December.
→ Sept 1, 2026: a 76-year-old asked about it at a town hall. He told him three times it was a "tax credit." It is a deduction. MISLEADING.
→ Oct 6, 2026, WETM debate: "We gave no tax on Social Security for our seniors." FALSE.
→ Oct 14, 2026, to WGRZ: the law "eliminated taxes on Social Security for middle class seniors." FALSE.

What the law actually did (P.L. 119-21, Sec. 70103): a $6,000 deduction per person 65 and over, for tax years 2025 through 2028. It shrinks by 6 cents for every dollar of income over $75,000 ($150,000 for a couple). Worth about $600 to a senior in the 10 percent bracket. Worth $0 to a senior with no taxable income.

What it did not do: touch the rules for taxing Social Security benefits (Internal Revenue Code Sec. 86). Up to 85 percent of benefits remain taxable above the same thresholds as before.

While he was at it, at the same debate: "We doubled the child tax credit." The doubling was the 2017 law, five years before he took office. His law moved it from $2,000 to $2,200. Rated MISLEADING.

The fair reading: the deduction is real and most seniors get something from it through 2028. A temporary deduction is not the elimination of a tax, and it is not a credit. He was told that in December and said it again in September and October.
