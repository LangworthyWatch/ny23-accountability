He says Hochul is "putting the handcuffs on law enforcement." His own counties' sheriffs say the law ended something their deputies were not doing. ICE's own reports show one encounter in all of New York in three months.

Two more campaign-page posts on Sept 10 and 11, six since Aug 26. The subject is New York's new ban on 287(g) agreements, the contracts that let local deputies do civil immigration enforcement for ICE.

CLAIM: Hochul is "shackling the cops" and "putting the handcuffs on law enforcement."
VERDICT: MISSING CONTEXT

→ The ban is real. The Local Cops, Local Crimes Act, signed May 27, voided every 287(g) agreement on Aug 25. Three of the 12 agencies the state notified were in NY-23: the Cattaraugus and Steuben sheriffs and the Allegany village police.
→ Cattaraugus Sheriff Eric Butler, complying: the law bars civil immigration enforcement through a 287(g) agreement, "something our deputies were not doing in the first place." His stated loss is federal reimbursement for deputies' time.
→ Steuben's legislature chair, on the county's task-force agreement: "I don't believe we've participated in any of those sorts of actions."
→ ICE publishes monthly encounter reports for its 287(g) partners. For May, June and July 2026 it reported one encounter in all of New York, in Cattaraugus on June 5, which began as a grand larceny arrest.

The fair reading: 15 sheriffs, including Cattaraugus, are suing; counties lose reimbursement; deputies can no longer serve ICE administrative warrants inside the jail. Whether the ban is good policy is a real dispute and we take no side in it.

What has not changed since Aug 26: $87 million in federal counterterrorism and police grants is still withheld from New York, including $17.7 million for the state program that funds sheriffs and police in all eight NY-23 counties. Six posts about backing the blue. Not one word about the money his own counties' departments are owed. His press archive was re-checked Sept 11.
