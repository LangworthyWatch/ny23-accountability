Health-industry PAC money to him: $6,000 in his first race, $85,250 in his second, $193,000 in this one. What changed in between was a seat on the committee that writes Medicaid and drug law.

VERDICT: DOCUMENTED PATTERN

→ December 13, 2024: he is named to the Energy and Commerce Committee and its Health Subcommittee. He called it an "unparalleled opportunity to influence" health policy.
→ The cycle before, health-industry PACs were 9.5 cents of every PAC dollar he raised. This cycle, 15.1 cents. Drug and device company PACs went from $5,500 to $62,000. Twelve of them gave for the first time.
→ Outside Erie County, every one of the 87,512 people in Medicaid managed care in his district is in a plan whose parent company's PAC has given to him: Fidelis Care (Centene), Highmark, Molina, UnitedHealthcare. The four parents gave $12,000 this cycle. Small money. Not a small relationship.
→ Fifteen of his health donors hold federal contracts. UnitedHealth's Optum, $81.3 billion over four years, mostly the military health system. McKesson, $44.9 billion. Centene's Health Net, $8.6 billion. Maximus, which runs Medicare's call centers, $6.0 billion.
→ He also sits on the Oversight Committee, whose jurisdiction includes "Federal procurement." He has no subcommittee seat there and missed 69 of its 145 recorded votes.

What cuts his way, and it is a lot: every Health Subcommittee member, in both parties, gets this money. His Medicaid vote reduced these plans' revenue. He voted for pharmacy benefit manager reform three times, against UnitedHealth's interest. All of it is lawful and disclosed. Nothing here ties a contribution to a vote.

What the record shows is simpler: the seat changed who funds him within one cycle, and the new funders include the companies paid to run Medicaid in every one of his counties and some of the largest health contractors in the federal government.

Every figure is from FEC filings, USAspending.gov and the state's September Medicaid enrollment report, with the classification rule published so anyone can rerun it.
