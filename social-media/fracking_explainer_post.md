The New York Post says fracking "enriched next-door counties in Pennsylvania." He shared it. Every one of those counties has lost people since 2010. The one with the most gas, Susquehanna, lost the most: 12 percent.

VERDICT: MISSING CONTEXT

→ Population, 2010 to 2024: Bradford, Pa., down 4.8 percent. Susquehanna, Pa., down 12.0. Tioga, Pa., down 2.9. Directly across the line: Chemung down 8.7, Steuben down 7.0, Tioga, N.Y., down 6.8. His op-ed said drilling brought "growing populations."
→ Private jobs, 2014 to 2024: down 3 to 9 percent in the three Pennsylvania counties. Tioga County, N.Y., was the only one of the six that added jobs.
→ Average pay, 2024: Bradford $60,899 against Chemung $58,195. Susquehanna $49,991 against Tioga, N.Y., $69,400. Tioga, Pa., $50,266 against Steuben $74,444. His op-ed called the border "East and West Berlin."
→ What Pennsylvania did get, and it is real: 16 percent of the nation's gas, $243.9 million in impact fees last year, 11,180 direct drilling jobs statewide (0.2 percent of its workforce), and gas at $15.04 per thousand cubic feet against New York's $17.58.
→ What it cost, per Pennsylvania's own institutions: a 2020 statewide grand jury that called the state's "wait and see" approach to health "unacceptable" and said wells belong 2,500 feet from homes, not 500. A $16.29 million no-contest plea over Dimock's water. 2023 state-commissioned studies finding lymphoma 5 to 7 times as likely within a mile of a well. Associations, not proof, and the entry says so.

What is fair: the gas is real and cheap, the fees pay for local roads, and families who leased were paid. New York would have gained something too. None of it shows up as growth in the counties he points to.

Pennsylvania chose the gas and its costs. New York chose neither. The border counties on both sides are losing people either way. The entry has every number, both ways, with the source for each.
