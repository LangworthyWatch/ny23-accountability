The New York Post says fracking "enriched next-door counties in Pennsylvania." He shared it. His own op-ed says drilling gave Pennsylvania "growing populations" and made the border "East and West Berlin." We pulled the numbers for the next-door counties.

VERDICT: MISSING CONTEXT

→ Every county on both sides of the line lost people from 2010 to 2024. Bradford, Pa., down 4.8 percent. Susquehanna, Pa., home of the Dimock gas field, down 12 percent. Tioga, Pa., down 2.9. Across the border: Chemung down 8.7, Steuben down 7.0, Tioga, N.Y., down 6.8.
→ Private jobs fell 3 to 9 percent in the three Pennsylvania counties from 2014 to 2024. Tioga County, N.Y., was the only county in the comparison that added jobs.
→ Average pay in 2024: Bradford $60,899, Susquehanna $49,991, Tioga, Pa. $50,266. Chemung $58,195, Steuben $74,444, Tioga, N.Y. $69,400. The New York side pays more.
→ What Pennsylvania did get: 16 percent of the nation's gas, $243.9 million in impact fees last year, 11,180 direct drilling jobs statewide (0.2 percent of its workforce), and gas at $15.04 per thousand cubic feet against New York's $17.58. All real.
→ What it cost, per Pennsylvania's own institutions: a 2020 grand jury that called the state's "wait and see" approach to health "unacceptable." A $16.29 million no-contest plea over Dimock's water. 2023 state-commissioned studies finding lymphoma 5 to 7 times as likely within a mile of a well. Associations, not proof, and the entry says so.
→ The ban is not Hochul's. It was imposed in 2014 and 2015 and written into law in April 2020. A governor cannot lift it alone, which is what she told Politico in the clip his page captioned "passing the buck."

What is fair to him: the gas is real and cheap, the fees pay for local roads, and families who leased were paid. None of it shows up as growth in the counties he points to.

The honest comparison: Pennsylvania chose the gas and its costs, New York chose neither, and the border counties on both sides are losing people either way.
