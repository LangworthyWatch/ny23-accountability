He stood at a firefighter cancer screening and said he is "fighting in Washington" for screenings. There are two firefighter cancer screening bills in Congress. He has cosponsored neither.

On Sept 21 at the Clarence Center Fire Department he posted: "Early detection is the key to saving lives and that's why I'm fighting in Washington to expand access to cancer screenings and treatments." His office's advisory, per Fingerlakes1, "did not identify a specific bill." We checked the official rosters.

CLAIM: "I'm fighting in Washington to expand access to cancer screenings and treatments" for firefighters.
VERDICT: MISSING CONTEXT

→ The FIRE Cancer Act, H.R. 1610, would let fire departments use federal grant money for cancer screenings, the kind he attended. The fire fighters' union lists it under "Expanding Access to Cancer Screenings." Seven cosponsors, including Rep. Lawler. Not him.
→ H.R. 2921, the Federal Firefighter Cancer Detection and Prevention Act: 11 cosponsors. Not him.
→ Also not on the responder mental health bill, the federal firefighters bill, public safety collective bargaining (Garbarino and Lawler are), or the volunteer first responder housing bill.

What he does support, from the same rosters and the Clerk's votes:
→ Voted Yea on the defense bill that made firefighter cancer a line-of-duty death, and on the 2024 fire grants law.
→ Cosponsors the PFAS-free gear bill, the 9/11 health funding fix, and the fallen-heroes act, which he signed nine days before it passed.
→ Cosponsors the Medicare cancer screening bill, along with 337 other members.

The fair reading: most members are on neither small bill, and showing up at a volunteer company's screening draws attention to a real risk. The gap is specific to the claim he chose to make at that event.

A note on the number: his post says 62% greater risk of esophageal cancer. The news clip he posted cites a 9% higher risk of any cancer diagnosis, from the federal study of 30,000 firefighters. We could not locate the source for 62% and do not dispute it.

If his office names the bill it meant, we will publish it.
