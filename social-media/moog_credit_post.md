There is no way for a congressman to "bring home millions" for a publicly traded defense contractor. The rules he works under ban it.

At Moog's 75th anniversary this week, Congressman Langworthy posted: "I've fought to bring home millions for Moog and other critical defense projects."

CLAIM: "I've fought to bring home millions for Moog"
VERDICT: Missing context

First, what is real, because it is real and we verified it at the primary source:
→ He co-led a bipartisan $7 million photonics amendment with Buffalo Democrat Tim Kennedy. The House adopted it in July 2025. That is genuine defense advocacy, on the record.
→ Moog is in NY-23, employs thousands here, and wins defense work on the merits. Nothing improper about any of that.

Now what the record shows about "millions for Moog":
→ House rules BAN directing project funding to for-profit companies. Moog is publicly traded. The route the caption describes does not exist.
→ His own published funding requests, all four years, all 65 of them: zero defense or aerospace companies. Water lines, schools, hospitals, radios.
→ Moog's federal money is competitively bid contracts, $17 million to $43 million each. Contracting officers award those. No House member does.
→ The building he was celebrating is Moog's own $150 million investment. The state's announcement of that event does not mention federal funding, Congress, or any member of Congress. Not once.

And the tell: in Moog's own press release that day, his on-the-record quote makes no funding claim at all. "Moog is one of the great Western New York success stories." The "millions for Moog" line exists only in the Facebook caption.

His second post that day said he fights "for investments in trusted photonics." That version is defensible. The $7 million amendment backs it up.

Advocating for a program is real work, and he did some. It is not bringing home money for a company. One of his posts knows the difference. The other erases it.
