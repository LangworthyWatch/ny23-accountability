The "$76 million statewide investment" Rep. Langworthy posted about today is money he already announced in December. It is the third announcement of the same award.

His post: "That's why we created the Rural Health Transformation Program... this $76 million statewide investment delivers on that promise."

→ New York's own funding guidance allocates $76,190,022 of the $212,058,207 award announced December 30 to one initiative. Applications for it opened June 12 and closed July 14. CMS's own release calls today's number "just one part of the larger overall funding amount awarded to New York for fiscal year 2026."
→ Three announcements, one award: the December 30 press release, the July 15 Centralus Health event, and today's post.
→ "We created" it: the fund was added in the Senate just before passage, as an offset to the Medicaid cuts in the same bill (KFF). The House passed the Senate's version.
→ What the post never mentions: that same law cuts about $137 billion from rural Medicaid over ten years. The fund replaces roughly 37% of that, and it expires in 2030, before 64% of the cuts land. New York hospitals face $8 to $10 billion a year in losses at full effect (HANYS).
→ Nine months after the award, no NY-23 hospital has been named to receive any of it. Seven NY-23 counties are eligible, and a Cattaraugus County health system said in July it was applying.

What is true: the $212 million is real, rural providers need it, and the initiative does target the Southern Tier. The gap is scale and repetition. $76 million is about a third of one year's award; the losses run to billions a year.
