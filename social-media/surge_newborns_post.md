He announced $35 million a year to help states implement the federal newborn screening panel. He did not mention that HHS abolished the expert committee that builds the panel.

On Sept 25 he stood with Jim and Jill Kelly of Hunter's Hope to introduce the "Surge to Save Newborns Act." He retold the 2024 Krabbe decision: "the advisory committee voted to add it to the panel." Here is the record.

CLAIM: A bill to close "the gap" between a federal screening recommendation and states' ability to implement it. "Non-controversial. Common sense."
VERDICT: MISSING CONTEXT

→ That committee was terminated by HHS on April 1, 2025. HHS's own Federal Register notices say so, twice. The Secretary now adds conditions on his own. The replacement, announced Aug 12, 2026: a $700,000 contractor "workgroup."
→ In July 2024 he thanked that committee by name. On Sept 25 he credited it again and did not say it is gone.
→ He already co-leads H.R. 4709, the Newborn Screening Saves Lives Reauthorization Act, which would put the committee back on a statutory footing. It cleared his health subcommittee Sept 10, 2025 and has not moved since. He sits on the full committee.
→ The $35 million figure is the Surge to Save Newborns coalition's $173 million ask spread over five years. The coalition is supported by Travere Therapeutics and BioMarin, which it discloses itself. New York's estimated share: $7.6 million.
→ Medicaid pays for about 4 in 10 births and nearly half of rural births. In 2025 he voted twice for the law that cuts federal Medicaid by $911 billion.

The fair reading: the gap is real, the bill is bipartisan, and it fits his Krabbe record since 2023. New York already screens; the babies helped are mostly born elsewhere. Nothing here is a criticism of the Kellys or Hunter's Hope, whose advocacy put Krabbe on the panel.

Jim Kelly said Thursday they have "a note out to Mr. Kennedy" seeking a meeting. The question for the congressman: does he want the committee back, and has he asked?
