Five bills. Five times the popular framing and the actual text did not match.

Every one of these policies polls well. That is what makes it work: a bill named for something popular gets less scrutiny than the text inside it.

SAVE ACT (H.R. 22, promoted on Facebook)
CLAIM: "83% of Americans support showing identification to vote"
VERDICT: Misleading
The bill requires documentary proof of citizenship, delivered in person. A standard REAL ID does not count.

STOP INSIDER TRADING ACT (H.R. 7008, cosponsor)
CLAIM: Members "shouldn't run from a briefing to a broker"
VERDICT: Misleading
It does not ban selling stock. The bill's own sponsor, Rep. Steil, calls that description "accurate but misleading."

KIDS ACT (H.R. 7757, voted for it)
CLAIM: "Holds Big Tech accountable"
VERDICT: Missing context
The House version dropped the duty of care, the bill's core accountability requirement.

SECURE DATA ACT (H.R. 8413, cosponsor)
CLAIM: A bill named for securing your data
VERDICT: Documented pattern
Section 12 gives you no private right of action. Section 15 would override stronger state privacy laws, including New York's.

INFRASTRUCTURE EXPANSION ACT (H.R. 3548, his own bill)
CLAIM: A bill named for expanding infrastructure
VERDICT: Missing context
It repeals New York's Scaffold Law, the fall-injury protection for construction workers, on any federally funded project.

Two things we are not saying.

→ That any of this is deliberate. We do not claim to know what anyone intended, and we do not need to. Every line above is the bill's own text, from congress.gov and govinfo, set next to how it was described.

→ That his bills never check out. Some do, and we say so. The biosimilars bill, the CAREERS Act, and the Jasper-Troupsburg award all came back Mostly True.

The pattern is not that the policies are bad. It is that the name on the bill keeps doing work the text does not.

Don't take our word for it. Read the text yourself.
