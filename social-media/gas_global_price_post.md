America produced more natural gas in 2025 than in 2024. The price still went up 61%.

That's the gap in Rep. Langworthy's pitch for his Energy Choice Act: "energy that is affordable, reliable, and proven." More drilling doesn't set the price. Exports, world demand and weather do too, and exports keep growing.

From the U.S. Energy Information Administration:
→ Production: up 4% in 2025. Henry Hub price: up 61%, from $2.19 to $3.53.
→ The U.S. is the world's largest LNG exporter. About 16% of the gas we produce this year is projected to ship overseas, up from 12% in 2024.
→ 2022: EIA cited Europe's demand for U.S. gas as one reason prices swung. The year averaged $6.45, the highest since 2008.
→ When one export terminal, Freeport LNG, shut after a fire, U.S. prices fell 40% in about a month. Less gas leaving meant cheaper gas here.
→ Winter Storm Fern pushed the January 2026 average to $7.72.

The fair reading: more supply does help. EIA says it keeps storage full and limits spikes, and forecasts $3.16 for 2027. And 2025's jump came off a low 2024 price.

His bill protects gas hookups. It does nothing about what the gas costs, and it ties more homes to a price set partly overseas.
