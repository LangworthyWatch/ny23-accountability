Rep. Langworthy has not held an in-person public town hall since he took office in January 2023. Over the same period, 55 cents of every dollar given to his campaign has come from PACs.

When constituents can't question their congressman face to face, it's worth knowing who funds him. So we went through every report his campaign has filed for 2025-26 (Jan 2025 through Sept 30, 2026): $2.2 million in contributions.

→ PACs and political committees: $1.2 million, 54.6%.

→ Donors with addresses in ZIP codes entirely inside NY-23: $194,775, 8.9%. Even counting ZIP codes NY-23 shares with a neighboring district, and assuming every small unitemized gift came from the district, it's at most 17%.

→ The rest came from donors elsewhere in New York (15%) and outside the state (12%).

→ Donors who describe themselves as lobbyists or government-affairs professionals gave $70,850. 84% of it came from Virginia and Washington, D.C.

His view, in a 2025 statement to WSKG: monthly telephone town halls are "a very successful and effective method of reaching all the constituents of NY-23," and he is "very in tune with the concerns and opinions" of constituents.

The fair reading: a high PAC share is common for incumbents. His ranks 7th of 26 New York House members, behind four Democrats and two Republicans. Donors in Buffalo count here as outside NY-23. He holds monthly phone town halls and cites newsletters, interviews and office meetings. And these records show who funds his campaign, not who influences his decisions.

You can weigh both: how you can reach him, and who pays for his campaigns.

Don't take our word for it. Every number comes from his campaign's own FEC filings, linked in the entry.
