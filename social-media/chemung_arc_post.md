On Facebook, Rep. Langworthy says he's "proud to secure $1.4 million" for Chemung County's sewers. His own press release says he "announced" a grant from the Appalachian Regional Commission. The post never mentions the agency.

The grant is real, and it's good news: $1.4 million from ARC plus $1.4 million in state money for a $2.8 million sewer upgrade at the Southern Tier Logistics Business Park in Horseheads. We checked what "secure" means here.

CLAIM: "I'm proud to secure $1.4 million for Chemung County."
VERDICT: MISSING CONTEXT

→ The decision is made without him. ARC grants go through a process run by the applicant, regional and state reviewers, and ARC itself, a federal-state agency. ARC decides what gets funded.
→ No step in that process gives a member of Congress a vote or a decision.
→ It's not an earmark. His project requests for 2024 through 2027 include no Chemung sewer project.
→ Sen. Schumer announced the same grant the same day and says the money comes from the 2021 infrastructure law, passed before Rep. Langworthy took office.
→ His release describes no role for him in winning the grant. The regional planning board says support letters from members of Congress are "nice to have." We found none from him, though such letters are usually not public.

The fair reading: announcing grants is normal, and this one meets a real local need. If his office wrote a support letter, that's a real role, and we'll say so. And Sen. Schumer's "I'm proud to deliver" is the same kind of claim.

But "secure" tells readers he got the money. The record shows he announced the result of a process that runs without him.

Don't take our word for it. His release, Schumer's release and ARC's own process are all linked in the entry.
