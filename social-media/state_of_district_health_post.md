Eight NY-23 hospitals are on a federal at-risk list, the most of any district in New York. Twenty-eight of the district's pharmacies have closed since 2023. His June town hall line: "every rural hospital that I've met is not on the verge of closure."

We built a baseline for the district from public records. State of the district, September 2026:

→ HOSPITALS: the Fiscal Policy Institute lists eight NY-23 hospitals drawing more than a quarter of their revenue from Medicaid and public appropriations, "the most of any district in the state." Brooks-TLC in Dunkirk: 97%. Two district hospitals have already closed since 2000 (Salamanca, Silver Creek). Cornell's demographers find that if the at-risk list closed, New York's emergency care desert population would rise 182%.
→ PHARMACIES: we pulled the full state pharmacy registry. NY-23 has 102 active community pharmacies and has lost 28 since January 2023, 24 of them in 2025, 25 of them Rite Aid. Six places now have no registered pharmacy: Mayville (the Chautauqua County seat), Silver Creek, Eden, Alfred, Salamanca, Lancaster.
→ FOOD: one in seven residents is food insecure in every county, from 12.7% in Tioga to 15.6% in Chautauqua (Feeding America).

The fair reading, stated plainly: rural hospital and pharmacy loss is national and decades old. The pharmacies closed in a corporate bankruptcy, not by any vote, and we attribute none of that to him. The $212 million rural health award is real.

The documented gap is narrower and sharper. He voted for the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, the law the at-risk analyses are modeled on (Roll Call 190, July 3, 2025). He told constituents the hospitals on that list are not at risk. He has announced the offset fund's money three times, and no NY-23 facility has been named to receive any of it.
