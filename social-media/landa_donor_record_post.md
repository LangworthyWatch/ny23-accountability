Langworthy voted to block the first federal nursing home staffing rule for 10 years. A nursing home owner gave his committees $55,000, every dollar of it before that vote.

Here is who that donor is, entirely from public documents.

→ ProPublica, 2015: New York regulators approved his expansion after leaving at least 20 federal fines off 15 ownership applications. He is a former member of the state health council that reviews those applications.

→ Buffalo News, 2018: at four Erie County homes, nursing assistants were cut from 80 to 38 and patient care time ran 89 minutes a day against a state average of 133. (Those four are his wife's. His spokesman says he is not involved in running them.)

→ NY Attorney General, 2022: two fraud lawsuits name him. One alleges $18.6 million diverted from an Albion nursing home through intentional understaffing. The other alleges $22.6 million diverted through 13 shell companies, and says he concealed his ownership. Civil allegations, not convictions.

→ HHS Inspector General, 2025: 99 of 100 sampled Medicare claims at a facility he co-owns failed payment requirements. Estimated overpayment, at least $31.2 million.

→ He sued a magazine for describing him this way. In August 2023 a federal judge dismissed his case, finding the statements about fraud and understaffing were substantially true or legally privileged.

Two things we want to be straight about, because you will hear them.

There is no Landa facility in NY-23. Nobody here lives in one of his buildings. The connection is the money, not the buildings.

And a court did once find him liable under the trafficking statute, in 2019. That order was vacated in 2022. We are not going to build a case on a ruling that no longer exists.

We are not claiming a deal, and we say so in the entry. What we are saying is that this is the public record of the man whose money helped fund the campaign, and Langworthy has never been asked about it.
