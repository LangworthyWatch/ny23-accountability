His campaign posted that his opponent supports the gas and propane ban. On the same debate stage, his opponent said: "I will never support things like bans on wood-burning stoves or propane or no more hookups for natural gas."

We checked four energy posts from the week after the Oct 6 WETM debate against the debate video, state law and court records.

CLAIM: "Now you know my opponent supports [Hochul's natural gas and propane ban]." (campaign page, Oct 9)
VERDICT: FALSE
→ In the same exchange, less than a minute before the part the clip uses, his opponent said he will never support those bans. The moderator asked if a state mandate of one form of power was good or bad. Answer: "That would be a bad thing."

CLAIM: "My opponent OPPOSES [the Energy Choice Act]." (campaign page, Oct 8)
VERDICT: TRUE
→ He said so on stage. But the clip stops seconds before his answer that he would never support a propane or gas ban.

CLAIM: "He believes that you should be mandated to be in an all-electric home."
VERDICT: MISLEADING
→ No law or proposal we found mandates that. The state law applies to new buildings only and leaves existing homes and their equipment alone. It is also suspended by court order until at least Dec 31, 2026.

CLAIM: "We lost 69 lives in western New York" in Winter Storm Elliott.
VERDICT: FALSE
→ The official count is 47. The storm was a disaster and 911 was overwhelmed, but the number he gave is not the record.

The fair reading: Rep. Langworthy and his opponent disagree on the federal Energy Choice Act, and that is a real policy difference. Opposing a federal bill is not the same as supporting the state ban, and his opponent said on camera that he does not.

The entry also covers "Kathy Hochul bans" gas drilling (the fracking ban came in 2015 and 2020, before she was governor) and the claim that Albany wants to take its climate law "nationwide."

Don't take our word for it. Check the debate video and his own posts, all cited in the entry.
