Rep. Langworthy asked a federal inspector general to investigate a housing project in downtown Buffalo. It is not in his district. Here is what was pending in his own district the same week.

On Sept 8 he wrote HUD's inspector general about the North Aud Block at Canalside: 251 units, more than $609,000 in public support per apartment, up from $10 million in the 2022 request for proposals. His words, per the Buffalo News: "I am concerned that the project has changed materially since it was competitively solicited."

Canalside is in NY-26, represented by Tim Kennedy. The Buffalo News: "Langworthy doesn't represent downtown Buffalo or Canalside." His adviser: his Oversight seat gives him jurisdiction "on a project that affects the region, not just the footprint of the site."

Pending in NY-23 the same week, each documented on our site:

→ $87 million in federal police and counterterrorism grants still withheld from New York, including $17.7 million for the program that funds sheriffs in all eight NY-23 counties. No public statement from him located; Malliotakis, Lawler and Garbarino pressed the administration.
→ Eight NY-23 hospitals on a federal at-risk list, the most of any district in the state. His June town hall line: "every rural hospital that I've met is not on the verge of closure."
→ 28 pharmacies closed since 2023; six places now have none.
→ Four days earlier, defending his own bill overriding a New York City ordinance, he told the city's members to stop "opining about how we should heat our homes in my district," then posted that "the socialists all of a sudden want local control."

The fair reading: Oversight jurisdiction is real, the subsidy question is contested across party lines, and he has worked district matters this year. We take no position on the project. The gap is specific: a probe request for Buffalo, and no located letter or statement on the two largest problems at home.

By his office's own standard, what region does $87 million in withheld police funding affect?
