Rep. Langworthy asked a federal inspector general to investigate a housing project in downtown Buffalo. It is not in his district. Here is what was pending in the district he does represent, the same week.

On Sept 8 he wrote HUD's Office of Inspector General about the North Aud Block at Canalside: 251 units, more than $609,000 in public support per apartment, up from a $10 million figure in the 2022 request for proposals. His words, per the Buffalo News: "I am concerned that the project has changed materially since it was competitively solicited."

Canalside is in NY-26, represented by Tim Kennedy. The Buffalo News: "Langworthy doesn't represent downtown Buffalo or Canalside." His adviser: his Oversight seat gives him jurisdiction "on a project that affects the region, not just the footprint of the site."

Pending in NY-23 the same week, each documented on our site:

→ $87 million in federal police and counterterrorism grants still withheld from New York, including $17.7 million for the program that funds sheriffs in all eight NY-23 counties. No public statement from him located, while Malliotakis, Lawler and Garbarino pressed the administration.
→ Eight NY-23 hospitals on the Fiscal Policy Institute's at-risk list, the most of any district in the state. His June town hall line: "every rural hospital that I've met is not on the verge of closure."
→ 28 community pharmacies closed since 2023; six places now have none.
→ The $212 million rural health award, announced three times, no NY-23 facility named.

The fair reading: Oversight jurisdiction is real, the subsidy question is contested across party lines, and he has worked district matters this year (grapes, grants, biosimilars). We take no position on the project. The gap is specific: a federal probe request for Buffalo, and no located letter or statement on the two largest problems at home.

By his office's own standard, what region does $87 million in withheld police funding affect?
