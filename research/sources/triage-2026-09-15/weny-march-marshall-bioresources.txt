WASHINGTON, D.C. —
 Animal rights advocates and a local member of Congress are once again looking to put a spotlight on a dog breeding facility in upstate New York.
The closure of a controversial dog breeding facility in Wisconsin earlier this year provided momentum for animal rights activists, who are turning their attention to a similar facility in Wayne County, New York.  
“With that momentum, we decided where to go next is Marshall BioResources, which is a facility in upstate New York that has at least 16,000 dogs in cages waiting to be sold, exploited and destroyed in laboratories,” said Gretchen Primack, co-lead for the March to Abolish Animal Testing. 
According to organizers, the 200-plus-mile march from the New York State Capitol in Albany to Marshall BioResources will cover about 15 miles a day and bring together activists, scientists and nearly 30 partner organizations. 
The breeding facility has come under fire by activists and even some federal lawmakers, including Congressman Nick Langworthy, R-N.Y. 
Langworthy said when he returns to Washington next week, he plans to introduce what he called “the single most comprehensive bill package to crack down on animal testing on dogs and cats that this country’s ever seen.” 
“I’ve taken up this cause. The fact that we are using our dogs and cats in this country for medical testing- the time for that has come and gone. The public has spoken. They’re outraged,” said Langworthy. “It’s a stain on the state of New York that we have a facility just a few hours from here. 60,000 animals are under the roofs of the buildings of those facilities. And those dogs are bred simply for the torture of medical testing.” 
Both Langworthy and advocates argue that inhumane animal and dog testing needs to be phased out, especially given advancements in new technology.
“In 2026, we know that there are alternatives to the kind of testing that these animals will suffer through. If we have alternatives, and we are a society that considers itself kind to animals, why are we still doing what Marshall BioResources — and these eventual placements for the animals — why are we still doing this,” asked Primack. “In order to call attention to something this intense and powerful against an industry that is this powerful, we knew that we had to take bold action. So we planned a two-week, 207-mile march,” Primack added. 
In a statement to WENY News this summer, Marshall BioResources said, “Breeding animals for use in medical research in the United States is highly regulated and subject to ongoing oversight. Our facilities are regularly inspected by the USDA to ensure compliance with federal animal welfare standards.” 
The company argues that ending animal research before validated alternatives are available could threaten national security and biomedical research. 
“The result would not be less animal testing; instead, the required animal testing would be moved to other countries to be conducted with less oversight, less accountability, and reduced influence over research practices and quality standards. This would affect the development of vaccines, biologics, military medicine, infectious disease countermeasures, and other critical medical advancements that support pandemic preparedness and biodefense capabilities,” the company said.
