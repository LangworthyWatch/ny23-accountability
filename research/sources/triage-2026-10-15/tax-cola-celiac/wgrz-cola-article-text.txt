Advocates and Erie County officials say people with disabilities and seniors continue to face financial challenges as inflation drives up the cost of housing, food and utilities.
A projected 3.6% 
Social Security cost-of-living adjustment
 for next year could provide some relief to beneficiaries, but advocates say many people living on fixed incomes will continue to struggle to afford basic necessities.
Todd Vaarwerk, chief policy officer at Western New York Independent Living, says the increase is not enough to address the financial challenges facing people with disabilities.
"3.6% is a way to stave off disaster, but is it enough? No, especially in the current economic climate," Vaarwerk said.
Vaarwerk says more funding is needed to support people with disabilities, particularly those who rely on Supplemental Security Income (SSI) to cover their living expenses.
He says the average SSI recipient receives about $1,100 a month, which can quickly be consumed by housing costs alone.
"Imagine that if you're paying for rent, that that's the entire $1,100, and that's supposed to cover everything," Vaarwerk said. "That's supposed to cover food, utilities, rent, expenses, everything."
Western New York Independent Living provides services and programs designed to help people with disabilities live independently. Vaarwerk says those services are offered free of charge.
Seniors are also feeling the effects of inflation, according to Randy Hoak, commissioner of the Erie County Department for the Aging.
Hoak says Erie County has established an energy and affordability assistance outreach program to help residents learn about programs that may provide financial relief.
The county plans to visit locations throughout Erie County during October and November to connect residents with available resources.
"We're gonna reach all corners of the county to make sure that people understand the programs that they might be available for that can provide a little bit of help," Hoak said.
Hoak says outreach events are scheduled at multiple locations, including West Falls and Jefferson Avenue, as officials work to ensure residents know what assistance may be available to them.
Beyond the immediate impact of inflation, Hoak says he is concerned about the long-term financial outlook for Social Security.
The Old-Age and Survivors Insurance Trust Fund is projected to exhaust its reserves within the next six years, according to the timeline cited by Hoak.
He warns that without congressional action, beneficiaries could face a significant reduction in payments.
"If Congress does not act, they're looking at a 22% reduction across the board — a 22% reduction in income for 220,000 residents of Erie County who receive a Social Security benefit of some kind," Hoak said.
U.S. Rep. Nick Langworthy welcomed the projected 3.6% cost-of-living adjustment, saying it would provide additional income for retirees. In a written statement, Langworthy said:
"I'm pleased that seniors will receive a 3.6% cost-of-living adjustment next year, just as Social Security benefits have been adjusted for inflation annually for decades. That increase will mean an average of $75 more per month for retired workers to help keep pace with costs, but more importantly, the Working Families Tax Cuts we passed last year eliminated taxes on Social Security for middle class seniors. I will always fight to protect this critical program and ensure our seniors receive the benefits they've earned."
U.S. Rep. Tim Kennedy also weighed in on the projected increase, saying seniors continue to face financial pressure from rising everyday expenses. In a written statement, Kennedy said:
"Our seniors deserve every penny they receive from Social Security after a lifetime of paying into the promise of a secure and comfortable retirement. This cost-of-living increase is a step in the right direction, but a 3.6% increase in benefits does little to ease the rising costs of gas, groceries, and housing for seniors struggling to make ends meet. President Trump's inflationary policies are tarnishing the golden years of millions of retirees across the nation and they deserve so much better."
