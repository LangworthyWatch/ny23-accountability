Blakeman Hits Hochul HARD in Debate
ND
October 13, 2026
8:14 pm
Democratic incumbent Governor Kathy Hochul and Republican challenger Bruce Blakeman face off in the only scheduled debate ahead of the November New York gubernatorial election, at the Spectrum News NY1 studios, in New York, US, Ocotober 13, 2026.  Anna Connors/Pool via REUTERS
(New York, New York) – Governor Kathy Hochul and Republican challenger Bruce Blakeman went head-to-head Tuesday night in their only debate before the November election, trading attacks over immigration, taxes, energy costs and the future of New York.
Blakeman repeatedly challenged Hochul’s record as governor, blaming her administration for the state’s high cost of living and questioning her handling of public safety.
The Nassau County executive criticized the billions of dollars spent on migrants in New York, arguing that the money should have been used to help taxpayers. He also defended cooperation between local police and federal immigration authorities.
Bruce Blakeman delivers a knockout punch to Kathy Hochul.  pic.twitter.com/PaNshjBVAN
— TalkRadio 77 WABC (@77WABCradio)  October 13, 2026
The two clashed over last week’s ICE shooting in Marble Hill. Blakeman questioned why the migrant involved was in the country, citing allegations about his criminal background. Hochul criticized the federal agents’ actions, pointing to the danger posed to a child inside the vehicle.
Energy costs were another major point of disagreement. Blakeman called for lowering utility bills and reopening the Indian Point nuclear power plant. Hochul defended her energy policies and pointed to federal decisions and the war with Iran as factors contributing to higher prices.
Blakeman also confronted Hochul over her handling of sexual assault allegations involving a Cornell University fraternity. He questioned why she had not pushed for an independent investigation in her role as a university trustee. Hochul rejected the criticism and defended her response to the case.
Throughout the debate, Hochul repeatedly connected Blakeman to President Donald Trump, warning voters about the policies he could bring to Albany.
Blakeman pushed back, accusing the governor of focusing too heavily on Trump instead of addressing problems in New York. He also highlighted differences between his own positions and the president’s, including his support for abortion rights.
The candidates also addressed New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani, with Blakeman accusing him of antisemitism and criticizing Hochul’s political relationship with the mayor.
Neither candidate backed down as the hour-long debate ended with Blakeman clearly coming out on top.

The two will face voters on November 3, with just three weeks remaining in the race for governor.
