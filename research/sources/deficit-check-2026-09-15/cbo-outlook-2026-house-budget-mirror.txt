Today, the Congressional Budget Office (CBO) released its updated budget projections
for fiscal years (FY) 2026-2036. CBO’s report helps Congress understand the fiscal and
economic challenges facing the country. According to the report:


Gross Federal Debt:
   •   Totals $63.7 trillion (136.4 percent of GDP) in 2036, up from $38.6 trillion (123.1
       percent of GDP) now. Debt is currently at the highest level in American history
       and keeps rising.

FY 2026 Deficit:
   •   Totals $1.9 trillion, the third highest figure in American history.
   •   The FY 2026 deficit is equivalent to 5.8 percent of GDP (the 50-year average is
       3.8 percent), the same as last year and down from 6.3 percent in 2024. This
       projection coincides with a 17.5 percent of GDP revenue projection (higher than
       50-year average of 17.3 percent). Higher spending, 23.3 percent of GDP
       compared to 50-year average of 21.2 percent, is the primary cause of higher-
       than-average deficit.

Ten-Year Deficit:
   •   Totals $24.4 trillion, greater than all deficit spending by the federal government
       from 1789 to 2023.
   •   The deficit increases by 68 percent over the budget window, growing from $1.9
       trillion this year to $3.1 trillion (6.7 percent of GDP) in 2036.

Spending:
   •   Totals $94.6 trillion over ten years.
   •   Grows from $7.4 trillion (23.3 percent of GDP) in 2026 to $11.4 trillion (24.4
       percent of GDP) in 2036.
   •   CBO projects mandatory spending (including interest) will increase from 75
       percent of the budget in 2026 to 80 percent of the budget in 2036.
       Mandatory Spending:
         o $57.9 trillion over ten years.
         o Grows from $4.5 trillion (14.2 percent of GDP) in 2026 to $7.0 trillion (15.0
           percent of GDP) in 2036.

       Net Interest Spending:
         o $16.2 trillion over ten years.
         o Grows from $1.0 trillion (3.3 percent of GDP) in 2026 to $2.1 trillion (4.6
           percent of GDP) in 2036.
         o In 2026, interest spending will be the third largest item in the federal
           budget behind only Social Security and Medicare.
         o Interest spending exceeds defense spending every year of the budget
           outlook, and is almost double defense spending by 2036.
         o In every year of the budget outlook, interest spending, as a percent of
           GDP, is at the highest recorded level in American history.
         o Interest costs are projected to increase from 9 percent of federal revenue
           in 2021 to 19 percent of federal revenue in 2026 to 26 percent of federal
           revenue in 2036.
         o Over the next ten years, for every dollar the federal government borrows,
           66 cents will go to pay interest on the national debt.

       Discretionary Spending:
         o $20.5 trillion over ten years.
         o Grows from $1.9 trillion (5.9 percent of GDP) in 2026 to $2.2 trillion (4.8
           percent of GDP) in 2036.
         o CBO reduced projected discretionary spending by more than $1 trillion
           due to legislation enacted by this Congress.

Revenue:
  •   Totals $70.2 trillion over ten years.
  •   Grows from $5.6 trillion (17.5 percent of GDP) in 2026 to $8.3 trillion (17.8 percent
      of GDP) in 2036.
  •   Revenue averages 17.7 percent of GDP over the decade, above the 50-year
      average of 17.3 percent of GDP.

Economic Growth:
  •   CBO projects 1.9 percent economic growth in 2025. The average annual rate of
      growth during the first three quarters of 2025 was 2.5 percent. With the Atlanta
      Fed’s GDPNow forecast projecting 3.7 percent for fourth quarter, growth would
      be close to 3 percent in 2025.
  •   CBO projects 2.2 percent growth in 2026 and a 1.8 percent annual average over
      the next decade.
  •   After enactment of the reconciliation law, CBO projects economy to be 2.4
      percent larger by 2035 than previously projected.

CPI Inflation:
  •   2.9 percent in 2026.
  •   2.3 percent annual average over the next decade.

Interest Rates:
  •   The projected interest rate on 10-year Treasury notes for 2026 has increased
      from 2.5 percent when President Biden took office to 4.1 percent in CBO’s latest
      projection.
  •   The 10-year average interest rate projection is 4.4 percent.

Unemployment:
  •   4.6 percent in 2025.
  •   4.3 percent annual average over the next decade.
  •   CBO notes that the reconciliation law reduces the unemployment rate in 2026
      and 2027.
