(WMTV) - The first group of dogs included in the Ridglan Farms agreement left the facility Friday, marking a major step in the plan to move 1,500 beagles from the Blue Mounds breeding and research facility to rescue groups. Rescue organizers said about 300 dogs were moved out Friday, the first phase in a transfer expected to continue over the coming days. First dogs leave Ridglan Farms, touch grass for first time as 1,500-dog transfer begins (WMTV) “We’ve got the first 300 of 1,500 out of Ridglan today, and it was the happiest feeling,” said Lauree Simmons, president and founder of Big Dog Ranch Rescue. “These sweet dogs have got such a bright future ahead of them. And we actually emptied three buildings.” The dogs were brought to a staging area, where they received vaccines, microchips and initial care before getting time outside. For many, organizers said, it was their first time touching grass. First dogs leave Ridglan Farms, touch grass for first time as 1,500-dog transfer begins (WMTV) “They’ve never been on grass. They’ve never had these smells. They’ve never seen that sky. And it’s quite remarkable,” said Wayne Pacelle, president of the Center for a Humane Economy and Animal Wellness Action. Pacelle said the contrast between the facility and the staging area was immediate. “It was a small world with a very low ceiling. You know, it was a lot of metal. There were no trees. They did everything in one place. They ate, they drank, they urinated, they defecated in other ways. And every day was the same,” Pacelle said. “This, for them, this is like the newest thing imaginable.” The move comes after Ridglan Farms confirmed earlier this week it reached an agreement to sell a substantial majority of its dogs to animal protection groups . Big Dog Ranch Rescue is taking responsibility for about 1,000 dogs, while the Center for a Humane Economy is coordinating placement for about 500 more through partner organizations. Those partners include the Dane County Humane Society, Beagle Freedom Project, Wisconsin Puppy Mill Project and Wisconsin Federated Humane Societies. Jeffrey Brown, who described himself as an animal rescuer, animal lover and volunteer, said the day was years in the making. “Yeah, this has been great leadership by the Big Dog Ranch and the Center for Humane Economy. But this has been years in the making of individuals and groups from Dane for Dogs and the Marty Project and others. This is a collective effort and today is the first day of freedom,” Brown said. Brown said watching the first van arrive was hard to describe. “Well, I’ve had the pleasure of just waiting here and seeing that first van come over the hill and open the door and there they just all sat patiently, a little scared, a little nervous. It’s a brand new world for them and it was just speechless,” he said. As the dogs explored, Brown said he was struck by how quickly many seemed to adapt. “They’re showing amazing levels of resilience and more importantly, forgiveness. I mean, this is a world that they were born for. Now they’re getting to experience it for the first time,” Brown said. Pacelle also pointed to that resilience. “Dogs can revert to who they are pretty quick. They’re incredibly resilient creatures,” Pacelle said. “I think that this is so new and so different that they’re distracted by it, so the fear that they may have is muted a little bit or subsumed and they’re still they’re still just figuring it out.” The dogs are not expected to be immediately available for adoption. Rescue groups have said many will need medical care, socialization and time to adjust before they are placed in homes. First dogs leave Ridglan Farms, touch grass for first time as 1,500-dog transfer begins (WMTV) Simmons said the goal is to prepare them for life outside the facility. “I am just so happy this is happening for these animals, and it’s been a long time coming,” Simmons said. “I’m just -- can’t wait to see them. going into loving arms of families and knowing the life they’re going to have and not living in a laboratory for the rest of their life.” Simmons said Friday’s group is only the beginning. “Monday, we start all over again. Tuesday we start all over again and just keep going.” The transfer follows weeks of protests , court developments and growing attention on Ridglan Farms . The facility has said it is still finalizing arrangements for the remaining dogs not included in the current agreement, but has declined to release updated animal numbers, citing safety concerns.
