Two organizations announced Thursday that they reached an agreement with Ridglan Farms to buy 1,500 beagles from the facility, setting the dogs up for adoption By Juliana Tornabene and Shaina Nijhawan Published : Apr. 30, 2026 at 10:48 AM CDT | Updated : Apr. 30, 2026 at 6:25 PM CDT Share Add Us On Google Add as a preferred source on Google MADISON, Wis. (WMTV) - A day after Ridglan Farms confirmed it would sell a majority of its dogs, the groups behind that agreement are now revealing new details, including how hundreds of those animals will be cared for in Dane County. Leaders with Big Dog Ranch Rescue and the Center for a Humane Economy announced Thursday they have reached a deal to purchase about 1,500 beagles from the Blue Mounds facility, calling it one of the largest coordinated efforts of its kind. “We are here to formally announce that we have reached an accord with Ridglan Farms to obtain 1,500 beagles from the company,” said Wayne Pacelle, president of the Center for a Humane Economy, adding the process of removing the dogs will begin immediately. How the plan works Under the agreement, Big Dog Ranch Rescue will take in about 1,000 dogs — housing some at its facilities in Alabama and Florida and placing others through a network of more than 50 partner rescues across the country. “This is one big movement over the next 10 days,” Pacelle said. The remaining roughly 500 dogs will be coordinated by the Center for a Humane Economy through partner organizations, with much of that effort centered in Wisconsin. “For the 500 dogs… staging is going to occur at the Dane County Humane Society here,” he said. While about 500 dogs are expected to be handled through this Wisconsin-based effort, officials say around 350 are expected to remain in Wisconsin, with at least 50 anticipated to be available for adoption through the Dane County Humane Society in the coming weeks. Pacelle said the effort is focused not just on moving the dogs quickly, but ensuring they are safely transitioned into new environments. “We are very excited about these animals who have lived in confinement… having an opportunity to touch soil and grass, breathe fresh air, and get into a loving home for the balance of their lives,” he said. Dane County’s role The Dane County Humane Society, along with partners including the Beagle Freedom Project, Wisconsin Puppy Mill Project and the Wisconsin Federated Humane Societies, will help care for and place hundreds of the dogs. DCHS says its teams will assist with transporting the animals, providing veterinary exams and medical care, and preparing them for adoption or transfer to other shelters. As excitement builds, officials say the process will take time — both for the organizations involved and for the dogs themselves. “These dogs are going to need time. They will need patience. They will need support as they adjust to environments they have never experienced before,” said Big Dog Ranch Rescue President Lauree Simmons. Officials say the dogs are not yet available for adoption and will need time for medical care and socialization before they are ready for homes. They also warned demand is expected to be high. “We’re probably not going to have enough to go around for the demand,” Pacelle said. The Dane County Humane Society has created an online interest form for potential adopters and says updates will be shared as the dogs become available. What’s next The agreement does not include all of the animals at Ridglan Farms. Organizers said they are continuing discussions about the remaining dogs but did not provide a number. The facility said it is working on finalizing arrangements for those dogs and would not be sharing the number of dogs still in its care to keep its staff and animals safe. “The dogs at Ridglan Farms are happy, healthy and well cared for. Publicly available USDA documentation shows this has been the case for many years,” Ridglan Farms said. “As the dogs sold this week leave our veterinary research facility, we hope they will continue to live happy lives in their new adopted homes.” Blue Mounds dog breeder Ridglan Farms facing $55K in fines after inspectors cite 300+ violations (Direct Action Everywhere) Local leaders also reacted to the agreement, calling it a positive step for the animals while acknowledging the tension surrounding the facility in recent weeks. “We are overjoyed to see these 1,500 beagles heading to loving homes and a far better life,” Dane County Sheriff Kalvin Barrett said. “Violence and law-breaking are never the answer.” Barrett added that what began as a planned peaceful demonstration “eroded to a coordinated incident,” and said he is proud of how deputies responded. Organizers also pointed to broader concerns about animal testing, saying efforts like this highlight the need for larger systemic change. “Because we cannot rescue our way out of these problems,” Pacelle said.
