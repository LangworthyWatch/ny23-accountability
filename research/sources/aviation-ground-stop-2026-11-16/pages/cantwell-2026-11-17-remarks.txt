We need a comprehensive bill that holds everybody to the same standards.” [ VIDEO ] WASHINGTON, D.C. – Today, U.S. Senator Maria Cantwell (D-Wash.), Ranking Member of the Senate Committee on Commerce, Science and Transportation, delivered the following remarks at the Families of Flight 5342 “Safer Skies for All Memorial.” The Families call on Congress to pass comprehensive aviation safety legislation following the tragic January 29, 2025 midair collision near DCA that took the lives of 67 people. A full transcript of her remarks is below: “Thank you to the families of Flight 5342, not just this event, but everything you have done to this point to bring awareness and certainty to the fact that we need to pass new legislation. I want to thank you for your willingness to turn tragedy and grief into action. “The dedication today [in honor] of those 67 souls who were lost on January 29, 2025, is a reminder that safety is a 365-day-a-year job, and we need to stay on it until the tasks are accomplished. Today, I’m glad to be joined by so many people who also want us to be successful: the Secretary of Transportation, the Administrator, pilots […]. Also, the NTSB chair, as Chairman Cruz said, has played a critical role over a long period of time, advocating for these changes. “As was mentioned last Thursday, we reached an agreement with the House Transportation Infrastructure Committee, in principle, on the civil aviation provisions of this legislation. Speaking with Chairman Graves, we were able to reach a deal on ADS-B In, helicopter route operations, ADS-B Out, and fees. And we need to get this agreement over the goal line. I know that Chairman Cruz and I, and Chairman Graves and Ranking Member Larson, who have all been working on this all summer long, are excited for this breakthrough in having these provisions settled among the four of us. Most importantly, I want to thank the families of the victims, whose advocacy has kept this effort focused on preventing another tragedy. “But let me be clear about where we are right now. While we have a deal in principle on the civil provisions, we do not have an agreement on the military provisions. We must have strong military accountability in the national airspace. The military must be a willing partner with the Department of Transportation and the FAA to make sure that what happened last January never, ever happens again. “When military aircraft operate in the same airspace, they must meet the same safety standards that protect everyone. Allowing a military loophole for routine or training operations in that immediate area undermines the very safety provisions we have agreed upon with Chairman Graves, Ranking Member Larson, Senator Cruz, and myself. “It is the FAA’s job to ensure that everyone in the flying airspace is safe. That responsibility requires effective oversight, [and] full cooperation, [from] everyone flying in our skies. “Our agreement on the civil provisions is important progress, but our work is not done. We need a comprehensive bill that holds everybody to the same standards. We need the flying public to know their safety comes first — always first — and we need the standards that meet that. So I look forward to working with my Armed Service Committee members, military partners, and the FAA to make sure this gets done. To the families, we know how long you’ve waited. It’s long enough. Let’s get this bill signed into law.” Click HERE to watch her remarks. ### Print Email Share Share Related Issues Aviation Previous Article Next Article
