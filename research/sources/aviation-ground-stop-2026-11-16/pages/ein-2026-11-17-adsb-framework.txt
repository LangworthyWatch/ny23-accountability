Key Senate and House Leaders Agree on ADS-B Safety Legislation Framework News Provided By U.S. Senate Committee on Commerce, Science, and Transportation November 18, 2026, 05:26 GMT Share This Article WASHINGTON, DC – At today’s press conference honoring the victims of the tragic January 2025 midair collision between American Airlines Flight 5342 and a UH-60 Army Black Hawk helicopter at Ronald Reagan Washington National Airport (DCA) and calling for action, Senate Commerce Committee Chairman Ted Cruz (R-Texas) announced an agreement in principle on a bipartisan, bicameral framework for aviation safety legislation. This includes an ADS-B In requirement first proposed in Chairman Cruz and Commerce Committee Ranking Member Maria Cantwell’s (D-Wash.) Rotorcraft Operations Transparency and Oversight Reform (ROTOR) Act. Key transportation committee leaders on the Senate Commerce, Science, and Transportation Committee and the House Transportation and Infrastructure Committee touted the agreement in principle, but noted lawmakers on the House and Senate Armed Services Committees have yet to sign on to reforms for military aircraft operating in civilian skies. Upon announcing the agreement, Chairman Cruz said: “I made a promise to the families of flight 5342 that Congress would pass comprehensive safety legislation. Today, the Senate and the House have reached an agreement in principle to mandate ADS-B In. Under this agreement, all aircraft required to broadcast their location via ADS-B Out will now be mandated to receive those same signals through ADS-B In. Although a few details are still being worked out with the House and Senate Armed Services Committees, it is critical that Congress pass comprehensive safety legislation by the end of the 119 th Congress, and I am confident we will. I want to thank the families for their persistence in ensuring that the legacy of the 67 lives lost will be a change in the law to protect millions upon millions flying in our country’s airspace.” Ranking Member Maria Cantwell said: “We reached an agreement with the House Transportation and Infrastructure Committee, in principle, on the civil aviation provisions of this legislation. But our work is not done. We need a comprehensive bill that holds everybody to the same standards. We need the flying public to know their safety comes first — always first — and we need the standards that meet that. I look forward to working with my Armed Service Committee members, military partners, and the FAA to make sure this gets done. To the families, we know how long you’ve waited. It’s long enough. Let’s get this bill signed into law.” House Transportation and Infrastructure Committee Chairman Sam Graves (R-Mo.-06) said: “The January 2025 midair collision in Washington, DC was a terrible tragedy, and Congress owes it to the victims and their families to get this safety legislation right. That’s been our goal, and working with House and Senate leaders, the NTSB and FAA, and safety stakeholders, including the victims’ families, I’m happy that we’ve reached an agreement in principle for the civil aviation components of the legislation, and that we are very close to a final agreement on the entire bill. We will continue working to see a final House-Senate bill signed into law before the end of this Congress that addresses the NTSB’s safety recommendations, honors the victims of this tragedy, and helps ensure this kind of disaster never happens again.” House Transportation and Infrastructure Committee Ranking Member Rick Larsen (D-Wash.-02) said: “The DCA midair collision was a stark reminder that we must remain vigilant in our pursuit of aviation safety – more can always be done to make air travel safer. We owe it to the victims, their families and friends, and the flying public to leave no stone unturned and to learn every lesson we can. I want to thank the friends and families of the victims; their presence keeps their loved ones’ memories alive, and their advocacy reminds us to always stay focused on improving aviation safety. Due in no small part to their efforts, today we have announced an agreement in principle with the Senate on a requirement for ADS-B In and other needed reforms for our civil aviation system. I am confident that we will soon reach a final agreement on the entire comprehensive bill and pass it into law. Safety is always my highest priority – let’s get this bill done.” The lawmakers are wrapping up a final deal on the full text for a bill that resolves differences between S. 2503, the ROTOR Act, which unanimously advanced through the Senate and fell one vote short of House passage, and H.R. 7613, the Airspace Location and Enhanced Risk Transparency (ALERT) Act of 2026, which previously passed the House. ### Legal Disclaimer: EIN Presswire provides this news content "as is" without warranty of any kind.
