Count federal aid too and the two come out about even. Published November 11, 2026 $3,279 State aid per resident, least-wealthy quarter of counties $2,075 wealthiest quarter $2,585 counties Trump won (per resident) $2,282 counties Harris won (per resident) A common belief in parts of upstate New York is that Albany sends more help to other communities. The State’s own financial filings show the opposite pattern for State aid to local governments: it is larger, per resident, where there is less property wealth to tax. Aid falls as taxable wealth rises Outside New York City, the 57 counties were ranked by full property value per resident and split into four groups. State aid per resident, counting every county, city, town, village and school district in each county, falls at every step (fiscal year 2024): Quarter of counties, by property value per resident State aid per resident Least property wealth $3,279 Second $2,849 Third $2,613 Most property wealth $2,075 The same ordering holds in preliminary fiscal 2025 figures ($3,410, $3,006, $2,716 and $2,176). Across the 57 counties, State aid per resident has a rank correlation of −0.64 with property value per resident and −0.71 with median household income. School aid, which is about three-quarters of State aid outside the city, is distributed by a formula written to send more to districts with less property and income wealth, more student need and fewer pupils per square mile. By the 2024 vote Counties Trump won received $2,585 in State aid per resident in fiscal 2024; counties Harris won, including New York City, received $2,282 . Per pupil, State school aid follows the same direction: $15,223 against $14,313. That Trump-won average combines two different places. Long Island (Nassau and Suffolk counties), home to 45.3% of the people in Trump-won counties, received $2,117 per resident. The other 41 Trump-won counties received $2,973. Those 41 counties have less property per resident than Harris-won counties outside New York City ($109,485 against $132,160 in full value) but about the same total local-government revenue per resident ($8,939 against $8,993). Federal aid narrows the picture. Adding federal aid to State aid, counties Harris won received $3,404 per resident and counties Trump won $3,325 in fiscal 2024, a 2.4% difference, mostly because of New York City’s federal aid. In preliminary fiscal 2025 figures, after pandemic federal school money wound down, the two were $3,300 and $3,293. These are associations, not causes. Aid formulas do not take votes into account, and the vote is measured by county, not by household or district. What these figures cover, and what they leave out Outside New York City: what every county, city, town, village and school district reported to the State Comptroller, added up by county. Federal aid is whatever each government reported as federal. New York City files no Comptroller local-finance report, so it enters as one unit from the city’s own annual financial report: General Fund categorical aid plus State and federal aid to its capital projects fund. School aid for the city includes $1.04 billion of State building aid booked under a miscellaneous agency. Preschool special education counts as non-school aid, because outside the city that reimbursement goes to county governments. About $85 million of the city’s aid is not split between State and federal in the report; counting all of it as State aid would add about $6 per resident to counties Harris won. The city’s asylum-seeker money: the report itemizes federal money for the asylum-seeker response; State money for that response is not itemized and could be large enough to account for the Harris-won lead in combined aid. Read the combined figures as about even. Per pupil counts district and charter K-12 pupils on both sides. Pre-K pupils are left out on both sides although pre-K aid is counted; the city enrolls more pre-K pupils. Left out everywhere: money paid straight to people (Medicaid, SNAP benefits, Social Security), the State’s own spending (prisons, SUNY, State roads), the STAR credit, and taxes paid. This analysis shows what local governments receive, not whether an area pays in more than it gets back. Fiscal 2025 is preliminary. At the Comptroller’s September 30, 2026 snapshot, Wayne County’s government and 173 cities, towns and villages (920,129 residents) had not filed; their aid is missing, so those counties’ figures are floors. School districts and New York City are complete. Fiscal 2024 federal aid still includes pandemic relief for schools. Not stated here: how much of the gap the school-aid formula’s own inputs explain, and how much comes from protections for districts with shrinking enrollment. District-level estimates exist but are not confirmed and are not among the figures cited on this page. Status This analysis is published with documented limitations.
