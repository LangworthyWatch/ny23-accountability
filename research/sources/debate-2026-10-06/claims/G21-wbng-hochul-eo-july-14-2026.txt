 NEW YORK, N.Y. (WBNG) — Gov. Kathy Hochul signed an executive order on Tuesday creating what her office called the nation’s first moratorium on new hyperscale data centers, temporarily pausing certain state environmental permits for up to a year while New York develops a statewide framework to regulate the fast-growing industry.
 During a conference on Tuesday, July 14, Hochul said that New York is seeing a surge in proposals for large-scale data centers driven by artificial intelligence and other computing needs, with projects that can require major amounts of electricity and water to power and cool thousands of servers. The executive order directs the state to use the pause to craft standards aimed at protecting utility customers, the environment, the power grid, and host communities.
 The executive order also directs Empire State Development to issue a Community Investment Framework within 60 days to help local governments negotiate community benefits tied to large data center projects, including infrastructure improvements, child care investments and direct financial support. Hochul said communities should see clear returns if they accept the projects.
