 State Comptroller: Homelessness is rising in the Southern Tier 
 A report released by the OSC reveals the number of unhoused individuals in the Southern Tier rose by 35% from Jan. 2024-Jan. 2025. 
 By Daniel Curren 
 Published : Aug. 4, 2026 at 4:31 PM EDT

 BINGHAMTON, N.Y. (WBNG) -- A report from the Office of New York State Comptroller states homelessness decreased by nearly 8% around New York from January 2024 to January 2025, but the Southern Tier was a different story.
 It says overall homelessness rose 35% in the Southern Tier in that same time span, the fourth highest jump of any of the surveyed regions. It also states 19.4% of the area’s unhoused population is under 18, nearly 5% above the national average.
 Rev. Dr. Scott Parnell is the Rector of the Christ Episcopal Church in Binghamton, which runs a food pantry and clothing center for those in need. He said the report’s numbers reflect what he’s seen on the ground. 
 “I think that it’s incredibly consistent,” Parnell said. “With what we see and experience on a day-to-day basis.”
 Parnell said the Henry Street Church fed 756 people and clothed 808 in 2025. He says those numbers are on track to increase by the end of 2026. 
 “We’ve also witnessed year-to-date a 51% increase in need for food,” Parnell said. “And a 59% increase in those seeking clothing from our clothing closet.”
 Matt Ryan with the Comptroller’s office says the Capital District, Hudson Valley and Western New York are among the regions that join the Southern Tier with increased homelessness. He believes housing prices are to blame.
