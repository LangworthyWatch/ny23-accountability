New York State's Minimum Wage
Minimum Wage Rate Schedule
Starting in 2027, the minimum wage annual increase is based on the three-year moving average of the Consumer Price Index for Urban Wage Earners and Clerical Workers (CPI-W) for the Northeast Region. The law includes “off-ramp” conditions that prevent an increase in the event that specified economic conditions are met.
On January 1, 2027, the state minimum wage will remain at $17.00 per hour in New York City, Long Island and Westchester County and $16.00 per hour for the rest of the state. This is because one of the the off-ramp conditions was met.
However, the minimum wage rate for home care aides will rise on January 1, 2027 to $20.00 per hour in New York City, Long Island and Westchester County and $19.00 per hour for the rest of the state. These rates will increase by inflation but are capped at $3.00 over the standard minimum wage.
Governor Hochul has proposed legislation to adjust the wage starting in March 2027.

Enforcement of Minimum Wage Laws
New York State Department of Labor helps collect underpayments for workers who have not received the minimum wage. Often, funds are collected without resorting to court action.
However, an employer that violates the Minimum Wage Law is subject to criminal prosecution and penalties. Action may also be taken in civil court.
The Commissioner of Labor may require an employer to pay:
Minimum wage underpayments and liquidated damages, PLUS
Interest and civil penalties up to 200% of the unpaid wages
