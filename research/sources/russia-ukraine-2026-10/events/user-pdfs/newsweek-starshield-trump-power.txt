                                   Over Ukraine

                                   As confusion reigned over who was responsible for abruptly imposing limits
                                   on Ukraine's use of the Starshield satellite system, one thing did become
                                   obvious: Kyiv remains painfully at the mercy of U.S. whims.


                                   Without the military-grade sister service of Elon Musk's Starlink, Ukrainian
                                   drone pilots found last week that their unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs)
                                   couldn't fly fast enough to effectively hit Russian targets before Moscow shoots
                                   them down.



                                                                                     ADVERTISING




                                   Alongside this, Kyiv said it hadn't received the usual intelligence data on
                                   incoming, and often devastating, Russian ballistic missile strikes leveled at its
                                   major cities.

                                   Although Ukraine's President, Volodymyr Zelensky, said on Sunday intelligence
                                   flows had resumed, the two near-simultaneous drops in vital U.S. services in
                                   Ukraine became an uncomfortable demonstration of how much Ukraine still
                                   leans on its foreign backers—especially an unpredictable U.S. under President
                                   Donald Trump—to effectively fight Russian troops.


                                   The pause came as already strained relations between Washington and Kyiv
                                   were pulled even further, as Trump painted Ukraine's strikes on Russian oil
                                   refineries, rather than the U.S. war in Iran, as responsible for sky-high fuel
                                   prices in the run-up to the midterm elections.


                                   When Starshield is fully operational, Kyiv's aerial and sea drones can take out
                                   Russian logistics, munitions depots and command-and-control centers nearly
                                   125 miles from the front line, even when battered by electronic jammers.



                                                                                     ADVERTISING




                                   Although Kyiv didn't apportion blame directly over a lack of early warnings for
                                   missile onslaughts and Trump denied halting the flow of intelligence, U.S.
                                   systems are the main way for Ukraine getting advance warning of ballistic
                                   missile launches.


                                   America previously blocked sending Kyiv U.S.-derived intelligence streams in
                                   early 2025.




                                   President Donald Trump listens as Elon Musk speaks outside the West Wing of the White House on
                                   September 29, 2026, in Washington. | AP Photo/Alex Brandon



                                   In response, Ukrainian officials pleaded for the old levels of access to be
                                   restored, and Ukraine's former drone czar said a fully functional Starshield
                                   connection was "critical" for Kyiv.



                                                                                     ADVERTISING




                                   The events show "the continuing dependence of Ukraine on Starlink, the U.S.
                                   technology sector, and the U.S. administration very clearly," said Anthony King,
                                   the director of the Strategy and Security Institute at the University of Exeter in
                                   the U.K.


                                   "It was a very good reminder" of the dynamic between Washington and Kyiv,
                                   Bohdan Sas, the co-founder of Ukrainian defense technology firm Buntar
                                   Aerospace, told Newsweek. "Militaries around the world definitely have to take
                                   note."


                                   On Monday, Secretary of State Marco Rubio said the White House had not
                                   ordered the slowdown. But anonymous U.S. officials told Reuters last week the
                                   Pentagon received a message from inside the White House, saying Starshield
                                   speed had to be restricted for the Ukrainian military.


                                   Musk, meanwhile, had batted attention toward the Trump administration,
                                   saying Starshield systems are "owned and controlled" by the U.S. government,
                                   meaning "any limits on speed or usage" are dictated by the U.S. military, and,
                                   ultimately, the U.S. president.

                                   Dimko Zhluktenko, a junior sergeant with Ukraine's 413th Unmanned Systems
                                   Regiment, told Newsweek on Thursday that Starshield-enabled drones are now
                                   able to fly at around 100 miles per hour, around double the speed they could
                                   reach on Wednesday.


                                   Previously there were no speed restrictions, he said. Zelensky, in a fresh
                                   update on Monday, said Starshield speeds were still capped at some points
                                   along the front line and would be discussed with Ukrainian allies at "every
                                   level."



                                   Does Ukraine Have Any Other Options?
                                   There's no real alternative to Starshield or Starlink for Ukraine, for now.
                                   Ukrainian forces mostly use the general Starlink network for communications
                                   and data transfers, rather than guiding strikes on the battlefield.




                                   A Starlink device in use by Ukraine's 155th Brigade on the front line on February 9, 2025, in Pokrovsk,
                                   Ukraine. | Pierre Crom/Getty Images




                                                                                     ADVERTISING




                                   It's already integrated into Ukraine's military systems, and Starlink is by far the
                                   biggest constellation in orbit. Europe is slowly working on its own designs as
                                   the continent also tries to move away from decades of dependence on the U.S.
                                   military.


                                   "In the short term, Europe cannot replace this capability," the European
                                   Union's top diplomat, Kaja Kallas, said as she warned that European nations
                                   must "fill these gaps as soon as possible."


                                   This comes as the U.S. is exerting real pressure on Ukraine and loosening its
                                   chokehold on Russia, including by reaching a deal with the Kremlin on Russian
                                   diesel and waiving a raft of sanctions on Moscow expected to take effect this
                                   week.


                                   "The current administration seems to be willing to leverage a critical
                                   technological dependence to pursue its policy, even if it is disadvantageous to
                                   Ukraine," King told Newsweek.

                                   Trump's decision to pause intelligence-sharing in March 2025 coincided with
                                   the president's efforts to push peace talks between Russia and Ukraine.



                                   Fresh Energy Strikes
                                   Earlier this month, Trump declared an "energy ceasefire" between Kyiv and
                                   Moscow, and while neither Russia nor Ukraine confirmed they had agreed to
                                   the deal, there were no reported Ukrainian strikes on Russian oil refineries
                                   between October 10 and October 14.


                                   Russia, however, said it had launched devastating strikes on Ukrainian power
                                   plants throughout last week, which Ukrainian authorities said cut electricity
                                   access for 600,000 people in Kyiv. Russia targeted Ukrainian energy
                                   infrastructure at least 76 times in the week after Trump announced his energy
                                   truce, Zelensky said.


                                   Russia's Defense Ministry then said on Thursday that Ukraine had "attempted a
                                   massive attack" on an oil refinery in the Ryazan region, southeast of Moscow.
                                   Regional governor Pavel Malkov said on Telegram that a fire had broken out at
                                   the facility.

                                   toward a peace deal, which the Ukrainian leader rejects.
