                                         “Hegseth reportedly cut off weapons to Ukraine without the President’s

                                         senators want to know if he was involved here as well,” Ullyot said.

                                         Senators who support continued U.S. military aid to Ukraine believe
                                         Hegseth’s office ordered a temporary halt to military aid flights to Ukraine in
                                         early February 2025 without notifying the White House.

                                         The halt to air traffic reportedly impacted nearly a dozen shipments of
                                         artillery shells and weapons from Dover Air Force Base and a base in the
                                         United Arab Emirates.

                                         Hegseth was accused of unilaterally delaying military aid to Ukraine again in
                                         May and July of last year.

                                         The Pentagon said in July 2025 that it delayed weapons shipments to
                                         Ukraine because of a concern over low stockpiles, but lawmakers at the
                                         time rejected that rationale.

                                         Rep. Adam Smith (Wash.), the top Democrat on the House Armed Services
                                         Committee, at the time argued that the Pentagon’s stockpile of weapons
                                         and ammunition wasn’t any lower than it had been in the previous 3 1/2
                                         years of the Russia-Ukraine conflict.

                                         Republican national security insiders think that if senior DOD officials
                                         ordered a limitation on Ukraine’s access to Starshield and other critical
                                         intelligence sharing, they may have acted without Trump’s explicit
                                         knowledge or approval.

                                         Suspicion on Capitol Hill over whether Hegseth, Colby or other senior
                                         Pentagon officials played a role in limiting Ukraine’s access to Starshield
                                         gained momentum Wednesday after Elon Musk, the founder and CEO of
                                         SpaceX, which owns and operates Starlink, pointed the finger at the
                                         Pentagon.

                                         Musk wrote on X, the social media platform which he also owns, that
                                         “Starshield systems are owned and controlled by the U.S. government” and
                                         that “any limits on speed or usage are decided by the U.S. military and
                                         ultimately” the president of the United States.

                                         Ukraine’s access to Starshield was quickly restored after the disruptions to
                                         high-speed data attracted media scrutiny and criticism from Republicans
                                         and Democrats on Capitol Hill.

                                         Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky on Wednesday called the loss of
                                         access to Starshield a “big mistake.”

                                         “People will die, of course, if attacks will continue and if Starlink will not
                                         work … it’s not helpful,” he warned.
