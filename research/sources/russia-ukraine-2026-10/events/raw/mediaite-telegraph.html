Musk Pitched Cuts to Ukraine’s Starshield Direct to Trump — Who Then ‘Agreed’: Report

David GilmourOct 16th, 2026, 4:13 pm
X
Facebook
 

(AP Photo/Alex Brandon)

Elon Musk reportedly pitched President Donald Trump with the idea of slowing Ukraine’s access to the Starlink system his company built and the president “agreed” to the proposal, according to a new report.

Four sources familiar with the discussions told The Telegraph on Friday that it was Musk who had proposed imposing speed limits on Starshield, the military-grade version of SpaceX’s satellite network, as the president publicly clashed with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky.

Ukraine was hit by several deadly Russian strikes on Tuesday after the Starshield network slowed down, which Ukrainian officials said had hampered drone defenses and other battlefield operations its military uses against faster Russian attack drones.

Speaking to The Telegraph, one source said Trump “agreed” with Musk’s suggestion, clearing the way for the Pentagon to implement restrictions that Ukrainian officials say disrupted drone defenses. Reuters had previously reported that the order to restrict Starshield originated inside the White House, although it was unclear who specifically authorized it.

On Wednesday, after Ukraine reported the issue, Musk explained via X that Starshield systems were controlled by the U.S. government and that “any limits on speed or usage” were “decided by the US military and ultimately POTUS.”

Starshield systems are owned and controlled by the US government. Any limits on speed or usage are decided by the US military and ultimately POTUS.

Starlink is a commercial service owned and controlled by SpaceX. Usage of Starlink is limited to civilian or defensive activities…

— Elon Musk (@elonmusk) October 14, 2026

“He tweeted out that he was innocent and that it was the U.S. administration that did it. The reality is that they were both in it,” another source told the newspaper.

A senior European official and two Ukrainian sources reportedly corroborated that version of events.

Trump, however, while leaving for Georgia on Friday, denied that either he or Musk was responsible for Ukraine losing access, just hours after Zelensky warned that service had not returned to previous levels.

“Well, I don’t know if it was anybody, but it wasn’t me, and I don’t think it was Elon,” Trump told reporters outside the White House. “And from what I understand, it’s the same as it’s always been.”

The episode comes amid a widening rupture between Trump and Zelensky over Ukraine’s attacks on Russian oil refineries. Trump has blamed those strikes for pushing energy prices higher and last week struck a deal allowing greater Russian diesel exports after easing sanctions on Moscow.
