Rubio: Ukraine Does Not Have Access to Starshield
In brief:  US Secretary of State Marco Rubio has announced that Ukraine currently does not have access to the military-grade Starshield satellite network, noting Kyiv has requested the system for deep strikes inside Russia. Rubio firmly denied that the White House or US government ordered velocity or bandwidth caps on commercial Starlink terminals in Ukraine, dismissing President Volodymyr Zelensky’s claims that the 161 km/h cutoff was a deliberate operation.

Ukraine currently lacks access to the specialized military-grade Starshield satellite network and has requested the technology to conduct deep-interdiction strikes inside  Russia , US Secretary of State Marco  Rubio  said.
Speaking to journalists in comments released by the US Department of State, Rubio  clarified  the distinction between SpaceX’s standard commercial Starlink communications framework and the dedicated Starshield architecture operated under contract with the  US  Department of Defense.

“Starlink is a commercial product, okay? And so it’s regulated and handled as a commercial product. Starshield is a defense product, a military product,” Rubio stated.
“That is something that the United States military uses. It’s not at this moment available to Ukraine, and that’s under conversation. It’s under discussion right now how they would want to use it.”

Responding to questions regarding reports of technological throttling, Rubio directly refuted claims that the Trump administration had mandated bandwidth or velocity caps on terminals operating in Ukrainian combat sectors.
“I know Ukraine wants access to Starshield. They do not have Starshield now. They’ve asked for access to it in order to conduct deep strikes inside of Russia. They do not have access to it at this moment,” Rubio emphasized.
“Those are two different products.”

White House denies intentional velocity caps
Rubio pushed back against assertions by President Volodymyr  Zelensky  that artificial operational constraints imposed on Starlink terminals inside Ukraine were deliberate administrative measures.
“I don’t know what Zelensky said. I’m not sure Zelensky knows what he’s talking about, but it wasn’t deliberate. We have nothing to do with that,” Rubio said.
“Nobody ordered that Starlink speed to be slowed down. It sort of happened, but my understanding is it’s been restored. It’s back to where it was before. That’s Starlink, though. They were using  Starlink  for their drones.”
Advertisement
Addressing media reports suggesting executive coordination to limit unmanned systems, the secretary of state characterized the allegations as completely unfounded.
“Nothing to do with the White House. I saw a report on that the other day. It’s just categorically false,” Rubio told reporters.
“No one at the White House ordered anyone to slow down internet or slow down speed so that certain drones couldn’t use it. But my understanding is it’s back up and running. That’s what I read – that’s what we heard two days ago.”
Zelensky’s 161 km/h disclosure
Rubio’s comments follow detailed disclosures delivered on Saturday, Oct. 17, by President Zelensky, who revealed that Starlink terminals operating inside Ukrainian territory had been subjected to a strict velocity limitation of 161 km/h (100 mph).
According to Zelensky, whenever equipped domestic platforms exceeded the 161 km/h threshold,  satellite connections automatically severed , causing operators to lose control of unmanned interceptors in mid-flight.
The velocity cap effectively disabled domestic drone interceptors tasked with neutralizing high-speed, turbojet-powered Russian Shahed loitering munitions, which penetrate sovereign airspace at speeds exceeding 400 km/h (250 mph).
Advertisement
Zelensky highlighted that Ukrainian technical teams conducted cross-border comparative evaluations, sending Starlink-equipped vehicles into  EU  territory, where the devices operated uninterrupted at velocities of 180 km/h (112 mph). The Ukrainian leader argued that the trials confirmed the restrictions were geographically targeted at Ukrainian airspace rather than stemming from global software glitches.
The Starshield controversy
The technological dispute underscores widening geopolitical friction between  Washington  and Kyiv  over long-range interdiction limits  and commercial communications dependencies.
Rubio’s denials echo statements by US President Donald  Trump , who told that  neither he nor SpaceX founder Elon Musk ordered operational caps  on Ukraine’s military communications, asserting that “everything is the same as it’s always been.”
However, both official denials contradict reporting by The Telegraph, which cited four sources familiar with the matter stating that  Musk  directly proposed satellite service limitations to Trump, who approved the measure, clearing the way for the Pentagon to restrict Starshield functionality.

Sources cited in the report claimed Musk sought to shift public accountability onto the White House amid concerns over potential consumer boycotts across  European  markets following civilian casualties in Kyiv.
