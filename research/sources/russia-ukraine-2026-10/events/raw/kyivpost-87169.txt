Ukraine Seeks 450 Kph Starlink Limit to Counter Jet Drones
In brief:  Ukraine is seeking unrestricted operation of military satellite terminals after speed limits disrupted drone missions. Journalist Alex Raufoglu reported that Germany funds the service under a contract running through April 2027, while Ukrainian sources said connectivity at speeds of at least 450 kph is needed against jet-powered Shaheds. Former Defense Minister Mykhailo Fedorov announced partial restoration, but the broader restrictions remain unresolved.

Ukraine is seeking to remove speed restrictions on military satellite terminals after disruptions on Wednesday, Oct. 14, hampered drone missions. Ukrainian sources say the system must support movement at speeds of at least 450 kilometers per hour (280 miles per hour) to help counter Russian jet-powered Shahed drones.
Journalist  Alex Raufoglu wrote on X , citing a source in Kyiv, that Ukraine wants unrestricted service restored, rather than simply a modest increase in the current threshold.

“A Kyiv source tells me Ukraine needs unlimited speed restored,” he wrote.
According to Raufoglu’s source, the service previously operated without speed restrictions and is funded by Germany under a contract running through April 2027. The source said the current limit of about 160 kph (99 mph) is insufficient to counter jet-powered Shaheds.

A government source separately told RFE/RL that Ukrainian forces need connectivity at speeds of at least 450 kph (280 mph), with the broader aim of returning to unrestricted operation. The distinction matters: raising the threshold would address some operational needs, while removing it would restore the conditions Ukraine says existed before the disruption.
Partial restoration leaves the speed cap in place
Former Defense Minister Mykhailo Fedorov said on Wednesday evening that connectivity was improving for terminals moving below the existing threshold.
“Starlink connectivity at speeds of up to 160 km/h [99 mph] is being restored,” he  wrote on X .

Fedorov added that completely removing Starshield’s speed limit remained critical for Ukraine’s defense. His announcement concerned service below the threshold and did not mean restrictions on faster-moving terminals had been lifted.
According to  DW , Fedorov also said strike drones and interceptor drones should be able to fly again as connectivity recovered. DW noted that he no longer holds the defense minister’s post.
The defense ministry subsequently told RFE/RL that the formal restriction remained approximately 161 kph [100 mph], although connection quality below 160 kph [99 mph] had improved. During the day, some units had experienced interruptions at considerably lower speeds. The ministry said it was continuing discussions with partners and SpaceX about stable service conditions and preventing sudden restrictions.

What stopped working
The reported restriction concerns the speed at which a terminal is moving, rather than the speed of its internet connection.
When a drone or vehicle carrying a terminal crosses the permitted movement threshold, it can lose its satellite connection. That can interrupt communications needed to control the aircraft or relay information during a mission.
Defense ministry sources said  military Starshield users began experiencing disruptions overnight on Oct. 13-14 , without advance warning. Units reported connection losses at different speeds, ranging from approximately 72 to 161 kph (45 to 100 mph).
The interruptions affected interceptor drones, medium-range strike operations, reconnaissance, communications relays, and frontline logistics. The ministry said it was gathering reports from units, contacting the US, SpaceX, and NATO partners, and checking backup communications channels.
Advertisement
The reports concern restrictions on moving terminals, rather than a nationwide shutdown of all Starlink connections.
Musk points to US control of Starshield
SpaceX owner Elon Musk responded to Fedorov by distinguishing commercial Starlink from the military-grade Starshield service.
“Starshield systems are owned and controlled by the US government,” Musk  wrote on X .
He said decisions about speed and usage limits were made by the US military and ultimately the US president. Musk described commercial Starlink as a separate SpaceX-controlled service intended for civilian or defensive activities.
His statement did not explain who authorized the latest restrictions or why Ukrainian forces received no advance warning. A senior US official separately told Axios that the Trump administration had not been involved, leaving  conflicting accounts of responsibility .
Faster Russian drones increase the pressure
The demand for a higher operating threshold comes as Ukraine adapts its air defenses to  Russia’s growing use of jet-powered drones .
These aircraft fly faster and higher than conventional propeller-driven Shaheds, giving defenders less time to react and making interception harder for mobile fire groups and slower counter-drone aircraft.
Ukraine is developing dedicated interceptors alongside other defenses, including fighter aircraft and small missiles. President Volodymyr Zelensky said earlier this month that Ukraine was  testing five high-speed interceptor models , with one potentially reaching production of around 400 units by the end of October.

Reliable communications are another requirement for interceptors using satellite links. Restoring connections below 160 kph (100 mph) can help some missions, but it does not resolve the problem for aircraft that must fly substantially faster to reach their targets.
