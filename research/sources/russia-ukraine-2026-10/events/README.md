## 1. The Graham Act waiver (Oct 16, 2026)

### Primary document
- **White House presidential action:** "Implementing the Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and
  Iran Act of 2026." Signed "DONALD J. TRUMP / THE WHITE HOUSE, / October 16, 2026."
  The page's `datetime` stamp is 2026-10-16T20:34:24-04:00 (Friday evening).
  URL: https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/10/implementing-the-lindsey-o-graham-sanctioning-russia-and-iran-act-of-2026/
  Retained: `raw/wh-eo.html`, `raw/wh-eo.txt`
- No Federal Register citation yet (FR publication typically lags a few days). Not checked
  against federalregister.gov: **gap**.

### Statutory authority (verbatim from the order)
- Preamble: "the Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act of 2026 (22 U.S.C. 10802 et seq.)
  (Graham Act), and section 301 of title 3, United States Code."
- Sec. 1: "Section 115 of the Graham Act authorizes the President to waive the application of any
  sanctions provision ... subject to the submission to the Congress of a written certification
  that such a waiver is in the national interests of the United States and a report explaining
  the basis for the certification."
- Sec. 4(a): "Pursuant to section 115(a) of the Graham Act, I hereby waive the application of any
  duties provided for in sections 112 and 113 of the Graham Act or this order."
- Sec. 4(b): waives the section 102(d) sanctions/restrictions, while stating that "nothing in this
  order affects any sanctions or other measures provided for under any Executive Order issued to
  address the national emergency that I continued on March 24, 2026 ... (91 Fed. Reg. 15515)".
- Sec. 4(c): waives "sections 102(a), 102(e), 103, 104, 105, 106, 107, 108, 109, 110, 111, and 117."
- Sec. 4(d): waivers "remain in effect until such time as the President or the Secretary of State,
  in consultation with the Secretary of the Treasury and the Trade Representative, as appropriate,
  determine that further action is appropriate." **No expiration date.**
- Sec. 2 nominally sets duties (500% ad valorem on Russian goods; 100% on the largest 5 importers of
  Russian crude/gas and on the top 5 sanctions-evasion facilitators), each "subject to ... the
  waivers issued in section 4." So the tariffs are set out and waived in the same order.
- Sec. 5 delegates the section 115 waiver authority to the Secretary of State.
- The certification and report to Congress referenced in the order are **not public**. The order
  states no reason beyond "national interests."

### The underlying law
- H.R. 5334 (119th), "Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act of 2026." Source:
  govinfo BILLSTATUS (`raw/BILLSTATUS-119hr5334.xml`):
  - 2026-04-27 passed House by voice vote (suspension)
  - 2026-08-07 passed Senate with amendment, 86-11 (Record Vote 224)
  - 2026-09-16 House agreed to Senate amendments, 262-159 (Roll no. 308)
  - 2026-09-17 presented to President; **2026-09-18 signed, Public Law 119-111**
- 30-day implementation deadline: "within 30 days of enactment (by October 18, 2026)" (Baker Botts,
  `raw/bakerbotts-act.txt`); same date in Morgan Lewis (`raw/morganlewis-act.txt`).
- Per CNN and ABC, Sen. Lindsey Graham died in July 2026; CNN says his sister, Sen. Darline
  Graham, was appointed to his seat.

### Date math: the "a month later, to the day" claim
- Enacted Sept 18 → EO signed Oct 16 = **28 days**. **Not** "a month to the day."
- Sept 18 → Oct 18 (the statutory deadline, when the sanctions would have taken effect) = 30 days,
  and that date is the one-month-to-the-day mark.
- So the third-party post is off by two days for the order. It is accurate only if read as
  "the sanctions were due a month later to the day, and the waiver came two days before that."
  ABC: "just two days before they were set to take effect." RFE/RL: "two days before the sanctions
  package was due to take effect on October 18."

### Related earlier action (the "second time in as many weeks")
- **OFAC General License No. 135**, dated October 9, 2026 (signed by Director Bradley T. Smith),
  authorizes transactions "related to the sale, delivery, offloading, or importation, including
  importation into the United States, of diesel fuel of Russian Federation origin ... through
  12:01 a.m. eastern daylight time, April 7, 2027." Retained: `raw/ofac-gl135.pdf`, `.txt`.
  URL: https://ofac.treasury.gov/media/937216/download

## 2. The Starshield/Starlink report

### What the user heard vs. what was reported
"He called for downgraded Ukrainian Starshield capabilities during an attack" is **stronger than
any retained report.** No report says Trump called for it. The reporting is:

1. **Reuters exclusive, Oct 15, 2026** (Erin Banco, Gram Slattery). Retained via the U.S. News
   syndication (`raw/reuters-usnews-starshield.txt`): "The order to limit a satellite system used by
   Ukraine for battlefield communications and drone operations came from inside the White House,
   according to two US officials and a Western official briefed on the matter." The officials
   "spoke on the condition of anonymity, did not specify who in the White House made the
   decision." A senior US official: "No such request was ever made by the president." The White
   House did not answer whether others made it. SpaceX, DoD, and the Ukrainian embassy did not
   respond. A senior Ukrainian official said "Kyiv was told it was a White House decision."
2. **The Telegraph, Oct 16** (URL: telegraph.co.uk/world-news/2026/10/16/musk-approached-trump-slow-down-starlink-help-ukraine/).
   **Primary not retained** (bot wall in the browser; not bypassed). Retained secondhand via
   Mediaite (`raw/mediaite-telegraph.txt`): "Four sources familiar with the discussions told The
   Telegraph" that Musk proposed the speed limits; "one source said Trump 'agreed.'" Anonymous
   sourcing. Cite only as "The Telegraph reported, per Mediaite" until the original is retained.

### Timing ("during an attack")
- Kyiv Post (`raw/kyivpost-87169.txt`): Defense ministry sources said disruptions began "overnight
  on Oct. 13-14, without advance warning," with losses at about 72 to 161 km/h.
- Mediaite: "Ukraine was hit by several deadly Russian strikes on Tuesday after the Starshield
  network slowed down." So the restriction coincided with Russian strikes. No source says it was
  ordered *because of* or *during* a specific attack.

### Denials and on-record statements
- Trump, Oct 16 (CNN `raw/cnn-starshield.txt`): "I didn't do that"; "Well, I don't know if it was
  anybody, because it wasn't me, and I don't think it was Elon (Musk)."
- Rubio (Kyiv Post 87400, `raw/kyivpost-rubio.txt`): "Nobody ordered that Starlink speed to be slowed
  down. It sort of happened"; "It's just categorically false"; and **"They do not have Starshield
  now."**
- Musk on X, Oct 14 (quoted in Mediaite/Reuters): "Starshield systems are owned and controlled by the
  US government. Any limits on speed or usage are decided by the US military and ultimately POTUS."
- The White House to Axios: denied involvement (reported secondhand by Kyiv Post and Ukrainska
  Pravda; **Axios original not retained**).
- **Pentagon:** no on-record statement found; it "did not respond" per Reuters.
- Two senior US officials told CNN the order did not come from Trump.

### Starshield vs. Starlink: the record is contested
- Reuters, CNN, and Musk call it **Starshield** (government-owned, military-grade service on the
  Starlink constellation).
- Rubio says Ukraine **does not have Starshield** and the slowed service was **Starlink**.
- Rep. Mike Turner on Face the Nation (Newsweek, `raw/newsweek-investigation.txt`): "The policy
  decision has been made that Starshield will be utilized" for Ukraine's defense. He called for a
  congressional investigation.
- **Recommendation:** write "military-grade Starlink/Starshield terminals" and note the dispute.

### Status: reported, not confirmed
The White House origin rests on anonymous officials (Reuters). The Musk proposal and Trump's
agreement rest on anonymous sources (Telegraph, via Mediaite). Both are denied on record. Who
ordered it is unconfirmed. **Classify as REPORTED.**

### Note on the ~80% radio figure
**No Sept-Oct 2026 national poll was found showing about 80% of Americans supporting Ukraine or
opposing Russia.** Searches covered Gallup, Pew, Chicago Council, Reagan Institute, Quinnipiac,
AP-NORC, and Marist. The closest real figures:
- 82% for "tougher economic sanctions on Russia," from a **June 2026, advocacy-sponsored** poll of
  registered voters (POS). This is the likeliest source of an "80%" figure.
- Pew: "eight-in-ten or more in each party" view Russia/Putin negatively (Mar 2026; the "Russia as
  enemy" item is from a Jan 2026 survey).
- 74-76% of Democrats sympathize more with Ukraine (YouGov). That is a subgroup, not all Americans.
- The current national level is **57%** sympathizing more with Ukraine (YouGov, Oct 14), down from
  62% in April.
