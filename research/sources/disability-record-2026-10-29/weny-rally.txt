ELMIRA, N.Y. — Advocates for people with disabilities rallied outside Congressman Nick Langworthy’s Elmira office Tuesday afternoon, calling on Congress to pass federal legislation supporting independent living.
People with disabilities, independent living advocacy groups and nonprofit organizations gathered to urge passage of the 
Latonya Reeves Freedom Act
, a bipartisan bill that would build on the Americans with Disabilities Act. The legislation is intended to ensure that people with intellectual, developmental and physical disabilities are not institutionalized, and have the ability to live freely in their communities. Advocates described the bill as a matter of civil rights for the disability community.
The bill is named for Latonya Reeves, a Tennessee woman who was institutionalized as a child. With support from an independent living center, she moved to Colorado, where advocates say she lived the rest of her life in the community rather than in a nursing home.
Those at the rally said Rep. Langworthy supports the bill. They are asking Congress to pass it when members return to Washington after the election.
“Having the strong support from Congressman Langworthy and so many others is so heartening to make sure that we continue to have the ability to live in our community and be active participants that contribute to society,” said Bryan O’Malley, vice president for external affairs at the Center for Disability Rights.
Rebecca Spinosa, whose son Jonathan has a disability, said the bill would give her family important choices.
“It means a lot to me and my husband to know that Jonathan will have the choice to live at home or where he wants to be, but more importantly, he has the choice to stay in our community, and that’s really important to us,” Spinosa said.
The bill is currently in the Health, Education, Labor and Pensions Committee in the U.S. Senate, and was referred in the House of Representatives to the Committee on Energy and Commerce, of which Rep. Langworthy is a member, as well as the Committee on the Judiciary.
