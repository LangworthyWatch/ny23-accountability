                                                                    testify, according to one person granted anonymity to share details of the private

                                                                    recessing until after the November elections.

                                                                 House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries calls on a reporter during a press conference at the U.S. Capitol on June 23, 2026.

                                                                                                   in order to build data centers,” Rep. Jim McGovern said.

                                                                                                                CALEN RAZOR
                                                                                                                09/03/2026, 8:00AM ET




                                                                                                   Democrats are trying to pin voter uproar over data centers on Republicans by
                                                                                                   spotlighting a tax provision buried in the GOP’s signature megabill last year.


                                                                                                   “A year ago, Republicans were bragging about giving tax breaks to Big Tech in order
                                                                                                   to build data centers,” Rep. Jim McGovern said. “With November on the horizon,
                                                                                                   some House Republicans are acting as if they did not turbocharge the data center
                                                                                                   buildout.”

                                                                                                                                                                              AD




                                                                                                   Democrats are hoping their messaging not only drives a wedge in the deepening
                                                                                                   Republican divide over data centers, but also undercuts GOP attempts to trumpet
                                                                                                   their One Big Beautiful Bill Act as a crowning legislative achievement ahead of the
                                                                                                   midterms.


                                                                                                   The fresh line of attack zeroes in on the “Opportunity Zone” tax credit program — an
                                                                                                   initiative designed to boost growth in areas deemed underdeveloped, which, at one
                                                                                                   point after the megabill’s passage, Republicans had touted as a way to spur the
                                                                                                   construction of data centers.


                                                                                                   President Donald Trump’s Monday post calling for Americans to “let Data Reign”
                                                                                                   gave Democrats their latest opening to strike a contrast, as Republicans struggle to
                                                                                                   bridge Trump’s unwavering support with their own constituents’ concerns.


                                                                                                   Poll after poll shows growing frustrations with the centers throughout the country,
                                                                                                   including in key House and Senate races necessary for the GOP to hold onto its
                                                                                                   majorities.


                                                                                                   Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries in a news conference this week accused House
                                                                                                   Republicans of voting to “unleash data centers on the American people in an
                                                                                                   unfettered fashion.”


                                                                                                   Rep. David Valadao of California, for example, has found himself under scrutiny for
                                                                                                   his vote on the 2025 megabill, but pushed back on Democrats’ strategy.


                                                                                                   “I mean, obviously there are local concerns that need to be addressed,” Valadao said
                                                                                                   in an interview, but added of Democrats’ attacks that, “trying to use something that a
                                                                                                   lot of people would argue is important for national security or national security
                                                                                                   interests as a political weapon sounds pretty irresponsible.”


                                                                                                   House Ways and Means Chair Jason Smith, who oversaw the expansion of the tax
                                                                                                   credits in the law, called Democrats’ narrative a “huge stretch.”


                                                                                                   “Opportunity Zones control, like, everything — from child daycare centers to
                                                                                                   manufacturers. Can data centers be used in them? Yes. But so can everything else,”
                                                                                                   Smith said.


                                                                                                   An analysis from the National Community Reinvestment Coalition published this
                                                                                                   month figures 14 percent of data centers are in Opportunity Zones, while 17 percent
                                                                                                   of those in the works are similarly situated.


                                                                                                   Some Republicans, nonetheless, have been pressing Speaker Mike Johnson on
                                                                                                   whether leadership has plans to put any data center-related legislation on the floor in
                                                                                                   the next month, but have received no commitments.

                                                                                                   midterms. “We’re going to take that final 30 days, you don’t want to go too much
                                                                                                   before that because people forget,” the president said, adding that he will rely on
                                                                                                   Johnson to supply names where the Republican is tied, losing or winning by a small
                                                                                                   margin.

                                                                                                   House Democrats are watching closely to see if Jeffries will follow through on reining
                                                                                                   in rogue members who helped bail Republicans out of a nearly failed agenda this
                                                                                                   week. Many are hoping their leaders send a message to Reps. Marie Gluesenkamp
                                                                                                   Perez and Jared Golden that such tactics won’t be tolerated in a future majority.
                                                                                                   Some have privately suggested kicking Gluesenkamp Perez off of her role on

                                                                                                   Third Way rolls out “new economic

                                                                                                   The platform consists of what the group calls 20 “ambitious and ruthlessly
                                                                                                   pragmatic” ideas, shared first with POLITICO, targeting working-class voters.

                                                                                                                ADAM WREN
                                                                                                                09/03/2026, 5:55AM ET




                                                                                                   The Democratic centrist group Third Way is launching a “new economic bargain”
                                                                                                   platform as part of its ongoing effort to steer the party toward a more coherent and
                                                                                                   big-tent vision heading into 2028.


                                                                                                   The platform consists of what it calls 20 “ambitious and ruthlessly pragmatic” ideas,
                                                                                                   shared first with POLITICO, targeting working-class voters. The ideas include a
                                                                                                   health care Bill of Rights, government-backed car loans to lower the cost of car
                                                                                                   ownership and forcing AI companies to pay for their own expansion without passing
                                                                                                   it on to consumers.


                                                                                                                                                                         Advertisement




                                                                                                                                                                              AD




                                                                                                   “Now that the primaries are over, the big question for Democrats is: What are you
                                                                                                   going to run on in the general election in ’26? What are you going to legislate on in
                                                                                                   ’27? And what are you going to promise to do and deliver if you become president?”
                                                                                                   Jon Cowan, Third Way’s president, said in an interview. “And so we see this now as
                                                                                                   the pivot moment away from the primaries and toward the policy debate in the
                                                                                                   party.”


                                                                                                   Cowan said the policy ideas provide an affirmative vision for the party, instead of just
                                                                                                   being anti-Trump, saying the agenda is aimed at “Democrats who want to be able to
                                                                                                   beat MAGA, because here’s the key thing: you can’t beat something with nothing.”


                                                                                                   Earlier this year, the influential think tank launched a $50 million effort seeking to
                                                                                                   make sure a “combative centrist” emerges as the party’s 2028 presidential nominee.


                                                                                                   Now, Third Way has elevated a dedicated staffer — Sasha Galbreath, a longtime
                                                                                                   House policy adviser — to establish connections between the group and staffers for
                                                                                                   potential 2028 presidential and vice presidential candidates. The group is already in
                                                                                                   conversation with at least 16 potential candidates and their staff, according to Cowan.


                                                                                                   “The ideas primary is really only just beginning now,” Cowan said. “I think the pivot
                                                                                                   into September and the end of the primaries is actually when the real ideas primary
                                                                                                   to determine the future of the Democratic Party begins.”
