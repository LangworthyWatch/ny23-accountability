   I now recognize myself for 5 minutes for a statement on the re-
port.
   Today, the House of Representatives Committee on Oversight
and Government Reform meets to advance a resolution recom-
mending that the House find Leon D. Black in contempt of Con-
gress for refusing to comply with duly issued subpoenas, our inves-
tigation examining how Federal Government for decades failed sur-
vivors of Jeffrey Epstein and Ghislaine Maxwell’s crimes, and how
Mr. Epstein sought to curry favor and exercise influence to avoid
scrutiny.
   I subpoenaed Leon Black for testimony and documents critical to
our investigation, but he has refused to comply. Leon Black is le-
gally required to appear for a deposition before the Oversight Com-
mittee and required to turn over all nondisclosure agreements to
which he was a party. The Committee also asked for all Non-Dis-
closure Agreement (NDA)s and confidentiality agreements that Ep-
stein and Maxwell were involved or referenced in. He defied Con-
gress and refused to do either. Instead, Mr. Black is hiding behind
litigation to delay having to provide answers to the American peo-
ple. No one is above the law. Mr. Black will be treated the same
as anyone else in this investigation, and his actions have con-
sequences.
   A little over two months ago, Mr. Black appeared before the
Committee for a transcribed interview, but only an hour into the
interview he began refusing to answer questions. So, we issued two
subpoenas to him, one for a deposition and one to produce his con-
fidentiality agreements. Mr. Black did not appear for a scheduled
deposition on September 3, 2026, and only produced one NDA to
the Committee, despite his counsel indicating there were many
more. Subpoenas are not suggestions. They carry the force of law
and require full compliance.
   Mr. Black’s testimony is crucial to our investigation because he
maintained a close personal relationship with Epstein for years
during the time when Epstein was engaged in sex trafficking. Mr.
Black and Epstein also had a significant financial relationship. He
funded Epstein’s lavish lifestyle for years, enabling Epstein to con-
tinue his horrific crimes. His testimony about Epstein, Maxwell,
and their crimes is invaluable. The Committee wants to ensure
that NDAs or other confidentiality agreements cannot be used to
cover up criminal activities. We made it clear to Mr. Black and his
attorneys that if he did not comply, then the Committee would ini-
tiate contempt proceedings. We have no choice but to hold him in
contempt. I encourage my colleagues to support this measure.

   I now yield to Ranking Member Garcia for his opening state-
ment.
   Mr. GARCIA. Thank you, Chairman Comer, and we are really
here today on a, really, bipartisan basis because Leon Black is re-
fusing to cooperate with our investigation as an Oversight Com-
mittee on the investigation, of course, of Jeffrey Epstein.
   Now, we believe that Leon Black is at the heart of the Epstein
network. This is a private equity billionaire worth roughly $13 bil-
lion. We need to know why Jeffrey Epstein got over $180 million
from Mr. Black. We know this money enabled Epstein’s activities
for years, and we need to know how and if it led to the abuse of
women and girls. We need to know about Leon Black and Jeffrey
Epstein’s financial dealings. We need to know about the role of the
Big Banks, and we need to, for us as a Committee, focus on how
we, in the future, combat money laundering and sex trafficking.
This investigation is important and can lead to so much for the
protection of women and girls across this country.
   We also need to know about the involvement of other powerful
and wealthy men and how law enforcement failed to protect sur-
vivors. We need to know about Mr. Black’s knowledge of Epstein’s
inner circle and activities. We need to know why he used nondisclo-
sure agreements to silence women connected to Jeffrey Epstein, but
Leon Black is refusing to provide this information to Congress.
Now, this Committee has tried to work with him to get answers,
but he has had no interest in cooperating, and I do want to thank
Chairman Comer for his efforts. We support and did support his ef-
forts on these subpoenas, and they are absolutely the right thing
to do to get to the truth.
   We should not allow Leon Black to defy congressional subpoenas.
Our investigation is about fighting for the survivors of Epstein’s
abuse, and we are going to develop reforms and legal changes to
ensure the system that allowed Jeffrey Epstein and his billionaire
friends to continue their activities for years comes to an end.
   Now, Leon Black walked out of an interview with our Committee.
He refused to turn over documents. He refused to show up and tes-
tify for the deposition. That is two congressional subpoenas now
that he has defied. It is unacceptable to this Committee, and, un-
fortunately, billionaires like Leon Black believe that they are above
the law. Some of them certainly do. He believes that he is above
this Committee’s oversight authority. He believes he is too powerful
for Congress or the American people to touch, but we are going to
stand up and remind Leon Black and the American people that
subpoenas are not suggestions.
   Finally, I want to note that Leon Black certainly is not the only
witness who has refused to answer our questions. For example, of
course, Alan Dershowitz has repeatedly said he would appear be-
fore this Committee to talk about Jeffrey Epstein, but when given
the opportunity, he has also backed out twice. Now, Dershowitz
may have been the President’s lawyer, but he is also not above the
law in answering this Committee’s questions. Alan Dershowitz
should come forward and tell the American people and our Com-
mittee what he knows, and certainly today, this Committee should
be focused on Leon Black and moving this motion that is in front
of us forward to the full House.
