NEW YORK STATE (WENY) —
Counties across New York were just awarded over $76 million to improve rural healthcare.
The money comes from the
Rural Community Health Integration initiative
(RCHI), which is giving 90 awards to 56 different organizations across the state. Funding is broken down into two categories: $22.7 million for planning awards and $53.5 million for implementation awards. There is an additional $1 million to increase the number of school-based health centers. 
Additionally, this award is part of a larger $212 million funding effort given to New York State through the federal
Rural Health Transformation program
(RHT). 
The rest of the money is going towards other facets of rural healthcare, like increasing access to high-quality medical technology, strengthening the healthcare workforce, cybersecurity, and information exchange programs. 
The RHT is a federal program which will provide around $50 billion to qualifying states from fiscal year 2026 to 2030. Administered by the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services, the program aims to improve healthcare access and effectiveness in rural communities. The RCHI is New York’s specific arm of the RHT, designed to distribute funds from the RHT to rural counties across the state. 
Specific funding awardees have not been announced yet, but Steuben, Chemung, Tioga, Schuyler, Tompkins, and Yates counties were all eligible.
