		Editorial: Missing Person Alert: Congressman Langworthy 
                    By Webdesk Advertiser                
on 
                    June 25, 2026                
The League of Women Voters recently hosted a forum for candidates seeking to represent New York’s 23rd Congressional District. Held at Clarence High School on June 17, the event featured Democratic candidates Kevin Stocker and Aaron Gies, who are competing for their party’s nomination.
One candidate was noticeably absent: Congressman Nick Langworthy.
According to the League of Women Voters, Langworthy neither attended the forum nor responded to the invitation. That is disappointing.
Campaign season is when candidates are supposed to be meeting voters, answering questions and defending their records. Public forums provide a valuable opportunity for constituents to hear directly from those seeking elected office and to compare candidates side by side. For an incumbent seeking another term, such appearances should be expected, not avoided.
Over the past 18 months, this newspaper has received numerous letters from readers requesting that Congressman Langworthy hold a public town hall meeting. Constituents have expressed concerns about federal policies, government spending, border security, foreign affairs and other issues affecting their daily lives. To date, there has been no public commitment to hold such a forum.
Representing a congressional district involves more than casting votes in Washington. It also requires maintaining a dialogue with the people back home. Voters deserve opportunities to ask questions, raise concerns and hear directly from the individual elected to represent them.
Congressman Langworthy may have legitimate reasons for declining this particular event. But when a candidate skips public forums, declines debate invitations and does not hold open town hall meetings, constituents are left wondering when and where they can engage with their representative.
If voters cannot get answers from their elected officials, perhaps it is time to direct those questions to the political organizations supporting their campaigns. Accountability is not just the responsibility of candidates; it is also the responsibility of the parties that nominate and endorse them.
Public service requires public engagement. The people of the 23rd District deserve nothing less.
