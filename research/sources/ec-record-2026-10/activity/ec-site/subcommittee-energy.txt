Subcommittee
 on 
Energy
National Energy Policy, energy infrastructure and security, energy related Agencies and Commissions, all laws, programs, and government activities affecting energy matters. National Energy Policy focuses on fossil energy; renewable energy; nuclear energy; energy conservation, utility issues, including but not limited to interstate energy compacts; energy generation, marketing, reliability, transmission, siting, exploration, production, efficiency, cybersecurity, and ratemaking for all generated power. Energy infrastructure and security focuses on pipelines, the strategic petroleum reserve, nuclear facilities, and cybersecurity for our nation’s grid. Our jurisdiction also includes all aspects of the above-referenced jurisdiction related to the Department of Homeland Security. Agencies and Commissions in our jurisdiction include: The US Department of Energy, the Nuclear Regulatory Commission;  and the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission.

Guthrie and Fulcher Celebrate President Trump’s Signing of Bipartisan Hydropower Licensing Transparency Act
WASHINGTON, D.C.
 – Today, Congressman Brett Guthrie (KY-02), Chairman of the House Committee on Energy and Commerce, and Congressman Russ Fulcher (ID-01), applauded President Donald Trump’s signing of H.R. 3657, the Hydropower Licensing Transparency Act. This bipartisan law will help protect reliable, baseload hydropower by making the federal relicensing process more transparent and accountable.
“In many communities across the country, hydropower provides the reliable and affordable electricity that Americans depend on,”
said Chairman Guthrie.
“As demand for electricity continues to grow, it’s vital that hydropower relicensing isn’t burdened by unworkable red tape. The Hydropower Licensing Transparency Act brings needed accountability to the relicensing process and will help ensure the continued flow of water-powered electricity. Thank you to President Trump for signing this legislation into law and to Congressman Fulcher for his tireless work to unleash American energy.”
“I am proud to see the Hydropower Licensing Transparency Act signed into law. This is an important piece of legislation that will effectively cut federal red tape around hydropower relicensing applications,”
said Rep. Fulcher.
“With nearly 600 water storage dams across Idaho, we must focus on efficient licensing processes to keep our dams running and support clean energy growth.”
BACKGROUND:
Congresswoman Kim Schrier (WA-08) and Congressman Russ Fulcher (ID-01) introduced H.R. 3657, the Hydropower Licensing Transparency Act, which requires FERC to report to Congress on the status of relicensing applications for hydropower dams.
Within 180 days of enactment, FERC must deliver its first report, providing a better assessment of baseload generation that will help keep hydropower facilities from retiring prematurely.
The legislation was reported by the Committee on Energy and Commerce by a vote of 47-0, before being passed by a voice vote in the House of Representatives and by unanimous consent in the Senate.

Chairman Guthrie Op-Ed: POWER Act puts permitting reform at center of energy affordability
WASHINGTON, D.C.
 – The following op-ed by Congressman Brett Guthrie (KY-02), Chairman of the House Committee on Energy and Commerce, appeared in the 
Washington Times
 this week.
“Over the past half-decade, energy prices for Americans have risen.
“This has often forced them to make tough decisions about what other goods and services they can afford. This should not be the norm. Working families deserve access to abundant and reliable energy, giving them flexibility to invest their hard-earned savings in whatever they deem most important.
“That goal is achievable, but not through unworkable electricity rate caps, green energy mandates or data center moratoriums, as we have seen proposed by blue-state governors.
“To lower prices while securing our grid, I have released the POWER Act, draft legislation that provides the commonsense permitting reforms needed to bring baseload energy onto our grid while promoting innovation.
“America’s permitting system is broken. Slow, burdensome and unpredictable review processes have produced an unreliable grid and higher costs for consumers. Manufacturers have been forced to contend with longer construction timelines and higher project costs that put the United States at a competitive disadvantage.
“In Democratic-run states, renewable portfolio standards, which are effectively green energy mandates, undermine the reliability of the grid and increase prices. Meanwhile, federal and state regulations are forcing reliable power to shut down before replacements are ready, creating a reliability crisis.
“Layered on top of these challenges, supply chain constraints and equipment backlogs are also slowing the building of industrial projects that America needs to compete.
“The POWER Act addresses these issues by combining into one piece of legislation the commonsense permitting reforms that America needs to speed up the construction of pipelines, power generation and electric transmission.
“This legislation contains three main pillars: producing energy, moving energy and powering industry to build and grow.
“Producing energy domestically is key to securing American energy dominance. From the outset of this Congress, through related hearings and legislation, our House Energy and Commerce Committee has focused on finding ways to generate the reliable, affordable power that our communities need.
“Last winter, we saw the consequences of relying on intermittent sources such as wind and solar, when New England states were forced to burn trash to keep the lights on after renewables failed to provide enough electricity.
“The Power Plant Reliability Act would help address this issue by empowering the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission to keep reliable power plants running for up to five years when retiring them would threaten the grid.
“As we work to build our grid and meet America’s growing power demand, driven partly by the need to support data centers, the POWER Act includes the text of the Ratepayer Protection Act. This section builds on the White House’s Ratepayer Protection Pledge, under which Amazon, Google, Meta, Microsoft, OpenAI, Oracle, xAI and other signers, including utilities and regulators, committed to covering their own data center energy costs.
“While developing the legislation, committee members held hearings and meetings with numerous stakeholders, including the data center industry and Big Tech, state regulators, utilities and community members. Through that work, it has become clear that across the entire industry, one entity is uniquely positioned to stand up for families — the ones paying electricity bills: Congress.
“Modernizing our nation’s infrastructure and building the pipelines needed to carry the oil and natural gas that provide baseload power are critical to lowering prices. That is why this legislation includes the GRID Power Act, which would ensure that grid operators have the tools they need to secure the reliability of the electricity grid in the event of a capacity shortfall.
“Likewise, the Improving Interagency Coordination for Pipeline Reviews Act streamlines permitting for interstate natural gas pipelines at the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission, supporting the development of dispatchable natural gas to communities across the country.
“Access to affordable, reliable power will be key as we work to increase domestic manufacturing and power innovative new technologies. For too long, burdensome regulations have stood in the way of projects that would help deliver the energy we need.
“That is why the POWER Act includes steps to make the permitting process faster and safer. The bill does this, in part, by streamlining the New Source Review permitting process for companies to upgrade facilities and install modern pollution controls, avoiding delays that would otherwise lead to higher costs.
“Far too often, duplicative reviews slow development while providing no tangible benefits to families. The RED Tape Act ends double reviews of projects by the Environmental Protection Agency if the projects are already vetted under the National Environmental Policy Act.
“Together, these individual measures form the basis for the permitting reform needed to deliver dispatchable baseload power to American families. Permits that take years to acquire slow down development and raise prices for families and businesses.
“The POWER Act cuts through that red tape, helping deliver the energy we need today.”
