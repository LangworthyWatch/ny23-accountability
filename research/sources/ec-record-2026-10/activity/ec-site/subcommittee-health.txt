WTAS: E&C Republicans’ Agenda “Continuing the Fight Against Fraud”
WASHINGTON, D.C.
 – Today, Congressman Brett Guthrie (KY-02), Chairman of the House Committee on Energy and Commerce, highlighted additional statements of support for Energy and Commerce Republicans’ agenda “Continuing the Fight Against Fraud.”
Don’t miss what advocates are saying:
Minnesota State Representative Kristin Robbins, House District 37A, in support of 
Medicaid Whistleblower Protection Act
:
“I am so grateful for the leadership of Congresswoman Miller-Meeks and the House Energy & Commerce Committee for introducing this Whistleblower Protection bill. Too often, as we have seen in Minnesota, brave whistleblowers are retaliated against by politicians and agency leaders who are more interested in protecting their political base than taxpayers and vulnerable citizens. The federal, state and county workers who run these programs are often the first to see red flags but, as we have seen time and again in Minnesota, if they dare to point it out, they are called racist, accused of creating a hostile work environment, demoted, reassigned or otherwise harmed personally and professionally.
“The Whistleblower Protection bill will expand protections for federal, state and county employees so they can do what they do best – serve the public, help vulnerable citizens and protect taxpayers’ hard-earned money. States across the country should look at their own whistleblower protection statutes and ensure that whistleblowers can report to someone outside their agency, such as legislators, federal officials, or law enforcement. They should also make sure the statute of limitations is long enough to provide robust protection for whistleblowers and to ensure fraud crimes are prosecuted. These cases can take years to come to light and investigate, and we need to ensure accountability by providing at least a 10-year statute of limitations.
“I am so grateful for the brave whistleblowers who CONTINUE to reach out and expose fraud in Minnesota, often at great personal and professional risk. They have made an enormous difference in our state, and it has led to the national efforts to fight fraud across the country. The whistleblowers are unsung heroes, and they deserve our protection at the state and federal level.”
Rachel Barkley, Director, Able Americans:
“Medicaid is a lifeline for millions of Americans with disabilities, making it possible for people to receive essential health care and long-term services and supports, live independently, and participate in their communities. Protecting the integrity of this program is essential to protecting the people who depend on it.
“Able Americans applauds the House Energy and Commerce Committee for advancing commonsense reforms to combat fraud, waste, and abuse in Medicaid and strengthen accountability for taxpayer dollars. Every dollar lost to fraud is a dollar that cannot be used to provide services and supports to people who genuinely need them.
“We particularly welcome efforts to strengthen oversight of home- and community-based services while preserving states’ ability to administer programs effectively. Strong program integrity and strong services for people with disabilities are not competing goals—they go hand in hand.
“Americans with disabilities deserve a Medicaid program that is sustainable, accountable, and focused on the people it was created to serve.”
Brandon Arnold, Executive Vice President, National Taxpayers Union:
“National Taxpayers Union (NTU) applauds Chairman Brett Guthrie and his colleagues on the House Energy and Commerce Committee for introducing a series of bills to root out fraud and abuse in the Medicaid program. With the national debt now exceeding $40 trillion, it’s imperative to cut the fat out of the federal budget. That process should begin with reducing waste, fraud and abuse in programs like Medicaid, which issued more than $31 billion in improper payments in fiscal year 2024. NTU looks forward to working with the Chairman and members of the Committee on this critically important effort.”
Dean Clancy, Senior Health Policy Fellow, Americans for Prosperity:
“Congratulations to members of the House Energy and Commerce Committee for leading the fight to eliminate wasteful spending in Medicaid, an essential part of making health care affordable for all Americans. Too many safety-net dollars today are diverted from needy families to greedy fraudsters, or else lost to bureaucratic errors and weak financial controls. This ultimately inflates costs for everyone. By giving states strong new tools to detect, prevent, and recoup funds, you are protecting not only the taxpayer but also patients’ access to affordable health care.”
Jenn Pellegrino, Founder, Defend Forgotten America:
“Defend Forgotten America applauds the House Energy and Commerce Committee’s efforts to strengthen Medicaid program integrity by giving states better tools and incentives to detect fraud, recover improper payments, improve accountability, and ensure taxpayer dollars reach the patients and providers who legitimately depend on them.
“Importantly, these bills would increase transparency, coordination, and data sharing among local, state, and federal agencies so officials can identify vulnerabilities and fraudulent activity earlier and act on reliable, actionable information.
“Energy and Commerce Republicans are putting forth commonsense reforms that can improve accountability across every level of government and protect the long-term integrity of Medicaid for the Americans it was designed to serve.”
Maya MacGuineas, President, Committee for a Responsible Federal Budget and Fix the Debt:
“We should be focusing on the best ways to bring health care costs down for the federal government; that includes making every effort to ensure health care spending actually pays for care. Focusing on opportunities to strengthen program integrity, improving managed care oversight, and going after improper payments ultimately makes Medicaid – and our fiscal health overall – healthier in the long-run. Lawmakers are right to expand the Medicaid Recovery Audit (RAC) program which takes a commonsense step toward this goal, and we applaud it.”

WTAS: E&C Republicans’ Agenda “Continuing the Fight Against Fraud”
WASHINGTON, D.C.
 – Today, Congressman Brett Guthrie (KY-02), Chairman of the House Committee on Energy and Commerce, highlighted widespread support for Energy and Commerce Republicans’ agenda “Continuing the Fight Against Fraud."
Don’t miss what advocates are saying:
Scott Partika, Director, Ohio Department of Medicaid, in support of the 
Strengthening Medicaid Analytics to Reduce Theft (SMART) Act
:
“We welcome initiatives that strengthen state–federal collaboration to safeguard the Medicaid program and appreciate Congressman Balderson’s leadership on the SMART Act. Expanding access to interoperable analytic tools would meaningfully support Ohio Medicaid’s oversight and transparency efforts and help further prevent fraud, waste, and abuse.”
Lanhee J. Chen, Ph.D., David and Diane Steffy Fellow in American Public Policy Studies and Co-Chair, Health Care Policy Working Group, Hoover Institution at Stanford University:
“This comprehensive package of legislation includes important reforms that will ensure that Medicaid continues to serve those who are legitimately eligible for the program, while cracking down on fraudsters seeking to cheat American taxpayers. By enhancing program integrity, making data-analytic tools widely available to assist in the detection of program waste, fraud and abuse, and aligning incentives to ensure that states share in the responsibility of ensuring Medicaid benefits only go to those who should get them, these bills will ensure that Medicaid operates not only more effectively, but in a more fiscally sustainable manner. Congratulations to Chairman Guthrie on his leadership and to the members of the Committee who have sponsored these important pieces of legislation.”
Keith Miskie, President of United Council on Welfare Fraud (UCOWF), in support of the 
Medicaid Whistleblower Protection Act
:
“Imagine two individuals doing the same day-to-day work in the same federal safety net program, but one does it as a federal employee in Washington, DC while the other does it as a state or local employee. The one in Washington, DC as a federal employee enjoys full federal whistleblower protections versus the state or local employee that identifies wrongdoing does NOT, putting themselves and their families at personal risk. Sadly, this is the reality in our nation. This ‘second class citizen’ approach to state and local fraud fighters represents a serious gap in our nation’s efforts to curb fraud, waste, and abuse. America’s fraud investigators are the frontline defenders of beneficiaries and taxpayers, and as the ‘boots on the ground’ ensuring our nation’s safety net is preserved for future generations, they desperately need this legislation. So, on behalf of the United Council on Welfare Fraud – the nation’s only professional organization solely dedicated to the prevention, detection, investigation, prosecution, and recovery of fraud in public assistance programs – we applaud the introduction of this legislation, and look forward to working with Chairman Guthrie, and his colleagues in the House, Senate and Administration to make this a reality and create an even playing field for America’s fraud fighters.”
Tarren Bragdon, President and CEO at the Foundation for Government Accountability:
“Medicaid exists to serve the truly needy, but bureaucrat-created loopholes and weak guardrails have left the program wide open to fraud, waste, and abuse. Every dollar taken by fraud is a dollar robbed from taxpayers and from the very people most relying on Medicaid. This builds on the anti-fraud reforms Congress passed in the One, Big, Beautiful Bill, giving states and federal officials the tools to stop fraud, claw back improper payments, and hold bad actors accountable. FGA applauds the Energy and Commerce Committee for leading the charge.”
Kevin Garcia-Gallindo, Policy Associate, The LIBRE Initiative:
“Latinos have a vested interest in ensuring Medicaid remains strong for those who truly need it. As the youngest major racial or ethnic demographic in the country, young Latinos are more likely to help finance the program today than rely on it, giving them a significant stake in its long-term sustainability. Yet Medicaid’s greatest vulnerability is that too many safety-net dollars never reach the families the program was designed to serve. Instead, billions are lost to fraud, administrative errors, and weak oversight. When taxpayer dollars are wasted, the people who suffer most are the patients who depend on Medicaid for care. Every state should use the strongest tools available to detect, prevent, and recover improper payments. Doing so not only protects taxpayers, but also helps ensure that more resources are directed to vulnerable families and preserves access to affordable, high-quality health care for those who need it most.”
Bill Rouse, President, The Transportation Alliance, in support of the 
Strengthening Tools for Oversight to Prevent (STOP) Medicaid Fraud Act
:
“Congressman Evans’ legislation is an important step toward bringing greater accountability to NEMT. Requiring electronic verification that a Medicaid-funded trip actually occurred will help identify fraud, stop payments for services never provided, and protect responsible operators from having to compete against bad actors gaming the system. That means more resources for the patients who depend on NEMT and greater accountability for the taxpayers who fund it. We thank Congressman Evans and the House Energy and Commerce Committee for advancing a common-sense reform that strengthens NEMT for patients, providers, and taxpayers alike.”
“We welcome initiatives that strengthen state–federal collaboration to safeguard the Medicaid program and appreciate Congressman Balderson’s leadership on the SMART Act. Expanding access to interoperable analytic tools would meaningfully support Ohio Medicaid’s oversight and transparency efforts and help further prevent fraud, waste, and abuse.”

E&C Republicans Introduce Critical Fraud-Fighting Package to Protect Vulnerable Americans and Safeguard Taxpayer Dollars
WASHINGTON, D.C.
 – Congressman Brett Guthrie (KY-02), Chairman of the House Committee on Energy and Commerce, released one of the most critical health care packages of the 119th Congress that puts American patients first.
Energy and Commerce Republicans’ agenda “Continuing the Fight Against Fraud” includes 14 bills to streamline necessary data collection and availability, bolster anti-fraud enforcement infrastructure, and incentivize fraud-fighting efforts so that states and the federal government can more effectively defend taxpayer dollars and safeguard important federally funded health programs.
Members issued the following statements of support for the package and bills:
Attributable to Congressman Brett Guthrie (KY-02), Chairman of the House Committee on Energy and Commerce:
“The amount of fraud that the Biden-Harris Administration allowed to go unchecked in government health programs is unacceptable. Every dollar stolen by fraudsters or wasted on improper payments is a dollar taken away from supporting families in need who rely on these programs. While states are on the front lines of confronting this fraud, we must take commonsense steps to ensure that they are able to recoup their costs, providing incentives to do the vital work that is needed, including needed data sharing and new technologies that support enforcement, to ensure the program can continue to work for the Americans it was designed to serve.
“By using enhanced data analytics to find and eliminate fraud, incentivizing states to crack down on fraud more diligently, and fortifying the integrity of critical health programs, Republicans are leading the charge to restore the long-term vitality of federally funded health systems. Our multi-faceted program integrity package will help to combat the fraud that has plagued our system for far too long, I’m grateful for the consistent focus of our bill sponsors and their tireless effort to protect these programs, and we look forward to continuing the fight to ensure Americans have access to the care they need.”

BACKGROUND:
Medicaid remains vulnerable to waste, fraud, abuse, and improper payments going undetected.
These legislative solutions would strengthen program integrity by providing states with better resources to detect and prevent improper payments, increasing incentives to recover fraud, requiring states to identify and address vulnerabilities, and establishing stronger—and clearer—accountability for program oversight.
** Better Data: ** Policies that incorporate enhanced data and technology in state programs to better identify fraud, waste, abuse, and improper payments
Strengthening Medicaid Analytics to Reduce Theft (SMART) Act
 – Rep. Balderson (OH-12):
 States often must maintain their own program-integrity capabilities, while fraud schemes and high-risk providers can operate across state lines. The bill requires HHS to make standardized data-analytics technology available to every state to identify and prevent Medicaid fraud, waste, and abuse.
Medicaid Integrity Improvement Act – Rep. Joyce (PA-13)
:
 High-risk providers can present significant exposure to Medicaid before problems are identified through traditional enforcement activities. The bill requires MFCUs to conduct annual audits to identify improper payments and fraud among providers with elevated risk indicators.
Public Access to HHS Health Data Act –
 Rep. Bentz (OR-02):
 Important federal data can be difficult for states, lawmakers, and the public to locate, access, and use consistently. The bill requires HHS to create and maintain a centralized, publicly accessible repository of HHS datasets for oversight, research, and program-integrity activities.
Strengthening Tools for Oversight to Prevent (STOP) Medicaid Fraud Act
 – Rep. Evans (CO-08):
 NEMT and ABA services can present vulnerabilities when Medicaid lacks reliable electronic confirmation that a service was actually provided. Expanding EVV to these services would extend an existing program-integrity tool to additional areas where verification can help prevent improper payments and identify gaps in care.
** Stronger Enforcement: ** Policies that equip states with stronger enforcement tools and incentives to identify and recover improper spending
Helping Encourage Lawful Payments and Strengthen Transparency, Anti-fraud, Tips, and Enforcement Strategies (HELP STATES) Act –
 Rep. Kean (NJ-07):
 States bear significant costs when investigating and recovering fraudulent Medicaid payments but generally must return the federal share of recovered funds. The bill allows states to retain up to 25% of the federal share of Medicaid overpayment recoveries attributable to fraud, provided the retained funds are reinvested in Medicaid-program integrity activities such as audits, investigations, data analytics, and information technology.
Medicaid Fraud Fighting and Taxpayer Protection Act
 – Rep. Griffith (VA-09):
 False Claims Act cases require substantial investigative and legal resources from states. The bill increases the additional share of federal Medicaid recoveries available to states with qualifying False Claims Act cases from 10 percentage points to 25 percentage points, which would provide a stronger incentive to investigate and recover funds.
Medicaid Whistleblower Protection Act
 - Rep. Miller-Meeks (IA-01):
 State employees and contractors may have information about misconduct, so stronger protections are necessary to reduce the risk of retaliation and encourage those with credible information to report problems. The bill requires states to establish whistleblower protections for individuals who report Medicaid fraud, waste, or abuse.
WALZ Act
 – Rep. Miller-Meeks (IA-01):
 The bill requires HHS OIG to review whether a 10 percent increase in Medicaid spending may be attributable to fraud, waste, or abuse.
STOP FRAUD in Medicaid Act
 – Rep. Schmidt (KS-02):
 The bill allows MFCUs to investigate and prosecute fraudulent applications for and receipt of Medicaid benefits.
** More Accountability: ** Policies that hold states accountable by mandating they identify vulnerabilities, establish clear responsibility, and take corrective action
Medicaid Integrity and Fiscal Accountability Act
 – Rep. Pfluger (TX-11):
 Responsibility for Medicaid financial integrity can be dispersed among multiple state agencies or offices. The bill requires each state to designate an individual responsible for key fiscal responsibilities, including claims processing, payment integrity, financial reporting, and internal financial controls.
Medicaid Fraud Prevention Act
 – Rep. Rulli (OH-06):
 States may identify individual program-integrity problems without having a standardized process for assessing and prioritizing their broader fraud risks. The bill requires states to conduct comprehensive annual Medicaid fraud risk assessments, implement corrective action plans addressing identified vulnerabilities, and report to HHS and Congress.
Medicaid RAC Improvement Act
 – Rep. Bilirakis (FL-12):
 This bill strengthens Medicaid program integrity by tightening federal oversight of state Recovery Audit Contractor (RAC) programs, requiring more detailed reporting on audits, overpayments, and recoveries, and expanding RAC requirements to Medicaid managed care payments. It phases out state exemptions, standardizes audit and reporting practices across states, and requires CMS to regularly report to Congress on program effectiveness.
Home and Community Based Services Anti-Fraud Reporting Act
 – Rep. Latta (OH-05):
 The bill requires each state to submit an annual report to the Secretary identifying fraud, waste, and abuse involving services provided under 1915(c) HCBS waivers and detailing the steps taken to prevent and address such improper payments.
Medicaid Provider Screening Accountability Act
 – Rep. Langworthy (NY-23):
 The bill requires state Medicaid programs to check, during provider enrollment and reenrollment, whether providers have been terminated from Medicare, another state Medicaid program, or CHIP using designated databases, such as the Data Exchange system. States must continue checking these databases at least monthly after providers are enrolled.
