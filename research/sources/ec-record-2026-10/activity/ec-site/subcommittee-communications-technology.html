Pallone & Guthrie Introduce Legislation to Improve the Process for Adding Tech and Telecom Equipment to the FCC’s Covered List

WASHINGTON, D.C. – House Committee on Energy and Commerce Ranking Member Frank Pallone, Jr. (NJ-06) and Chairman Brett Guthrie (KY-02) have introduced new bipartisan legislation to update and improve the Secure and Trusted Communications Networks Act of 2019.

Their bill – the Communications and Technology Transparency Act – expands the types of malicious technology and equipment from foreign adversaries that can be added to the Federal Communications Commission’s (FCC) Covered List, streamlines the process for barring these products and services from coming into the United States, and clarifies the method for determining what is considered malicious technology or equipment. These changes improve consistency and transparency around the FCC Covered List, ensuring that it targets foreign adversaries and is used as Congress intended to safeguard our national security.

“The current process for identifying and barring malicious foreign technology has worked in many cases, but lacks the transparency, consistency, and clarity needed to meet our long-term national security needs as technology rapidly advances. Having learned important lessons since the Secure and Trusted Communications Networks Act was signed into law, it’s time for Congress to address these discrepancies and ensure there are no gaps in how we deal with our foreign adversaries or the telecom equipment they develop and control,” said Chairman Guthrie and Ranking Member Pallone. “Every day our adversaries are relentless in their attempts to breach our communications networks, and every day those efforts become increasingly sophisticated. This bill meets that challenge, builds on our past efforts, and creates an enduring and transparent process for safeguarding and securing America’s communications networks.”

The bipartisan Communications and Technology Transparency Act:

  • Stipulates that technology or equipment which has been deemed malicious by Congress or the Department of Commerce, in consultation with our national security agencies, may be added to the FCC’s Covered List;
  • Clarifies that equipment manufactured from one of four adversarial nations – China, Russia, North Korea, and Iran – is eligible for inclusion on the FCC’s Covered List;
  • Ensures that entities on the FCC’s Covered List and their affiliate(s) are barred from receiving an FCC license, authorization, or any other grant of authority issued by the FCC; and
  • Enhances congressional oversight of certain additions and removals to the Covered List.

Chairman Hudson Delivers Opening Statement at Subcommittee on Communications and Technology Markup of Six Bills to Strengthen Communications Networks and Modernize Satellite Regulations

WASHINGTON, D.C. – Congressman Richard Hudson (NC-09), Chairman of the Subcommittee on Communications and Technology, delivered the following opening statement at today’s markup of six bills to strengthen communications networks and modernize satellite regulations.

Subcommittee Chairman Hudson's opening statement as prepared for delivery:

“Good afternoon, and welcome to today’s subcommittee markup of six bipartisan telecommunications bills. These important pieces of legislation will bolster American leadership in communications, secure our networks from threats, and improve how the federal government gets broadband to those who still need it. I am pleased to see the subcommittee move forward with these bills.

“Three of the bills we are considering deal with threats to our networks, many of which originate abroad. The GUARD Act addresses robotic technology developed by our adversaries that has the potential to collect data and spy on Americans and whose operations can be manipulated by these adversaries.

“Removing this threat, especially before this technology becomes more widespread, is critical to our national and economic security.

“The Foreign Robocalls Elimination Act, as its name suggests, seeks to protect our networks from foreign robocalls.

“According to the Federal Trade Commission, the majority of the robocalls that annoy and defraud Americans originate abroad. This bill would create a task force to make recommendations on how to combat those calls, make important reforms to improve the traceback work—which identifies the origins of robocalls, and make it harder for untrusted carriers to bring traffic onto our networks. These are critical steps to ensuring these calls do not reach Americans. This bill strikes an important balance between taking action to reduce unwanted and illegal robocalls without adding unnecessary burdens on legitimate businesses trying to reach consumers.

“Next is the AI for Secure Networks Act. This bill would direct a study on the impact of artificial intelligence on the security of communications networks. Although we know AI can pose a threat to our networks, we can also leverage it to protect our networks. Understanding how we can do this will ensure efficient, resilient, and secure networks moving forward.

“We are also marking up two bills to address broadband deployment.

“The PLAN for Broadband Act directs the NTIA to develop a government-wide strategy for closing the digital divide. This bill implements a recommendation from a Government Accountability Office report that found the federal government has over 130 broadband programs spread across 15 agencies. A strategy will ensure these programs are better coordinated so we can efficiently close the digital divide. In addition, the Data Bridge Act would direct the FCC to include agricultural areas on its National Broadband Maps. This would ensure we know which agricultural areas still lack service and can better target resources to those areas.

“I want to thank my colleagues for their continued commitment to these issues and for their work developing these proposals. I look forward to moving these bills through committee and onto the floor.”

Press Release

C&T Subcommittee Advances Six Bills to Strengthen Communications Networks and Modernize Satellite Regulations

WASHINGTON, D.C. – Today, Congressman Richard Hudson (NC-09), Chairman of the Subcommittee on Communications and Technology, led a markup of six bills to strengthen communications networks and modernize satellite regulations.

“These important pieces of legislation will bolster American leadership in communications, secure our networks from threats, and improve how the federal government gets broadband to those who still need it,” said Chairman Hudson. “I want to thank my colleagues for their continued commitment to these issues and for their work developing these proposals.”

Legislative Vote Summary:

  • H.R. 8255, the Satellite and Telecommunications (SAT) Streamlining Act, was forwarded to the Full Committee, by voice vote.

  • H.R. 9129, the GUARD Act, was forwarded to the Full Committee, by voice vote.

  • H.R. 6152, the Foreign Robocall Elimination Act, was forwarded to the Full Committee, by voice vote.

  • H.R. 7294, the AI for Secure Networks Act, was forwarded to the Full Committee, by voice vote.

  • H.R. 2805, the PLAN for Broadband Act, was forwarded to the Full Committee, by voice vote.

  • H.R. 4950, the Data BRIDGE Act, was forwarded to the Full Committee, by voice vote.

Watch the full markup here.

Below are key excerpts from today's markup:

Congressman Buddy Carter (GA-01): “The federal government has numerous programs working to expand broadband access, but these efforts need to be coordinated effectively if we want to close the digital divide. This bipartisan bill directs NTIA to develop a national strategy to close the digital divide and improve the management and coordination of federal broadband programs and deployment efforts. Connecting every American requires more than simply spending money — we need a clear strategy to ensure federal programs are working together and resources are being deployed effectively.”

Congressman August Pfluger (TX-11): “Recent reporting revealed that millions of attacks were launched against U.S. telecommunications providers in a single year — a double-digit percentage spike that cost billions of dollars in losses worldwide and put Americans' privacy and security at risk. [...] That's why I'm proud to co-lead H.R. 7294, the AI for Secure Networks Act to harness AI to strengthen real-time threat detection, boost resiliency and interoperability, and make our next-generation networks more secure and efficient.”

Congresswoman Erin Houchin (IN-09): “The FCC's National Broadband Map helps determine where federal broadband dollars are invested. That map is currently built on homes and businesses. It says nothing about farmland, so the acres where the farming actually happens go unaccounted for. A farmhouse can register as served, while the fields around it have no usable signal. But the equipment that runs a modern farm — the guidance system, soil sensors, yield monitors, irrigation controls — needs a network out in the field, not the front porch.

“Our bill fixes that without collecting a single new data point. [...] The Data BRIDGE Act is a low-cost, commonsense fix, using data the government already has to make every broadband dollar end up closer to where the work gets done.”
