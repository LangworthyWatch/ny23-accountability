Commerce, Manufacturing, and Trade
Subcommittee
Subcommittee
 on 
Commerce, Manufacturing, and Trade
Interstate and foreign commerce, including all trade matters within the jurisdiction of the full committee; consumer protection, including privacy matters generally; data security; motor vehicle safety; regulation of commercial practices (the Federal Trade Commission), including sports-related matters; consumer product safety (the Consumer Product Safety Commission); product liability; and regulation of travel, tourism, and time. The Subcommittee’s jurisdiction can be directly traced to Congress’ constitutional authority “to regulate Commerce with foreign nations, and among the several States, and with the Indian Tribes.”

Chairman Guthrie Applauds Trump Administration Efforts to Reduce Vehicle Costs and End Biden-era EV Mandates

Press Release
Chairman Bilirakis Delivers Opening Statement at Subcommittee on Commerce, Manufacturing, and Trade Markup of 12 Bills to Strengthen American Competitiveness and Enhance Consumer Protections
WASHINGTON, D.C.
 – Congressman Gus Bilirakis (FL-12), Chairman of the Subcommittee on Commerce, Manufacturing, and Trade, delivered the following opening statement at today’s markup of 12 bills to strengthen American competitiveness and enhance consumer protections.
Subcommittee Chairman Bilirakis' opening statement as prepared for delivery:
“Good morning and thank you all for being here. Today’s markup brings together a dozen bills that reflect two of this Committee’s priorities: protecting American consumers and strengthening America’s competitiveness.
“I am proud of the bipartisan work that has gone into these important initiatives. American consumers deserve better protections in the marketplace, and these bills go a long way towards earning back consumers’ trust.
“The Strategic Task Force on Scam Prevention Act is necessary to ensure the relevant federal agencies coordinate their efforts to identify emerging scams and develop strategies to prevent Americans from becoming victims in the first place. The MAIN Event Ticketing Act builds on our prior work by cracking down on the bots and bad actors that have made buying a concert ticket feel like a rigged game. And when it comes to what’s actually in the products families buy and what claims companies make about them, the PACK Act and the Recycled Materials Attribution Act bring much needed transparency to recycling claims.
“In addition to advancing proposals that protect consumers, we are also pushing back against the Chinese Communist Party. The Chip EQUIP Act ensures that companies receiving CHIPS Act funding aren’t turning around and buying manufactured equipment from firms owned or controlled by the Chinese government.
“The Memory Chip Competitiveness Assessment Act and the American Quantum Competitiveness Act direct the Commerce Department to get serious about where our supply chains are vulnerable and how we out compete China in the technologies that matter most. Finally, the Automotive National and Economic Security Act shines a light on how deeply our auto industry’s relationships with foreign adversaries go.
“I look forward to moving these bills to the full committee and then to the House floor. Thank you to all my colleagues on both sides of the aisle who have worked diligently to protect consumers and bolster American competitiveness.”

Below are key excerpts from today’s markup:
Congressman Tom Kean (NJ-07):
“In my home state of New Jersey, I have heard from constituents who spoke firsthand about the dangers of this product found at gas stations and convenience stores across the country. This product — nitrous oxide canisters, also known as ‘whippets’ — contains nitrous oxide, also known as laughing gas. [...] In recent years, however, there’s been a sharp increase in misuse of nitrous oxide as an inhalant that gives users a high. Its abuse can lead to heart attacks, nerve damage, muscle spasms, numbness, birth defects, paralysis, and even death. [...] Today, I’m announcing my co-sponsorship of the Nitrous Oxide Safety Act, because I believe it’s important to address this issue and to ensure that nitrous oxide isn’t available to unsuspecting consumers.”
Congressman Gabe Evans (CO-08):
“The underlying bipartisan bill takes a nuanced approach to strengthening consumer protections by establishing a nationally consistent framework for recycled content and recycled content marketing claims — at a time when we’re seeing a patchwork of different definitions and frameworks around the country, which causes consumer confusion and erects barriers to increasing recycling, with potentially 50 different standards that need to be met.”
Congressman Craig Goldman (TX-12):
“American businesses shouldn’t have to comply with burdensome foreign regulations that hurt our competitiveness, especially when they aren’t even subject to the foreign law in the first place. The PROTECT USA Act protects entities important to U.S. national interests from being forced to comply with foreign sustainability rules, like the EU’s Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive.
“Here’s what that looks like on the ground in my district. The gas station owner in Fort Worth with just a handful of employees isn’t a multinational corporation doing business in Europe. He’ll never file a compliance report in Brussels. But if he’s partnered with a major American fuel brand that does business in Europe, his franchise contract can get rewritten to satisfy EU mandates — with new questionnaires, new supplier codes, and new audit reporting requirements, all from a regulation he had no say in, passed by a government he doesn’t vote for. That’s a small businessman in my district spending time and money to comply with EU overreach because of who he partners with to sell gas to local families, truck drivers, and other small businesses.”
