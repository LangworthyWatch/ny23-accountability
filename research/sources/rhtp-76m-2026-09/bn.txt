 Many health care analysts say the five-year, $50 billion Rural Health Transformation Program will not be enough to offset the upcoming reduction in federal Medicaid spending nationwide. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 In the first funding year, the U.S. Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services awarded $212 million from the rural health fund to New York – the 12th most of all states. New York has estimated the cost of its plan at $200 million per year, meaning around $1 billion in federal funds could come to New York over five years if the state lands similar annual awards in the years ahead. New York’s application it submitted to CMS in November focused on four key areas: establishing rural health partnerships to boost care coordination and patient access; strengthening rural communities with access to technology-enhanced primary care; building a sustainable rural healthcare workforce; and investing in technology innovation and cybersecurity improvements. 
 
 What does this aim to accomplish? The initial round, which the state Health Department is calling the Rural Community Health Integration initiative, is focused on establishing rural health partnerships that bolster care coordination and patient access.
