# H.R. 7757 (KIDS Act), House-passed text: preemption and loophole scan (2026-10-18)

Source: bill-text/BILLS-119hr7757eh.txt (GPO engrossed, passed House June 29, 2026). Committee version: amendments/BILLS-119-HR7757-A000370-Amdt-2.txt (Guthrie substitute GUTHRI_008, adopted Mar 5, 2026; Langworthy Yea on final 28-24).

## Preemption
- Committee version: six express preemption sections, e.g. Sec. 106(a): "No State, or political subdivision of a State, may prescribe, maintain, enforce, or continue in effect any law ... to the extent that such law ... requires a provider of a covered platform to use technology verification measures ..." (similar clauses at Amdt-2 lines ~1095, 1390, 1465, 1630, 1771). Democrats' amendments (Castor, Landsman, Soto, Matsui, Mullin) would have replaced these with "greater protection" savings clauses; Langworthy voted Nay on each.
- House-passed version: Sec. 704(a) preempts state law "only to the extent that such law ... conflicts with a provision of this Act"; 704(b)(2) saves state laws that provide "greater protection to minors"; 704(b)(1) saves contract, tort, product liability. 704(c) rewrites COPPA's preemption clause the same way. No other preemption clause found in the passed text.

## Loopholes / limits in the passed text
1. Minor = under 17 (Sec. 2(8)); teen = 13-16 (Sec. 212(8)). 17-year-olds are not covered.
2. Covered platform must meet ALL five criteria (Sec. 201(1)(A)-(E)), incl. user-generated content as "primary purpose," searchable/followable usernames, a design feature, AND use of personal information for ads or recommendations.
3. Harms list (Sec. 213(a)) is four items: threats of violence impacting a major life activity; sexual exploitation; drugs/tobacco/cannabis/gambling/alcohol; financial harm from deceptive practices. It does not list anxiety, depression, eating disorders, self-harm or suicide. Policies need only be "reasonable" and "appropriate to the size and complexity" of the platform (213(b)).
4. Sec. 213(c)(2): nothing "may be construed to ... impose a duty of care on a provider."
5. Safeguards (214(a)) are options a known minor can use; defaults must be most protective (214(a)(3)), but nothing in 214 bars a teen from changing them.
6. Parents of teens (13-16) can only VIEW privacy/account settings (214(b)(2)(A)); only parents of children under 13 can change them (214(b)(2)(B)) or disable messaging (214(b)(2)(F)). Time limits may not "amount to full exclusion of access" (214(b)(2)(D)).
7. Sec. 214(b)(6): existing accounts where a parent previously opted out of tools need not have tools enabled.
8. "Compulsive usage" (212(2)) = use that "substantially limits 1 or more major life activities" (ADA standard), a high bar for the design-feature safeguard (214(a)(1)(D)).
9. Sec. 220: no age gating or age verification required. Combined with knowledge triggers ("knows," defined in Subtitle A as "know or should have known," 212(6)), duties attach only to minors the platform knows or should have known about.
10. Enforcement (Sec. 701): FTC (as an FTC Act rule violation) and state attorneys general (parens patriae). No private right of action found (grep "private right"/"private cause": 0 hits). State AGs may not sue a defendant on the same violations while a federal action is pending (701(b)(3)(B)).
11. Sec. 703(5): does not "expand, limit the scope of, or alter the meaning of section 230."
12. Timing: Act effective 1 year after enactment (706); audits begin 18 months after enactment (219(a)).
13. Title V is largely studies/reports (e.g., Secs. 512, 514, 516-519); Paperwork Reduction Act waived for Subtitle A (502).
