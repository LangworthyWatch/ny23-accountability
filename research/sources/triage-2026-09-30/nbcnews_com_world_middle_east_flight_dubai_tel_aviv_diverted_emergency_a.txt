The intervention of passengers and crew prevented the plane from crashing, Israeli officials say.Listen to this article with a free profile00:0000:00Add to GoogleNetanyahu says pilot stabbed fellow pilot in apparent attempt to crash plane06:03Get more newsLiveonShareAdd NBC News to GoogleSept. 30, 2026, 4:56 AM EDT / Updated Sept. 30, 2026, 5:17 PM EDTBy Matt Bradley, Yarden Segev, Chantal Da Silva, Raf Sanchez and Nicole AcevedoTEL AVIV — A pilot stabbed his co-pilot aboard a plane headed to Israel on Wednesday and may have tried to crash the aircraft with 174 people onboard, Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said.

Passengers lived through scenes of terror as the Flydubai flight approaching Tel Aviv shook violently and abruptly plummeted. The flight had originated in Dubai.

“I thought it was a plane malfunction, but then I started hearing screaming about the stabbings,” Aviatar Alevi, a 17-year-old student, told NBC News. “Then I understood the weight of the actual situation.”

Alevi said he thought he was going to die as he felt the plane rapidly descend, with “screaming, people running, panicking, blood.”

Another passenger, Motti Cohen Or, 70, told NBC News that he heard a woman shouting that “someone is stabbing someone in the cockpit” before the aircraft dived “toward the ground.”

Flight data indicates the Boeing 737, flight number FZ 1073, plunged by 14,125 feet in just 29 seconds, according to Flightradar24.

All Flydubai flights to and from Israel have been temporarily suspended as authorities continue their investigation into what occurred on flight FZ 1073, a spokesperson for the airline said in a statement.

“This temporary suspension will allow the relevant authorities to continue their work and establish all the facts surrounding the incident,” the spokesperson said in the statement.

The midair incident led to a diversion and an emergency landing at Saudi Arabia’s Prince Sultan bin Abdulaziz International Airport, known as Tabuk Airport. A rescue flight later brought passengers to Israel’s Ben Gurion Airport.

At Ben Gurion, Netanyahu personally thanked a passenger still wearing a bloodied T-shirt, who told cameras he was among those to enter the cockpit and subdue the attacking pilot. Netanyahu’s office named the man as Yaniv Hayun.

Hayun told reporters at Ben Gurion that he and three other passengers helped in the effort to save the plane and those aboard.

“Thank God, we pulled it off. We really put our heads together and worked as a team,” Hayun said.

The group struggled to open the cockpit door.

“We managed to get it open, but what made it difficult for us was the pilot himself— he had been stabbed and was on the other side of the door,” he said.

“It was between fear and a sense of a do-or-die situation,” Hayun added. “We had to win, we had to pull through and come out on top.”

Israeli officials also praised the stabbed pilot, identified as Indian national Captain Smit Machchhar, for his actions in opening the cockpit door, allowing passengers and crew to rush in, secure the cockpit and prevent the plane from crashing.

“He saved the lives of 174 people, including Israeli citizens and other nationals,” Netanyahu said in an X post. “I wish Captain Machchhar a speedy and full recovery. He is a true hero.”

Video from onboard appeared to show two people lying on the floor, visibly bloodied as others crowd around them.

Machchhar is in stable condition in a hospital in Saudi Arabia, according to the Indian embassy in Riyadh.

Four U.S. citizens, who are dual nationals, were on the flight, per two U.S. officials.

Passengers from the earlier diverted Flydubai flight arrive at Israel's Ben Gurion Airport on Wednesday.Ariel Schalit / APPrime minister’s spokesperson Doron Spielman said that “there is a strong opinion” that the incident was a terror attack, “but we can’t be sure until we debrief everybody.”

No country or terror organization has claimed the incident.

The pilot who committed the stabbing, who is in custody in Saudi Arabia, is believed to be a citizen of Oman, an Israeli official tells NBC News.

The Clippers still believe they can win.Two senior U.S. law enforcement officials briefed on the investigation also told NBC News that investigators say the pilot who began stabbing the co-pilot is from Oman.

The officials did not identify the name of that pilot.

NBC News has contacted the Omani embassy in the U.S. for comment and did not receive an immediate response.

An investigation by Saudi authorities is ongoing, David Mencer, a spokesperson for the Israeli Prime Minister’s Office, said.

The stabbing increasingly looks like an act of terrorism based on the early results from the investigation, according to a senior U.S. official briefed on the Israeli investigation and a Saudi official briefed on the investigation.

The Saudi official cautioned that the investigation is still in its early stages and the next 24 hours should yield more concrete information.

United Arab Emirates’ Deputy Prime Minister and Minister of Foreign Affairs Sheikh Abdullah bin Zayed Al Nahyan and Israel’s minister of foreign affairs, Gideon Sa’ar, spoke on the phone on Wednesday afternoon.

According to the UAE Foreign Ministry, Al Nahyan asked about the safety of passengers on the Flydubai flight which had “experienced a security incident that was successfully brought under control” and wished “good health and well-being” for the passengers.

There are no indications that Iran directed or was involved in today’s attack, a senior U.S. official briefed on the Israeli investigation told NBC News.

The preliminary Israeli investigation strongly suggests that the incident was an attempted terrorist attack by the Omani pilot who stabbed his co-pilot, the official said.

The aircraft was “successfully secured” and landed by on-duty crew traveling onboard, Flydubai said.

“At this early stage, the underlying reasons and motives behind this event are unknown and remain subject to a formal investigation,” the airline said, adding: “We urge all parties to refrain from premature speculation while authorities gather the facts.”

Israeli Defense Minister Israel Katz meanwhile told Israeli media the incident “was a jihadist terrorist attack attempt,” without saying how he reached that conclusion.

A still image taken from video verified by NBC News shows a chaotic scene onboard the Flydubai plane as a man covered in blood receives treatment.via TelegramAfter hours on the ground in Saudi Arabia, the diverted passengers were brought back to Ben Gurion Airport, where they were greeted by well-wishers waving Israeli flags and reunited with family members.

Some of those returning waved to the cameras and discussed their experiences, while exhausted parents carried their young children through the terminal.

Peleg Itali, 17, said there was a family with small children near him on the flight.

“The mother doesn’t even know what to tell them,” he said. “No one knows everything, because no one knew what was going on.”

At least one person, a passenger in his 50s, was taken to a the hospital with a “chest injury,” said Aryeh Myers, a paramedic with Magen David Adom, Israel’s national emergency agency. Myers described the person’s health condition as “mild.”

Passengers from the earlier diverted Flydubai flight arrive in Israel.Jack Guez / AFP via Getty ImagesEarlier, U.S. Ambassador to Israel Mike Huckabee expressed relief in a post on X that the incident didn’t turn out to be “more serious.”

“This had everyone in the Middle East (esp Israel) on FULL ALERT this morning,” he said. “Having pilots inflight detouring to ‘fist city’ not your ordinary experience.”

Department of Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin told reporters that domestically, the TSA will continue to be “at high alert,” adding: “We have no credible threats to us right now that would coordinate what took place over there.”

The incident comes at a time of high tensions in Israel ahead of next month’s parliamentary election, a key test for Netanyahu as he faces discontent over Gaza and Iran. The leader has made national security a core part of his platform ahead of the vote on October 27, while also facing pressure from hardliners who favor a more aggressive stance in the West Bank.

Matt Bradley and Yarden Segev reported from Tel Aviv. Chantal Da Silva and Raf Sanchez reported from London. Nicole Acevedo reported from New York.
