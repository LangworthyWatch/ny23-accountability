## Post 1 (~Sept 30, 11 AM): "My bipartisan Medicaid Provider Screening Accountability Act mentioned in this story..." (Fox News link)
- **H.R. 1875**, sponsor Langworthy, introduced Mar 5, 2025, co-lead Morelle; **3 cosponsors (1 R, 2 D)**; referred to E&C Mar 5, 2025;
  **no action since** (`BILLSTATUS-119hr1875.xml`, last updated May 30, 2025). Requires states to check the Data Exchange System
  so a provider terminated in one state's Medicaid cannot re-enroll in another.
- The Fox story (Sept 29, retained) covers the E&C Republican staff report (Sept 29, 87 pp, retained) and a 14-bill package;
  his bill is in the package and Fox names it: "legislation by Rep. Nick Langworthy, R-N.Y., would require states to check
  whether someone enrolling for Medicaid was removed from another taxpayer-funded healthcare program." "Mentioned in this story" is accurate.
- **The $521 billion in the Fox headline is not a health-care figure.** Fox's own text: "While no estimate for Medicare and
  Medicaid fraud exists specifically, the report noted, a 2024 estimate by the Government Accountability Office said $233 billion
  to $521 billion taxpayer dollars going to federal programs are lost to fraud every year." The E&C report itself: "$233 billion
  to $521 billion, approximately 3 percent to 7 percent of federal outlays, are lost" (government-wide, GAO-24-105833). His post
  does not repeat the number. Low priority; a roundup line at most ("the headline's $521B is all federal programs, not health care").
- Context already on the site: only 2.5% of the OBBBA's Medicaid cuts address fraud, waste and abuse (CCF, in
  `children-health-2026-09-25/`); the House is out until Nov 9, so the package cannot move before then.

## Post 2 (~Sept 30, 8 AM): "...stopped this attempted hijacking and terrorist attack... pass my Safer Skies Act to ensure our
   planes can never again be used as a weapon of terror." (NY Post link)
- **The incident** (NBC, Al Jazeera, NPR, CBC, Insurance Journal, all Sept 30, retained): Flydubai FZ1073, Dubai to Tel Aviv,
  Boeing 737 MAX 8, 174 aboard; one pilot stabbed the other in the cockpit and the aircraft dropped 14,125 feet in 29 seconds;
  the stabbed captain (Smit Machchhar) opened the cockpit door and passengers and crew subdued the attacker (an Omani national
  per reports), who is in Saudi custody after an emergency landing at Tabuk. Netanyahu: "apparently tried to crash the plane."
  Defense Minister Katz: "an attempted jihadist terrorist attack," per NPR, "without providing details." PM's spokesperson:
  "there is a strong opinion" it was terror "but we can't be sure until we debrief everybody." Flydubai: "the underlying reasons
  and motives behind this event are unknown and remain subject to a formal investigation"; UAE GCAA investigating.
- **His bill, H.R. 2353** (`BILLSTATUS-119hr2353.xml`, `BILLS-119hr2353ih.htm`): 52 cosponsors (26 R, 26 D); referred to the
  Homeland Security Committee's Transportation and Maritime Security Subcommittee Mar 26, 2025; **no action in 18 months.**
  Text: makes "covered air carrier operations" subject to TSA's Aircraft Operator Standard Security Program, defined as Part 135/380
  operators that sell individual seats on published schedules, fly aircraft with more than nine seats, and "do not enplane or
  deplane in a checkpoint managed by the Transportation Security Administration." It is a passenger-checkpoint bill for U.S.
  public charters (the JSX model). It says nothing about pilots, cockpit access, crew vetting, insider threats, or foreign carriers.
- **Gap:** a UAE airline's own pilot attacking his colleague at the controls is the "insider threat" (Baum to Al Jazeera: "the
  insider threat is probably the greatest threat to civil aviation nowadays"). Passenger screening at U.S. charter terminals would
  not have touched it. "Attempted hijacking and terrorist attack" adopts the Israeli government's early characterization while
  the airline and UAE regulator say the motive is unknown.
- **Candidate: MISLEADING** ("pass my Safer Skies Act to ensure our planes can never again be used as a weapon of terror" attached
  to an event his bill does not address). Short entry: post text; what happened; what H.R. 2353 does, in its own words; its
  status; the fair reading (the bill closes a real, separate gap; 52 cosponsors; he chairs the Aviation Safety Caucus).
