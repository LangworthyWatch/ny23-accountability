New Law Bans NYS Law Enforcement from Working with ICE

  • Aug 25, 2026
  • Aug 25, 2026 Updated Aug 25, 2026

STEUBEN COUNTY, NY (WENY) — New York Governor Kathy Hochul touted her ‘Local Cops, Local Crimes Act’ that went into effect on Tuesday. The law bans agreements between local police and the Department of Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE), operating under 287(g).

“We will not allow local cops to be diverted as ICE agents to conduct civil immigration enforcement,” said Gov. Hochul.

The Local Cops, Local Crimes Act went into effect on August 25th. Emails and letters were sent last month to a dozen law enforcement agencies throughout the state currently operating under the federal agreements, including the Steuben County Sheriff’s Office.

RELATED: Dozen NYS Law Enforcement Agencies Could End Agreements with ICE

Steuben County Sheriff Jim Allard said this will ultimately cost taxpayers more money. Under a 287(g) agreement, participating law enforcement agencies are eligible for reimbursement for each eligible trained officer, including annual salary and benefits, including overtime.

“The total effect of this is that it costs more for Steuben County to provide public safety because the reimbursement program is no longer there for when we do assist our federal counterparts when they come to Steuben County. All it’s done is put a bigger burden on the taxpayer, and it has not made anyone safer whatsoever in this county or region,” said Sheriff Allard.

According to Gov. Hochul, the law is meant to protect immigrant communities, strengthen public trust, and limit the use of state and local resources for federal, civil immigration enforcement.

“Local police will assist ICE only if a real crime has been committed. But on civil immigration matters, ICE is on their own… We need local cops hunting down criminals, not kindergarteners. Breaking up drug rings, not breaking up families. Arresting gun runners, not grandmas. With this law, we can ensure cops, police, and sheriffs can stay laser-focused on their job at hand,” said Gov. Hochul.

She added, “ICE is dangerous and uses aggressive tactics to pursue civil immigration; again I’m very clear here: for civil immigration enforcement, this only continues to invoke fear on our streets, and our local cops will not be part of it.”

Sheriff Allard feels this law is an overreach and that his department will continue to keep Steuben County safe whenever and however necessary.

“It’s not a be-all-and-end-all, but it is a great tool to have in the toolbox… I don’t see anything different other than we won’t have formal agreements anymore. We’ll still work with our federal counterparts. We’ll still support all of our federal agencies, whether it be FBI, DEA, or ICE. It doesn’t matter which three letters lead up to that enforcement agency. We will still work with them and collaborate with them,” said Sheriff Allard.

According to the Sheriff, they assist ICE with about two or three cases a quarter.

“It’s a great tool to have in the tool bag when we have someone here illegally committing crimes that we know they’re not just going to get arraigned, released, so that they can go out and commit more crimes or disappear from the system. This way, they know they’re held accountable and are no longer committing crimes in our region,” said the Sheriff.
