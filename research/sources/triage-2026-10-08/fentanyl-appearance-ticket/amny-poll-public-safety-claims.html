2026 Governor’s Race: Hochul leads Blakeman by 11 points in new poll as Dems take a bite out of his public safety claims

By Adam Daly
… Comments
Posted on October 7, 2026
Republican gubernatorial candidate Bruce Blakeman takes a bite of “The Blakeman,” a turkey sandwich named after him, during a campaign stop at Mario’s Meats & Gourmet Deli in Flushing, Queens, on Oct. 2, 2026
Photo by Lloyd Mitchell

Gov. Kathy Hochul holds an 11-point lead over Republican challenger Bruce Blakeman in a new statewide poll released Wednesday, as New York Democrats seek to turn one of the Nassau County executive’s signature campaign issues, public safety, against him.

The Marist poll found Hochul leading Blakeman 53% to 42% among registered voters, with 5% undecided. Blakeman led among independents, 49% to 42%, while the two candidates were tied at 48% among suburban voters.

Hochul held a commanding advantage in New York City, 69% to 24%, while Blakeman led Upstate, 51% to 43%.

The 11-point margin falls between two statewide polls released on Sept. 23. Siena had Hochul ahead 50% to 41% among likely voters, while Quinnipiac showed a wider 58% to 39% advantage. The surveys are not directly comparable, however, because Marist polled registered voters while Siena and Quinnipiac surveyed likely voters.

The new Marist poll was conducted Oct. 1 through Oct. 4 among 1,361 registered New York voters and has a margin of error of plus or minus 3.6 percentage points. Respondents were reached by phone, text and online.

Dems challenge Blakeman’s policing record

Against the positive polling backdrop for Hochul, her ally, New York State Democratic Party Chair Jay Jacobs, is accusing Blakeman of “defunding” the Nassau County Police Department, pointing to staffing vacancies and a proposed reduction in police equipment spending.

“Bruce Blakeman is defunding the Nassau PD while trying to sell New Yorkers on lies about his safety record,” Jacobs said in a statement shared with amNewYork. “Blakeman’s own county is losing police officers on his watch, and now he wants to slash funding for equipment that allows law enforcement to do their jobs.”

Some of the figures underlying Jacobs’ attack are reflected in Blakeman’s proposed 2027 county budget. The Police Department had 3,275 full-time employees on board as of Sept. 3, down 50 from the 3,325 employed at the end of 2025 and 183 below the 3,458 positions authorized for 2026.

County payroll data also show a longer-term decline in the department’s full-time workforce since Blakeman took office in 2022. Nassau’s Open Payroll database tracks Police Department employment over that period.

The proposed budget also reduces the Police Department’s equipment line from about $3.53 million in the county’s 2026 midyear projection to $2.66 million next year, a drop of roughly 25%.

Despite the equipment cut and staffing gaps, overall police spending would rise to about $1.24 billion in 2027, an increase of $40.9 million, or 3.4%, from the 2026 midyear projection. The proposed budget also funds 36 additional police officer positions and two recruiting classes intended to maintain staffing levels.

The sworn staffing numbers are also more nuanced than the overall department headcount. As of Sept. 3, Nassau had 2,554 members of its three sworn police unions on board, compared with 2,629 positions authorized for 2026 — a gap of 75, or about 3%.

Rank-and-file PBA staffing was at its full authorized level of 1,894. The shortfalls were among detectives, with 297 members of the Detectives Association on board compared with 360 authorized, and superior officers, with 363 on board compared with 375 authorized.

The staffing figures follow an early-retirement initiative that Blakeman promoted as a cost-saving measure. The New York Post reported earlier this year that 241 Nassau police officers had opted into the county’s voluntary separation incentive program, which offered eligible employees $2,000 for each year of service. The Post reported that roughly 100 recruits were expected to graduate from the police academy, with another class of about 150 expected to enter in May.

Blakeman told the Post at the time that the county would continue hiring to offset the departures.

“To make up for any loss, we will continue to hire new police officers so that we operate at full strength,” he told the outlet. 

Blakeman’s campaign did not respond to amNewYork’s request for comment. 

Jacobs also challenged Blakeman’s broader record on crime, saying violent crime in Nassau County had reached a 10-year high on his watch.

“While Governor Hochul has invested nearly $4 billion in public safety, Blakeman is all talk when it comes to making our communities safer,” said Jacobs. 

State data show violent index crime in Nassau did reach its highest annual level in a decade during Blakeman’s tenure, although the increase from 2022 to 2024 was slight.

State Division of Criminal Justice Services data show Nassau recorded 2,294 violent index crimes in 2024, the highest annual total from 2015 through 2024. The figure stood at 2,271 in 2022, the year Blakeman took office, meaning the 2024 total was about 1% higher.

The same data show the county’s overall index-crime total declined over that period, from 18,553 incidents in 2022 to 16,260 in 2024.

Public safety is central to Blakeman’s pitch for governor. His campaign website claims Nassau as the “#1 safest county in America” and credits him with hiring hundreds of new police and law-enforcement officers, arguing that he would bring the same approach statewide.
