 Hochul, Blakeman campaign in Erie County as governor’s race enters final month
 BY 
 Nái Rhoden
 Erie County
 PUBLISHED 7:53 PM ET Oct. 05, 2026
 PUBLISHED 7:53 PM EDT Oct. 05, 2026
 SHARE
 BUFFALO, N.Y. — With less than a month until Election Day, the race for New York governor ran through Western New York Monday as Democratic Gov. Kathy Hochul and Republican challenger Bruce Blakeman made separate appearances in Erie County and sharpened their arguments over affordability and the state’s economy.
Blakeman used his visit to criticize Hochul’s record on taxes, utility costs and the state’s business climate, arguing New York has become too expensive for families and businesses.
“Kathy Hochul wants to talk about anything but her record,” Blakeman said.
Blakeman said his administration would take a different approach to businesses and work to encourage more economic development in Western New York.
“I treat businesses as partners, not as enemies. Kathy Hochul treats them as the enemy,” Blakeman said.
Hours later, Hochul was also in Erie County, where she announced an expansion of the state’s free community college program.
Asked about Blakeman’s criticism of her record on affordability, Hochul defended her administration’s efforts to lower costs.
“I’ll put my record up to anybody’s any time,” Hochul said.
The governor pointed to middle-class tax cuts, rebates, childcare initiatives and free community college. She also sought to tie Blakeman to President Donald Trump, arguing the president’s tariff policies are increasing costs for New York businesses.
“It’s crushing New York businesses,” Hochul said. “I just left a meeting of business owners. They don’t know what to do.”
Blakeman’s Western New York visit also drew protesters from the Seneca Nation as the Nation and Hochul administration continue negotiations over a new gaming compact.
The Seneca Nation has said an agreement in principle has been negotiated that would not include a revenue-sharing provision. Hochul, however, said Monday that negotiations remain underway.
“Currently there is no agreement and negotiations are ongoing,” Hochul said.
Hochul said the state remains committed to helping Buffalo, Niagara Falls and Salamanca if the communities are financially affected by changes to the compact. She pointed to previous state assistance provided when Seneca gaming payments stopped.
The candidates’ separate Erie County appearances come as the gubernatorial campaign enters its final weeks, with both sides increasingly focused on the cost of living and the state’s economic direction.
Hochul and Blakeman are scheduled to meet face-to-face in a debate next week.
