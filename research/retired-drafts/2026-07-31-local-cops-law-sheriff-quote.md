## Why This Matters for NY-23

On July 24, 2026, the New York Attorney General's office sent letters to twelve law enforcement agencies telling them their agreements with ICE are void as of August 25.

**Three of those twelve are in NY-23:** the Cattaraugus County Sheriff's Office, the Steuben County Sheriff's Office, and the Allegany Village Police Department.

Rep. Langworthy shared a story about a fourth agency, the Madison County Sheriff's Office, which is not in NY-23.

That is not the finding of this entry, and a member of Congress is free to comment on any state law he likes. It is context for the one that follows: the quotation he chose to amplify from that story, and the two sentences from the same sheriff, in the same interview, that point the other way.

---

## A. The Post

Posted on or about **July 30, 2026**, over a WSYR-TV report, to **[facebook.com/ChairmanNickLangworthy](https://www.facebook.com/ChairmanNickLangworthy/)** — the campaign page, listed as the Facebook page for Nick Langworthy for Congress. This is a **different account** from [facebook.com/RepLangworthy](https://www.facebook.com/RepLangworthy/), the official congressional office page. Both are active; the distinction is noted because this site attributes posts to the account that carried them.

> "Allowing our local law enforcement officials to work with ICE to hand over dangerous illegals charged with a crime is the right thing to do & just plain common sense. 'A rape in the first degree. Imagine something like that happening to one of your family members. You would want the police working together, and there's the divide where they aren't allowing that to happen in New York State.'"

**Two things have to be separated here, and the site is going to be careful about it.**

The first sentence is Langworthy's own, and it is an **opinion**. Whether local police *should* hand people over to ICE is a policy view, not a checkable claim, and this entry assigns it no verdict.

The material in quotation marks is **not his**. It is Madison County Sheriff Todd Hood, quoted in the article. The WSYR-TV story attributes it plainly: *"…said Hood."* He reproduced it accurately and did not alter it.

**So the question this entry examines is narrow:** the quoted passage ends on a factual assertion about New York law — that the state is not "allowing that to happen" — and what the rest of that same interview says about it.

---

## B. What the Sheriff Said in the Rest of the Interview

The article is by Ashley Cafaro, WSYR-TV, posted **July 29, 2026, 7:10 PM EDT**, updated 11:09 PM. Hood is quoted four times. Here are the other three.

**Before the quoted passage**, in the article's fourth paragraph:

> "I'm still going to contact ICE when we have somebody come in here that's illegal and committed a crime. They are going to come get them, and we're going to hand them off, and they are going to go with them and get the issue that they have with ICE taken care of," said Hood.

**Three paragraphs after the quoted passage:**

> "They are doing this as a political stunt because it really doesn't change the way we're operating," said Hood.

**And:**

> "These are people that are in here committing crimes. They are not the ones that are in here working. We have a ton of farmland in Madison County, and we have a lot of people that are in here that are illegal, that are working and doing jobs and staying out of trouble. The ones that get into trouble, they come here," said Hood.

**In plain language:** the same sheriff, in the same interview, says both that New York "aren't allowing that to happen" *and* that he will still contact ICE, still hand people off, and that the law "really doesn't change the way we're operating." The headline of the article Langworthy shared says as much on its face: *"Madison County sheriff says he'll continue to work with ICE despite new state law."*

## C. What the Law Actually Does

This is from the Attorney General's July 24 letter itself, not from coverage of it.

The Local Cops, Local Crimes Act was enacted **May 27, 2026**, in the state Public Protection and General Government budget bill, and adds Executive Law § 170-k.

| What it does | Citation |
|---|---|
| Bars any local agency from entering, renewing, modifying, or **remaining in** a Section 287(g) agreement. Effective immediately on enactment | Exec. L. § 170-k(2)(a) |
| Deems existing 287(g) agreements "void and unenforceable" 90 days after enactment, i.e. **August 25, 2026** | Exec. L. § 170-k(7)(a) |
| Directs agencies to exercise any termination provision in the agreement | Exec. L. § 170-k(7) |
| Requires the twelve agencies to report their compliance status to the AG by **August 14, 2026** | AG letter, Jul. 24, 2026 |

**The law is a real change, and this entry is not going to pretend otherwise.** Hood's 287(g) agreement is genuinely being voided. He told WSYR-TV the agreement helped his deputies honor **seven detainer requests since September 2025**, in cases involving robbery, DWI, weapons offenses, and rape. Those are the stakes he is describing, and they are not imaginary.

**There is also a genuine unresolved legal question**, and it deserves to be stated rather than resolved. The AG's letter says local officials "may not engage in civil immigration enforcement **pursuant to a Section 287(g) agreement** by arresting an individual based on a civil ICE warrant or detainer request." Hood says he will keep notifying ICE and handing people off. Whether *notification and transfer outside a 287(g) agreement* survives the statute is a question the letter does not squarely answer, and this site does not have the answer either.

Supporters' framing, as the article reports it: the law "allows police agencies to focus on local crimes, including if an immigrant commits them, without wasting resources on federal civil immigration enforcement."

## A Note on What the Record Does — and Doesn't — Show

**Documented:** the WSYR-TV article of Jul. 29, 2026, and all four Hood quotations, verified verbatim against a full PDF capture of the page; the AG's letter of Jul. 24, 2026, and the twelve recipient agencies; the statutory citations and both effective dates; that three recipient agencies are in NY-23.

**Not documented, and not claimed:** that Langworthy altered or misquoted the sheriff — he did not; that he was obligated to quote the full interview; that he acted with any particular intent, which this site does not assert about anyone; that the Local Cops, Local Crimes Act is good or bad policy; that Sheriff Hood's reading of his own obligations is wrong, or that the Attorney General's is right. **Nor do we suggest the law changes nothing** — it voids a real agreement on a real date, and the sheriff's stated concerns about serious charges are quoted here in full rather than summarized away.

---

## Verdict: MISSING CONTEXT

The quotation is accurate and correctly reproduced. What is absent is the rest of the same interview, in which the same sheriff says he will still contact ICE, still hand people off, and that the law "really doesn't change the way we're operating."

A reader of the post comes away understanding that New York has stopped local police from turning over people charged with serious crimes. A reader of the article it links to comes away understanding that the Madison County sheriff says he will keep doing exactly that, without a contract.
