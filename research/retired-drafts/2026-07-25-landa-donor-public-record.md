## Why This Matters for NY-23

Rep. Langworthy voted to block the first federal nursing home staffing standard for a decade. One of the larger individual donors to his campaign committees is a man who owns nursing homes. We documented that sequence in [an earlier entry](/fact-checks/2026-05-28-nursing-home-staffing-donations/).

This entry answers a narrower question that entry did not: **who is the donor, and what does the public record say about how his facilities are run?**

The answer is not drawn from opposition research or anonymous sources. It is drawn from two ProPublica investigations, a Buffalo News investigation, two lawsuits filed by the New York State Attorney General, a federal Inspector General audit, and a federal court opinion. Every document is linked below and every one is publicly available.

**One limit, stated up front:** we found **no Benjamin Landa facility inside NY-23**. His Western New York exposure is in Erie County and Orleans County, and the Erie County homes are owned by his wife (see below). Nobody in Allegany, Cattaraugus, Chautauqua, Chemung, Schuyler, Steuben, or Tioga County lives in one of his buildings. The relevance here is not that his facilities harm this district's residents. It is that money from this operator helped fund this district's representative, who then voted on the federal rules governing this operator's industry.

---

## Who He Is

**Benjamin Landa**, of Lawrence, New York (Nassau County). Co-founder and chief executive of **SentosaCare LLC**, described by the Buffalo News in 2018 as New York's largest for-profit nursing home chain. His longtime business partner is Bent Philipson. In FEC filings, his employer is listed as "The Premier Network."

ProPublica's Nursing Home Inspect affiliate database currently associates **49 facilities** in Florida, Kentucky, New Jersey and New York with Landa. CMS ownership records used in our [earlier entry](/fact-checks/2026-05-28-nursing-home-staffing-donations/) identified 106 facilities across 10 states. **These two counts are not contradictory so much as differently scoped** — ProPublica's affiliate matching and the CMS ownership file use different thresholds for what counts as an ownership link. We cite both rather than picking the larger number.

In **October 2025, President Trump nominated Landa to be United States Ambassador to Hungary.** Two months before the nomination, in August 2025, Landa contributed **$5 million to MAGA Inc.**, a pro-Trump super PAC. New York State valued his net worth above $300 million in 2016. *Source: ProPublica, Mar. 23, 2026.*

## The Money to Langworthy

**$55,000** in directly traceable contributions from Benjamin and Judy Landa to Langworthy's two named committees across the 2022–2025 cycles. Every dollar arrived **before** the July 3, 2025 vote. The largest tranche, $25,000, arrived March 31, 2025 — three months before Roll Call 190.

The full itemized breakdown, and the method used to exclude FEC memo entries that would otherwise double-count the total, is in [Nursing Home Donations and the Staffing Rule That Disappeared](/fact-checks/2026-05-28-nursing-home-staffing-donations/).

## The Public Record

### A. Regulators approved his expansion despite a record of harm (ProPublica, 2015)

*This is the earliest major investigation and it concerns the regulatory system, not just the facilities.*

ProPublica reported in October 2015 that Landa, Philipson and family members "have been able to expand their nursing home ownerships in New York, easily clearing regulatory reviews meant to be a check on repeat offenders." The investigation found the state Department of Health **omitted at least 20 federal fines across 15 ownership applications**, and repeatedly reported "no repeat violations" for homes that had been cited multiple times for the same serious deficiency. At the time, Landa, Philipson or family members held stakes in at least 33 New York nursing homes.

ProPublica also noted that Landa is a **former member of the state health council** — the body that approves such applications.

**In plain language:** the agency that was supposed to screen him for a history of violations left the violations off the paperwork.

### B. Four Western New York homes declined sharply after a sale (Buffalo News, 2018)

*This is the closest geographic link to this district, and it comes with an important ownership caveat.*

An October 2018 Buffalo News investigation by Lou Michel examined four Erie County nursing homes — Emerald South, Emerald North, Safire Northtowns and Safire Southtowns. It documented CMS star ratings falling (Emerald South 4 stars to 2; Emerald North 3 stars to 1; Safire Northtowns to 1), certified nursing assistants cut **from 80 to 38**, and patient care time of **89 minutes per day against a state average of 133**. Annual rent charged to the operating companies rose from $58,008 to $1.2 million by 2016. A $10,000 fine followed the 2016 death of resident Ruth Murray. Emerald South was designated a **special focus facility** after a 2018 death.

**The ownership caveat matters and we are not going to bury it.** These four homes are owned by **Judy Landa**, Benjamin's wife. The Buffalo News reported that a spokesman for Benjamin Landa, Mark Weiss, said Judy Landa is a "passive investor" and that **Benjamin Landa "isn't involved in running the Erie County nursing homes that his wife has an ownership interest in."** Benjamin Landa's own documented role was as the DOH-appointed **receiver** of the facilities beginning July 2012, for about a year, before his wife's company purchased the operations for $2.6 million.

Judy Landa is a co-donor on the contributions to Langworthy documented above.

### C. Two Attorney General fraud lawsuits name him (2022)

*These are civil allegations. No court has found Landa liable on them. We state them as what they are: the sworn allegations of the State of New York.*

**The Villages of Orleans (Albion, Orleans County) — filed November 29, 2022.** Attorney General Letitia James named "Benjamin Landa and his son-in-law Joshua Farkovits" among the owner-respondents. The complaint alleges **$18.6 million was diverted** from $86.4 million in Medicare and Medicaid funding between 2015 and 2021, through systemic intentional understaffing. The facility's rating fell from three stars to one.

**Cold Spring Hills (Woodbury, Nassau County) — filed December 16, 2022.** The AG's release names Landa as a respondent and states he **"concealed his ownership."** It alleges **$22.6 million** in Medicaid and Medicare funds diverted through 13 shell companies, and describes adult children of the principals being installed as "straw owners... put in place to conceal their fathers' control" — naming Esther Farkovits, Landa's adult daughter, among them. The complaint alleges residents were left without wound care leading to amputations and infections, and that 166 residents died between March and June 2020, with 51 of 98 COVID deaths unreported.

We have **not** been able to independently verify the current disposition of either case, and we are not reporting outcomes we could not confirm.

### D. A federal audit found near-total Medicare non-compliance at a facility he co-owns (2025)

HHS Office of Inspector General report **A-02-22-01017**, issued November 14, 2025, examined Pinnacle Multicare Nursing and Rehabilitation Center. It found **99 of 100 sampled claims** did not meet Medicare payment requirements for calendar years 2020–2021, with $1.1 million in sampled overpayments and an estimated **at least $31.2 million** in total overpayments. Pinnacle did not concur with any of the OIG's recommendations.

**Important attribution note:** the OIG report **names only the facility, not Landa.** The ownership link between Landa and Pinnacle Multicare comes from ProPublica's March 2026 reporting, not from the audit itself.

In February 2026, Pinnacle Multicare sued the Department of Health and Human Services to block recoupment of the money, naming Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. and CMS Administrator Mehmet Oz as defendants. A temporary restraining order was denied. *Source: ProPublica, Mar. 23, 2026.*

### E. He sued a magazine over these characterizations, and a federal judge threw the case out (2023)

*This is the single most useful document in this entry, because in it a federal judge assessed whether published claims about Landa were accurate.*

Landa sued The American Prospect over a January 2022 article, "The Nursing Home Slumlord Manifesto." On **August 16, 2023**, Judge Joan M. Azrack of the Eastern District of New York **granted the defendants' motion to dismiss in its entirety** (*Landa v. American Prospect, Inc.*, No. 2:22-cv-01825).

The court held that the article's statements about Landa's involvement in Medicare and Medicaid fraud, understaffing, and the Special Focus Facility list were **"substantially true," protected by New York Civil Rights Law § 74's fair-report privilege, or non-actionable opinion.**

On the fraud characterization specifically, the opinion found that Landa, **in his capacity as Excellent Home Care LLC's "Managing Member," personally signed** the settlement of New York Attorney General allegations that the company "knowingly presented, or caused to be presented, false claims to Medicaid." That settlement was **$3.7 million**.

**In plain language:** Landa went to federal court arguing these published descriptions of him were false. The court examined them and dismissed his case.

### F. A human trafficking ruling that was later vacated — and why we are telling you that

*We include this because it circulates widely in shortened form, and the shortened form is not accurate.*

In *Paguirigan v. Prompt Nursing Employment Agency LLC* (E.D.N.Y. No. 17-cv-1302), a class action brought by Filipino nurses, Landa was a named defendant. In September 2019, Judge Nina Gershon granted the plaintiff summary judgment on liability, and in June 2021 held Landa and co-defendants liable for at least **$1.56 million** under the Trafficking Victims Protection Act. The case subsequently settled.

**That summary judgment order was then vacated on the parties' joint motion, in approximately May 2022.** This is documented in footnote 3 of the *Landa v. American Prospect* opinion. Separately, the Second Circuit (No. 19-3494, Sept. 22, 2020) dismissed the trafficking claim for lack of jurisdiction and **made no holding on Landa's personal TVPA liability**.

So the accurate statement is: *a federal judge granted summary judgment finding TVPA liability in 2019; the case settled and that order was vacated on joint motion in 2022.* Anyone who tells you a court has found Benjamin Landa liable for human trafficking, full stop, is overstating the record.

## A Note on What the Record Does — and Doesn't — Show

**What is documented:**
- Benjamin and Judy Landa gave $55,000 to Langworthy's committees, all before the July 3, 2025 vote (FEC records).
- Langworthy voted YES on the bill containing §71111, blocking the federal nursing home staffing rule until 2034 (Roll Call 190).
- Landa is named as a respondent in two New York Attorney General fraud lawsuits alleging a combined $41.2 million in diverted Medicare and Medicaid funds.
- A federal judge dismissed his defamation suit, holding that published descriptions of his fraud involvement and understaffing were substantially true or privileged.
- ProPublica documented state regulators omitting his facilities' fine history from ownership applications.

**What is not documented, and what we are not claiming:**
- Any connection whatsoever between Landa's donations and Langworthy's vote.
- Any communication between Landa and Langworthy, or between Landa and Langworthy's office, about the staffing rule or any other matter.
- Any criminal conduct by Benjamin Landa. The AG matters are **civil** suits containing **allegations**. Allegations are not findings.
- Any liability finding against Landa under the Trafficking Victims Protection Act that remains in force. It was vacated.
- Any involvement by Benjamin Landa in operating the four Erie County facilities, which his spokesman has denied and which are owned by his wife.

Contributions from health care operators to members of Congress who vote on health care policy are legal and routine. Nothing here suggests otherwise. What this entry adds to the earlier one is context about **who** the contributor is, drawn entirely from documents that were already public before we wrote a word.

## Verdict: DOCUMENTED PATTERN

A donor who gave Langworthy's committees $55,000 before a vote blocking federal nursing home staffing standards is, according to two ProPublica investigations, a Buffalo News investigation, two New York Attorney General lawsuits, a federal Inspector General audit, and a federal court opinion dismissing his own defamation suit, an operator with a sustained documented record of understaffing and alleged Medicaid and Medicare fraud. Langworthy has not been asked about it publicly and has not addressed it.
