<div class="tt-intro">

**Why this page exists.** After the November 2026 election, Rep. Langworthy
said what kind of representative he intends to be. This page holds him to
his own words. Each commitment below is quoted from him. Next to it are the
measures that show whether it is happening, the baseline from the 119th
Congress (2025-2026), and the running count for the 120th Congress
(2027-2028).

Each measure comes from a public record: bill-status files from the
Government Publishing Office, House Clerk roll calls, his own pages and
releases. This page judges him by his standard, not ours. Where the record
supports him, the page says so first.

*Updated monthly. Next update: early January 2027, when the 120th Congress
convenes.*

</div>

{{< commitment n="1" title="Relationships across the aisle" >}}
> "It's important that you have relationships across the aisle. We're not antagonistic to one another. We're of different parties. We vote differently, but we dig in together and work hard for important priorities for the region."

> "There are plenty of things that we could work on together that aren't necessarily Democratic or Republican solutions. They're just common sense solutions."

<p class="tt-source">Interview clips from WENY and WIVB, posted on his campaign Facebook page on November 5, 2026. Both quotes were checked against his own voice in the clips.</p>

| Measure | 119th Congress baseline | 120th Congress |
|---|---|---|
| His bills with at least one Democratic cosponsor | 28 of 42 (67%) | Starts January 2027 |
| His bills with a Democratic original cosponsor | 24 of 42 | Starts January 2027 |
| Democrat-led bills he cosponsored | 58 of 297 cosponsorships (19.5%) | Starts January 2027 |
| Democrat-led bills he joined as an original cosponsor | 17 | Starts January 2027 |
| His bills that became law | 0 of 42 | Starts January 2027 |

<div class="tt-plain">

**In plain language:** the baseline supports him on the first part. Two of every three bills he introduced in the 119th Congress drew at least one Democratic cosponsor, among them his FEMA Act and the Safer Skies Act. The weaker number is the last one: none of his bills became law. In the minority, a bipartisan cosponsor list is the main route to passage, so it is the measure to watch.

</div>

<p class="tt-method">Method: House bills only (H.R.), not resolutions. Counts come from each bill's govinfo BILLSTATUS record, refreshed November 11, 2026. Withdrawn cosponsorships are excluded.</p>
