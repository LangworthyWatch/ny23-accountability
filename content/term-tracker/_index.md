## Why this page exists

After the November 2026 election, Rep. Langworthy said what kind of
representative he intends to be. This page holds him to his own words. Each
commitment below is quoted from his own posts. Next to it are the measures
that show whether it is happening, the baseline from the 119th Congress
(2025-2026), and the running count for the 120th Congress (2027-2028).

Each measure comes from a public record: bill-status files from the
Government Publishing Office, House Clerk roll calls, his own pages and
releases. This page judges him by his standard, not ours. Where the record
supports him, the page says so first.

*Updated monthly. Next update: early January 2027, when the 120th Congress
convenes.*

## 1. "Relationships across the aisle"

> "It's important that you have relationships across the aisle. We're not
> antagonistic to one another. We're of different parties. We vote
> differently, but we dig in together and work hard for important
> priorities for the region."
>
> "There are plenty of things that we could work on together that aren't
> necessarily Democratic or Republican solutions. They're just common sense
> solutions."

*Campaign Facebook page, interview clips from WENY and WIVB posted about
November 6, 2026.*

| Measure | 119th Congress baseline | 120th Congress |
|---|---|---|
| His bills with at least one Democratic cosponsor | 28 of 42 (67%) | Starts January 2027 |
| His bills with a Democratic original cosponsor | 24 of 42 | Starts January 2027 |
| Democrat-led bills he cosponsored | 55 of 293 cosponsorships (19%) | Starts January 2027 |
| Democrat-led bills he joined as an original cosponsor | 17 | Starts January 2027 |
| His bills that became law | 0 of 42 | Starts January 2027 |

**In plain language:** the baseline supports him on the first part. Two of
every three bills he introduced in the 119th Congress drew at least one
Democratic cosponsor. Among them are his FEMA Act (29 Democrats) and the
Safer Skies Act (26). The weaker number is the last one: none of his bills
became law. In the minority, a bipartisan cosponsor list is the main route
to passage, so it is the measure to watch.

*Method: House bills only (H.R.), not resolutions. Counts come from each
bill's govinfo BILLSTATUS record. Withdrawn cosponsorships are excluded.
Baseline as of the October 2026 bulk files.*
