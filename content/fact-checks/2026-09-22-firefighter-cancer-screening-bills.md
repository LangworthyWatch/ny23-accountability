## Why this matters

Most of NY-23 is protected by volunteer fire companies, and cancer is the
leading cause of line-of-duty death in the fire service. On September 21,
Rep. Langworthy visited the Clarence Center Fire Department during an
esophageal cancer screening for firefighters and posted that he is "fighting
in Washington to expand access to cancer screenings and treatments." His
office's advisory for the event named no bill. This entry checks the claim
against the bills the fire service itself is asking Congress to pass.

---

## Statement

**Source:** Official Facebook page, September 21, 2026, over a WGRZ clip
about the screening:

> "Our firefighters put their lives and health on the line to protect us.
> Data now shows that because of what they are exposed to on the job, they
> have a 62% greater risk of developing esophageal cancer. Early detection is
> the key to saving lives and that's why I'm fighting in Washington to expand
> access to cancer screenings and treatments."

Fingerlakes1, reporting on his office's media advisory the day before: he
"will also discuss efforts in Washington to expand access to cancer
screening and treatment for firefighters, though the media advisory did not
identify a specific bill." The advisory also did not say which medical
organization would conduct the tests.

---

## The record

The International Association of Fire Fighters publishes a legislative
priority book each year. Its 2026 edition has a section headed "Expanding
Access to Cancer Screenings." The bill under that heading is the FIRE Cancer
Act, H.R. 1610, which "would establish a new funding source within the
Assistance to Firefighters Grant program to help departments provide cancer
screenings." The Congressional Research Service summary says it covers
"cancer prevention programs (e.g., multi-cancer early detection testing)
for firefighting personnel."

Each bill below was checked against its official cosponsor roster.

**In plain language:** he has supported the fire service bills that most of
Congress supports, and he voted for the two that became law. The one thing
his post says he is fighting for, cancer screening for firefighters, is the
subject of two bills. He has signed neither. A neighboring New York
Republican has signed the one the fire fighters' union is asking for.

## The statistic

The WGRZ clip he posted cites a different number: the firefighters'
association and the federal occupational health institute "report that
firefighters have a 9% higher risk of being diagnosed with cancer." The
Firefighter Cancer Support Network attributes that figure, and a 14 percent
higher risk of dying from cancer, to the institute's study of 30,000
firefighters. The 62 percent esophageal figure appears in his office's
advisory; Fingerlakes1 describes it as "a figure also used in firefighter
screening program" materials. This entry did not locate the underlying study
and does not dispute the figure.

One related item on the record: the federal National Firefighter Registry
for Cancer went offline in the spring of 2025 after layoffs at the
occupational health institute, and the trade publication FireRescue1
reported it back online on May 15, 2025. No statement from him about the
registry was located on his website.

## The fair reading

- **The general claim has support.** He cosponsors the Medicare
  multi-cancer screening bill, the fallen-heroes act, the PFAS gear bill and
  the 9/11 health funding fix, and he voted for the defense bill that made
  firefighter cancer a line-of-duty death. "Fighting to expand access to
  cancer screenings" is a fair description of the Medicare bill.
- **Most members are on neither screening bill.** H.R. 1610 has 7
  cosponsors and H.R. 2921 has 11. Not signing a small bill is ordinary.
  The gap is specific to the claim he chose to make at this event.
- **Showing up counts.** Attending a screening at a volunteer company draws
  attention to a real risk. This entry does not question the visit.
- **His office may have had another bill in mind.** The advisory named none.
  If his office identifies one, this entry will be updated.

## Questions this raises

1. Which bill did his office mean by "efforts in Washington to expand access
   to cancer screening and treatment for firefighters"?
2. Will he cosponsor the FIRE Cancer Act, which would let fire departments
   like Clarence Center's use federal grant money for the kind of screening
   he attended?
3. Who conducted the screening, and did the company or its trade group ask
   his office to attend?
