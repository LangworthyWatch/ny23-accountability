## Why this matters

Three weeks ago we documented Langworthy announcing money for two hospital
radiology labs as "delivered" and "secured" when it was only a **request** in a
draft bill. He has now used the same past tense for the Olean Police Department.
This one has gotten further — it passed the House on June 4 — but it is still not
law, and the reason that matters is not hypothetical: the last time he "secured" a
batch of these earmarks, Congress **wiped out every one of them.**

## The Facts

**What is true: it did pass the House.** The **City of Olean Police Department
Modernization Project** is on Langworthy's official **FY2027** Community Project
Funding disclosure. His own **federal-nexus letter** (March 19, 2026) shows the
money would flow through the **USDA Rural Development Community Facilities
program** (7 U.S.C. §1926(a)) — that is, the **FY2027 Agriculture-Rural
Development appropriations bill**. That bill is **H.R. 8646**, and it **passed the
House on June 4, 2026**, by **213-210** (Roll Call 205; Langworthy voted yes). The
project is named in the Community Project Funding table of the accompanying
report, **H. Rept. 119-632**: *"Olean, NY — City of Olean — Police Department
Modernization — 1,000,000 — Langworthy."* His post's line "this funding passed the
House last month" is **accurate**.

**What is not true is "secured."** H.R. 8646 was **received in the Senate on June
8, 2026**, and the Senate has taken no action on it since. **No FY2027
appropriations bill has become law.** By his own words in the same post, "the
Senate must act." The Olean Times Herald described it, accurately, as **"House
movement."** (The nexus letter also confirms this is a **single federal request**
to modernize a 1959 facility that has "reached critical failure" — there is no
separate, already-secured funding stream for it.)

**The same announcement bundled a second request that has not passed anything.**
On the same day, he also touted the **Cattaraugus County Emergency 911 Center
Modernization and Upgrade Project** (303 Court St., Little Valley) — another item
on his official FY2027 CPF disclosure, part of what the Olean Times Herald
summarized as **"House movement"** on more than **$2 million** in local
public-safety requests. Unlike the police facility, the 911 project is **not** in
H. Rept. 119-632's project table, and it is not in the only other FY2027
appropriations bill the House has passed (H.R. 8595, National Security and State,
H. Rept. 119-631). It has cleared **neither** chamber. His Facebook post says the
money "will strengthen our 911 system" for Cattaraugus County — future tense doing
the work the past-tense headline ("secured") skips.

**This is the third documented instance of the same past-tense framing** — after
the [FY2026 appropriations credit-claim](/fact-checks/2026-02-11-fy2026-appropriations-credit/)
and the [June 2026 hospital-radiology posts](/fact-checks/2026-06-24-hospital-radiology-credit-claim/)
(UPMC Chautauqua $300K, Arnot $500K), where he said "delivered" and "secured" for
identical not-yet-enacted FY2027 requests.

**And here is why "secured" is the wrong word.** For **FY2025**, Congress never
passed full-year appropriations; it enacted a **full-year continuing resolution
(P.L. 119-4) that funded zero Community Project Funding.** Every FY2025 earmark
sitting in the House and Senate bills — **Langworthy's own included** — received
**nothing**, and he had to **re-file the same projects for FY2026** to get them
enacted. Being in a bill did not "secure" a dollar. Olean is at that same,
vulnerable stage now.

**In plain language:** He told a police department he "secured" their money. He
filed a request for it. His last batch of identical requests was erased before it
became law.

---

## Context

The request is genuine, the project (a 67-year-old police facility) is real,
filing earmarks is a legitimate part of the job, and Langworthy **did disclose**
in the same post that "the Senate must act." The issue is not that he is doing
anything improper — it is the recurring gap between the headline ("I secured")
and the reality (a request that has not passed, in a process that erased his last
batch). "Requested $1 million for next year's budget" is accurate. "Secured" is
not, not yet.

---

## Questions This Raises

1. Is money that has passed one chamber, awaits the other, and is not law — money
   his own post says still needs Senate action — accurately called "secured"?
2. Given that his FY2025 earmarks were zeroed out at this same stage, what
   assurance does Olean have that this survives?
3. Will he tell Olean if the funding is reduced or dropped, as the FY2025 items
   were?

## Correction and Update

**September 8, 2026.** A routine re-check of the FY2027 appropriations record
against primary sources found an error in this entry, now corrected.

**What was wrong.** This entry originally described the FY2027 Agriculture-Rural
Development bill as "one of the twelve FY2027 bills the House has been moving
through **committee**, one at a time," and asked in Question 1 whether money "that
has **not passed the House floor**" could be called secured. **Both were
incorrect.** The bill is **H.R. 8646**, and it had **passed the House on June 4,
2026** — five weeks before this entry was published — by 213-210 on Roll Call 205,
with Langworthy voting yes. The Olean project is named in the Community Project
Funding table of the accompanying report, **H. Rept. 119-632**, at the full
$1,000,000. **Langworthy's statement that "this funding passed the House last
month" was accurate, and this entry wrongly implied otherwise.** The error came
from treating the twelve-bill process as still being at the committee stage
without checking the roll-call record. Both passages have been rewritten.

**What did not change.** The verdict stands. H.R. 8646 has sat in the Senate since
June 8, 2026 with no action; **no FY2027 appropriations bill has become law**; and
his own post said "the Senate must act." "Secured" still describes money that does
not yet exist. The Olean Times Herald's "House movement," quoted here from the
start, was the accurate description then and now.

**And the FY2025 risk this entry warned about is now live again.** On September 2,
2026, Congress enacted a stopgap (**P.L. 119-103**) funding the government at
FY2026 levels **through December 11, 2026**. It carries **no Community Project
Funding** — the phrase does not appear in the enacted text. That is the same
posture that erased his FY2025 earmarks: projects sitting in an unfinished
appropriations bill while a continuing resolution carries the government past
them. The Olean money remains unappropriated.

Two other Langworthy projects appear in the same House-passed report and were not
previously noted here: **Hornell fire truck replacement ($896,646)** and **Village
of Angola snow removal equipment ($500,000)**. Like Olean, both passed the House
and await the Senate.
