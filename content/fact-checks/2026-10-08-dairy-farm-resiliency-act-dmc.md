## Why this matters

Dairy Margin Coverage (DMC) is the federal government's main income safety
net for dairy farms. It pays out when the gap between the national milk
price and feed costs falls below a level the farmer chooses. The program
did get stronger in 2025: the amount of milk a farm can insure at the cheap
Tier I rate rose from 5 million to 6 million pounds a year, production
histories were updated, and the program was extended through 2031.

Those changes are real, and Rep. Langworthy voted for the bill that made
them. The question is the possessive. He told constituents that *his* Dairy
Farm Resiliency Act strengthened the program. The record shows a bill that
was introduced by another member first, that he reintroduced word for word,
that never received a hearing, a markup or a vote, and whose one original
idea was not enacted. The 6-million-pound change became law because the
House Agriculture Committee put it in its own farm bill a year before he
filed his, and then carried it into the One Big Beautiful Bill Act.

## The record

### What his bill says and what happened to it

H.R. 294, the Dairy Farm Resiliency Act, was introduced by Rep. Langworthy
on January 9, 2025, with Reps. Derrick Van Orden (R-WI) and Joe Courtney
(D-CT) as original cosponsors. It is one page. Section 2 does two things:

| Provision | Bill text |
|---|---|
| (a) Production history | Strikes "during any one of the 2011, 2012, or 2013 calendar years" and inserts "in the most recent three-year history, as calculated once every five years" |
| (b) Tier I and Tier II premiums | In Section 1407(b) and (c) of the Agricultural Act of 2014, "by striking `5,000,000' and inserting `6,000,000'" |

Its history, from the Government Publishing Office status file:

| Date | Action |
|---|---|
| January 9, 2025 | Introduced; referred to the Committee on Agriculture |
| February 14, 2025 | Referred to two subcommittees |
| Since | Nothing. No hearing, no markup, no vote. Six cosponsors in 21 months. |

The One Big Beautiful Bill Act's status file lists 29 related bills. H.R.
294 is not one of them.

### The bill was Rep. Marc Molinaro's first

A bill with the same name and the same operative text, H.R. 4125, was
introduced on June 14, 2023 by Rep. Marc Molinaro (R-NY-19), with Reps.
Abigail Spanberger, Derrick Van Orden and Elissa Slotkin as original
cosponsors. It drew eight cosponsors, including Rep. Claudia Tenney.
Rep. Langworthy was not among them. It died in subcommittee in July 2023.

The two bills' Section 2 texts are identical. After Mr. Molinaro lost his
seat in November 2024, Rep. Langworthy reintroduced the bill under his own
name.

His January 10, 2025 press release announcing seven agriculture bills said
"Six of the seven bills were previously introduced by Congressman
Langworthy in the 118th Congress." For this bill, the status files do not
show that. The same release described the Dairy Farm Resiliency Act only
as "ensuring that production levels are updated every five years." It did
not mention 6 million pounds.

### The committee had the 6-million-pound text a year before he filed

On May 21, 2024, House Agriculture Chairman Glenn "GT" Thompson introduced
H.R. 8467, the Farm, Food, and National Security Act of 2024, the
committee's farm bill. Section 1402(a) reads:

> "Tier I.--Section 1407(b) of the Agricultural Act of 2014 (7 U.S.C.
> 9057(b)) is amended-- (1) in the heading, by striking `5,000,000' and
> inserting `6,000,000'; and (2) in paragraph (1), by striking `5,000,000'
> and inserting `6,000,000'."

Section 1401 set production history to "the highest annual milk marketings
of the participating dairy operation during any one of the 2021, 2022, or
2023 calendar years." The committee ordered the bill reported on May 24,
2024, by a vote of 33 to 21. Rep. Langworthy, by his own January 2025
release, "served as a member of the House Agriculture Committee during his
first term," so he was on the committee that adopted this text. That is a
real connection to the provision. It is not the same as his bill having
done it.

### What became law

The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (Public Law 119-21), Section 10313, "Dairy
Policy Updates," is the provision that changed the program. It tracks the
committee's 2024 farm bill, section for section:

| Element | Enacted law, Sec. 10313 | H.R. 8467 (committee, May 2024) | H.R. 294 (Langworthy, Jan. 2025) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Production history | Highest of 2021, 2022 or 2023 | Highest of 2021, 2022 or 2023 | "most recent three-year history, as calculated once every five years" |
| Tier I and II premiums | 5,000,000 to 6,000,000 | 5,000,000 to 6,000,000 | 5,000,000 to 6,000,000 |
| Payment cap (Sec. 1406) | 5,000,000 to 6,000,000 | not included | not included |
| 25% premium discount | 2026 through 2031 | 2025 through 2029 | not included |
| Program extended | through 2031 | not in these sections | not included |

The one provision that was distinctive to his bill, a production-history
reset every five years, was not enacted. The one provision his bill shares
with the law, the 6-million-pound threshold, was in the committee's text
before he sponsored anything.

Rep. Langworthy voted for the One Big Beautiful Bill Act on May 22, 2025
(Roll Call 145, Yea) and again on July 3, 2025 (Roll Call 190, Aye). That
vote is the whole of his documented role in enacting the change.

### Who the dairy industry credits

The National Milk Producers Federation, May 14, 2025, on the committee's
reconciliation text: "We commend Chairman GT Thompson and committee
members for advancing important investments that will help support and
create opportunities for dairy." Its statement in the committee's May 21,
2025 release: "NMPF commends House Agriculture Committee Chairman GT
Thompson (R-PA) and members of the committee." Neither statement names
Rep. Langworthy or the Dairy Farm Resiliency Act.

Spectrum News, reporting from Wisconsin on May 15, 2024, described the
6-million-pound change as the committee's proposal and noted that Rep. Van
Orden "cosponsored the legislation to do this," meaning the Molinaro bill.

### What the post gets right

"It protects up to 6 million pounds of milk when prices drop or feed costs
soar." USDA's Farm Service Agency, September 30, 2026: the law "expanded
the amount of production eligible for Tier 1 protection from five to six
million pounds," and DMC "provides financial protection when the
difference between the national all-milk price and average feed cost falls
below a producer-selected coverage level." Milk above 6 million pounds can
still be covered at the higher Tier II premium, so the figure is the cheap
tier's ceiling rather than the program's. As plain-language description of
the program, the sentence is accurate. Enrollment for 2027 runs October 5
through December 18, 2026.

## Fair reading

- Rep. Langworthy sponsored a bill containing the 6-million-pound language
  and voted for the law that enacted it. A member who sponsors a bill, sits
  on the committee, and votes for the final package can fairly say he
  supported the change.
- "My Dairy Farm Resiliency Act strengthened the Dairy Margin Coverage
  program" says something different. The Act did not strengthen anything:
  it was never acted on, is not a related bill to the law, and its
  distinctive provision was dropped. The change was the committee's text,
  introduced by the chairman, first proposed in a colleague's bill, and
  enacted in a 900-page reconciliation law.
- The press release's "championed ... and successfully advanced into law
  through H.R. 1" is closer to defensible, but it still presents committee
  work that predated his bill as a provision he carried.

This is the pattern the site has documented before: announcing an outcome
that his office did not produce as though it did (see the farm-bill and
grants entries linked below).

## Questions this raises

1. The January 2025 release said six of the seven bills were previously
   introduced by Rep. Langworthy in the 118th Congress. The Dairy Farm
   Resiliency Act was Rep. Molinaro's. Which of the seven was the exception?
2. Did Rep. Langworthy vote for H.R. 8467 at the May 2024 committee markup,
   when the 6-million-pound text was adopted? The committee's roll call is
   not in the public status file.
3. Why did the 2025 release describe the bill only as a five-year
   production-history update, the part that did not become law, and the
   2026 release describe it only as the 6-million-pound change, the part
   that did?
