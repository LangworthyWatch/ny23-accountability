## Why this matters

NY-23 is a safely Republican district. Rep. Langworthy won it by about 20
points this month. A common response from his critics is that the district
is gerrymandered. This entry checks that claim against the court record and
the maps themselves, and checks what Rep. Langworthy has said about New
York's maps.

The short version: the lines were drawn by a court in 2022 and redrawn by
the Legislature in 2024. No court has found either version to be a
gerrymander. The only New York map a court did find to be a partisan
gerrymander was struck down before it was ever used, and it would have made
NY-23 more Republican, not less.

## Three maps in five years

| Map | Drawn by | Status | NY-23's lean |
|---|---|---|---|
| February 2022 | The Legislature | Struck down April 27, 2022, never used | FiveThirtyEight: R+26 |
| May 2022 | A court-appointed special master | Used in the 2022 election | FiveThirtyEight: R+23. 2020 presidential vote: Trump 57.6%, Biden 40.4% |
| February 2024 | The Legislature (Chapter 92 of 2024) | Used in 2024 and 2026 | 2020 presidential vote: Trump 58.1%, Biden 39.9%. 2024: Trump 60.1%, Harris 39.1% |

*FiveThirtyEight's partisan lean and the presidential results by district,
calculated by The Downballot, are different measures. Compare within a
column, not across.*

**The 2022 map that was struck down.** The Court of Appeals, New York's top
court, held that the Legislature had bypassed the constitutional process,
creating maps "in a nontransparent manner controlled exclusively by the
dominant political party." A majority of the court also held that the
congressional map was "substantively unconstitutional as drawn with
impermissible partisan purpose," after the Appellate Division found it was
"drawn to discourage competition and favor democrats." Four of the seven
judges joined that holding. That version of NY-23 ran from Chautauqua
County east through Steuben and Tioga into parts of Broome and Chenango
counties, and FiveThirtyEight listed two Republican incumbents in it.

**The 2022 court map.** The case had been filed in Steuben County. The trial
judge there, Justice Patrick McAllister, appointed Dr. Jonathan Cervas as
special master and adopted his maps on May 20, 2022. The judge wrote that
"the maps remain almost perfectly neutral, meaning the maps do not favor or
disfavor any political party," and that "Location of a candidate received
zero consideration from the court." Cervas wrote that the maps "were drawn
blind to the homes of incumbents," and that "Most of these indicators show
a slight Republican bias" for the map statewide. The Princeton
Gerrymandering Project grades the court map A overall: A for partisan
fairness, A for geography, C for competitiveness. Its report card for the
struck legislative map currently gives it a D overall, with a Democratic
partisan advantage.

**The 2024 map.** In December 2023, the Court of Appeals ruled 4 to 3 that
under the 2014 constitutional amendments, "New York courts no longer have
the blanket authority to create decade-long redistricting plans," and
ordered the redistricting commission to try again. The commission approved
a plan 9 to 1 on February 15, 2024, which WAMC reported left the court's
map "largely intact." The Legislature rejected it and passed its own map on February 28,
2024, 115 to 33 in the Assembly and 45 to 17 in the Senate, with what WAMC
reported as "a handful of Republican minority party lawmakers also voting
yes." The governor signed it that day.

**In plain language:** the one map a court found to be rigged was drawn by
Democrats, would have made NY-23 *more* Republican in order to help
Democrats elsewhere, and was thrown out. The neutral map a court drew,
without looking at where incumbents lived, voted for Trump by 17 points in
2020. The 2024 Legislature's version is about one point more Republican
than that. Under every version drawn so far, NY-23 is a Republican
district.

## What the 2024 map changed in NY-23

Census Bureau relationship files for the 118th and 119th Congresses show
which towns moved. Populations are the Census Bureau's 2020 base counts.

| Moved out of NY-23 | Population | Now in |
|---|---|---|
| Lackawanna (Erie) | 19,961 | NY-26 |
| Most of West Seneca (Erie) | Part of 45,528 | NY-26 |
| Avoca, Cohocton, Dansville, Prattsburgh, Pulteney, Wayland, Wheeler (Steuben) | 14,205 | NY-24 |
| Hector, Reading, Tyrone (Schuyler) | 8,253 | NY-24 |

| Moved into NY-23 | Population |
|---|---|
| All nine towns of Tioga County | 48,458 |
| Pendleton (Niagara) | 7,034 |
| Part of the town of Lockport and a small part of Wheatfield (Niagara) | Not available at the town level |

NY-23 now contains all of Allegany, Cattaraugus, Chautauqua, Chemung and
Tioga counties, and parts of Erie, Niagara, Schuyler and Steuben.

**In plain language:** the 2024 map swapped some suburbs south of Buffalo
and seven northeastern Steuben towns for Tioga County and a slice of
Niagara County. Where a town was split, the Census files give land area,
not people, so this entry does not estimate the population of the pieces.

**His home town.** Rep. Langworthy's official House biography says he and
his wife "currently reside in Pendleton, NY," and Niagara County property
records show he has owned a home there since May 2021. Under the 2022 court
map, which was drawn "blind to the homes of incumbents," Pendleton was in
NY-26. For his first term he represented a district he did not live in,
which the Constitution allows; House members must live only in their
state. The Legislature's 2024 map moved all of Pendleton into NY-23.
Pendleton borders Clarence, which is in NY-23 under both maps, so the
change keeps the district in one piece. New York's constitution says
districts shall not be drawn "for the purpose of favoring or disfavoring
incumbents." This site has found no record of why Pendleton was moved or
whether anyone asked for the change.

## "A gerrymander that's led to them taking two seats"

No court has found the 2024 map to be a partisan gerrymander. The one court
challenge to it this site found, over NY-11 on Staten Island, was a voting
rights claim. The U.S. Supreme Court stayed a lower-court order to redraw
that district on March 2, 2026, and the parties dismissed the case with
prejudice on March 19.

What the 2024 map did do is shift a few districts. Measured by the 2020
presidential vote on each set of lines:

| District | 2020 margin, court lines | 2020 margin, 2024 lines | Change | 2024 House result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| NY-22 | Biden +7.5 | Biden +11.4 | 3.9 points toward Democrats | Democrat gained the seat, won by 9.1 |
| NY-3 | Biden +8.2 | Biden +11.3 | 3.1 toward Democrats | Democrat held the seat, won by 3.6 |
| NY-4 | Biden +14.5 | Biden +14.5 | None | Democrat gained the seat, won by 2.3 |
| NY-19 | Biden +4.6 | Biden +4.5 | 0.1 toward Republicans | Democrat gained the seat, won by 2.2 |
| NY-1 | Biden +0.2 | Trump +1.8 | 2.0 toward Republicans | Republican held |
| NY-23 | Trump +17.2 | Trump +18.2 | 1.0 toward Republicans | Republican held |
| NY-24 | Trump +17.2 | Trump +20.3 | 3.1 toward Republicans | Republican held |

*House margins are two-party shares of all votes on each candidate's party
lines, from the House Clerk's official 2024 statistics.*

**In plain language:** he is right that the 2024 map moved two districts,
NY-3 and NY-22, toward Democrats, and Democrats won both. But Democrats had
already won NY-3 on the court's lines: Rep. Tom Suozzi took the oath for
the seat on February 28, 2024, after a special election, the same day the
new map was signed. In NY-22, the Democrat's margin was more than twice the shift. Two of
the three seats Democrats gained in 2024, NY-4 and NY-19, were essentially
unchanged by the new map. The same map also moved NY-1, NY-23 and NY-24
toward Republicans. Whether any seat would have gone the other way on the
old lines cannot be known from these numbers.

## "Once a decade"

He has said this twice this year, and it fits what he said as state party
chairman in 2022. Then, he called the Legislature's first map "textbook
filthy, partisan gerrymandering" (WSKG, February 2, 2022) and greeted the
court's maps with: "Today is a good day for democracy" (New York Republican
State Committee, May 21, 2022). In August 2025, responding to Gov. Hochul's
talk of redrawing New York's map after Texas redrew its own, he told WIVB:
"She can't do that before the midterm elections. That's all political
posturing."

New York is now partway down that road. On June 3, 2026, the Legislature
gave first passage to a constitutional amendment (S.10637-A/A.11553-A).
It would let the Legislature change congressional districts before the
2030 census, and those changes "shall not be subject to any of the
provisions" of the sections that now bar districts drawn "for the purpose
of favoring or disfavoring incumbents or other particular candidates or
political parties." The amendment must pass the Legislature again in 2027
and then win a statewide vote. On November 12, Gov. Hochul said, "We're
halfway there," without committing to the second step.

Two House bills would write his position into federal law by barring states
from redistricting more than once after each census. H.R. 4889, introduced
by Rep. Kevin Kiley of California in August 2025, does only that. H.R. 7219,
Rep. Mike Lawler's FAIR MAP Act, does it along with other provisions. Rep.
Langworthy is not a cosponsor of either, according to their govinfo bill
status records, and the Clerk lists one signature on the discharge petition
for H.R. 4889 (No. 21): Rep. Kiley's.

## Claims checked

| Claim | Finding |
|---|---|
| "When the courts put fair maps on the table, that we ran on" | **TRUE.** The 2022 map was drawn by a court-appointed special master without regard to incumbents' homes. The court called it "almost perfectly neutral," and the Princeton Gerrymandering Project grades it A. He ran on it in 2022. |
| "We've already had a gerrymander that's led to them taking two seats in the last election" | **MISSING CONTEXT.** The 2024 map moved NY-3 and NY-22 toward Democrats, and Democrats won both. But NY-3 had already flipped on the court's lines, the NY-22 winner's margin was more than double the shift, and no court has found the 2024 map to be a gerrymander. |
| "Redistricting should be done once a decade" | **Not rated.** This is a stated position. It is consistent with his statements about New York since 2022. |

**Overall: MOSTLY TRUE.** His description of the 2022 court map holds up.
His "two seats" claim is partly right and leaves out context.

## So is NY-23 gerrymandered?

On the record, no. Its current lines are the Legislature's 2024 map, not the
court's 2022 map, but no court has found the 2024 map to be a partisan
gerrymander. NY-23 was Republican by double digits under the court's
neutral version, and it is about a point more Republican under the current
one.

---

## The fair reading

- A district can be lopsided without being gerrymandered. Who lives where
  matters, and these counties have voted Republican under every map drawn
  for them this decade.
- He is consistent on New York. His "once a decade" position matches what
  he said in 2022, and New York's Democratic Legislature is the one now
  moving to allow a mid-decade redraw.
- His "two seats" line has a factual core. The 2024 map did help Democrats
  in two districts, and Democrats won both.
