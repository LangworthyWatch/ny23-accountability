## Why This Matters for NY-23

The Town of Newstead is getting $5 million for a new community center. **That money is real, it is enacted, and Rep. Langworthy genuinely helped secure it.** This entry does not dispute any of that, and readers looking for a story about phantom funding will not find one here.

What it examines is narrower: on the same day Langworthy announced he "secured" the $5 million, New York's two senators announced they had secured the same $5 million for the same building. His announcement did not mention them. Six months later, the same money was announced again, as news, with a press conference and an oversized check-style sign.

---

## A. The Project, and Why It Is Not in Doubt

From Langworthy's own February 4, 2026 press release, the need is specific and documented:

- A **32,000-square-foot** community center to replace inadequate existing space
- The current Senior Center serves **more than 880 members** and operates out of a **shared facility with the Newstead Fire Department**, under a lease **expiring in 2030**
- Local youth programs serve **more than 350 participants** a year without room to grow
- The new building is to include a flexible Senior Center, multipurpose rooms, classrooms, fitness and recreation areas, and a gymnasium, and to serve as a **designated emergency warming shelter with backup power**

Town Supervisor Dawn Izydorczak, quoted in that release: *"This new center will be a tremendous boon for the community, bringing together residents of all ages."*

**A note on the two names, because it looks like a discrepancy and is not.** His August posts and the sign at the event say "Senior Center"; the caption and the Spectrum News chyron say "Community Center." His own February release resolves it: the Community Center **contains** a flexible Senior Center. Both labels describe the same building. This entry treats that as a non-issue.

---

## B. The Same $5 Million, Announced by Three Offices on the Same Day

**February 4, 2026 — Rep. Langworthy's office:**

> "Congressman Nick Langworthy announced today that **he has secured** $5,000,000 in Community Project Funding for the Town of Newstead to construct a new, state-of-the-art Community Center..."

**February 4, 2026 — Sens. Schumer and Gillibrand:**

The senators announced **$9,275,000** for Western New York projects in the **FY 2026 Consolidated Appropriations Bill**. Among the seven projects listed:

| Project | Amount |
|---|---|
| FeedMore Western New York | $500,000 |
| National Comedy Center (Jamestown) | $500,000 |
| St. Bonaventure University | $1,100,000 |
| **Town of Newstead Community Center** | **$5,000,000** |
| Canisius University | $1,000,000 |
| Chautauqua County IDA | $650,000 |
| Niagara University | $525,000 |

Their description of the Newstead project: *"The new 32,000 square-foot Community Center will expand community services and promote healthy lifestyles to address the Town's rapid growth of both senior membership and youth recreation programs."* The same 880 seniors, the same 350 youth, the same emergency warming shelter with backup power.

**In plain language:** this is one appropriation, in one bill, for one building. Same amount, same square footage, same beneficiaries, announced the same day by a House member and two senators. It is not $5 million from Langworthy plus $5 million from the senators.

---

## C. What Each Announcement Left Out

Neither Langworthy release nor the August posts mention Sens. Schumer or Gillibrand. The August 5 live video caption reads:

> "Proud to be in the Town of Newstead with Supervisor Dawn Izydorczak to announce the **$5,000,000 I have secured** for their new Senior Center project"

**This cuts both ways, and the entry says so.** The senators' release does not mention Langworthy either. Credit-sharing omissions in appropriations announcements are not unique to any one office.

**What is fair to him, stated plainly:**

- Community Project Funding requires a **House member** to submit and defend the request. Langworthy did that. Without a House sponsor the project does not appear in the House bill at all.
- Joint House-and-Senate requests are a normal and often decisive path for these projects. Two chambers backing the same request makes it likelier to survive.
- The senators securing it **does not mean he did not**. Both statements are true at once.

**What is missing from his version:** that the money he describes as "secured" was secured in parallel by the two senators, and that a constituent reading "the $5,000,000 I have secured" would reasonably conclude it came through one office.

---

## D. The Second Announcement

The February release announced the award. Roughly six months later, on or about **August 5, 2026**, the same $5 million was announced again across three posts on the verified congressional page: a live video from Newstead with the same Town Supervisor, a follow-up post reading *"I'm proud to secure $5 million in Community Project Funding to help make it a reality,"* and coverage from Spectrum News. The event featured a check-style sign reading **"CONGRESSMAN NICK LANGWORTHY / FIGHTING FOR OUR COMMUNITY / SECURED $5,000,000 FOR THE TOWN OF NEWSTEAD SENIOR CENTER."**

**In fairness:** marking a project milestone months after an award is ordinary constituent communication, and there is nothing improper about holding an event for a real project. Local officials generally want them.

**The documented point** is that the announcements use the present tense of a fresh accomplishment for money awarded in February, without indicating that the award had already been announced. This site has documented the same construction before: **[the same $1.25 million in Allegany County, announced a third time](/fact-checks/2026-07-16-allegany-water-funding-third-announcement/)**, verdict DOCUMENTED PATTERN, and **["What 'I Secured' Leaves Out"](/fact-checks/2026-07-09-earmark-credit-shared-out-of-district/)**, verdict MISSING CONTEXT.

## A Note on What the Record Does — and Doesn't — Show

**Documented:** Langworthy's February 4, 2026 press release and its project details; the Schumer-Gillibrand release of the same date listing the identical $5,000,000 for the identical project in the FY 2026 Consolidated Appropriations Bill; the August posts and the event signage; that Newstead is in NY-23 and in Erie County.

**Not documented, and not claimed:** that the $5 million is not real, that it was not enacted, that Langworthy did not secure it, or that he took credit for money he had no role in. He requested it, it passed, and the town is getting it. **Nor is it claimed** that announcing a project milestone is improper, that the "Senior Center" and "Community Center" labels conflict, or that the senators' offices behaved differently in their own release. No claim is made about anyone's intent.

---

## Verdict: MISSING CONTEXT

The $5 million is real, enacted, and headed to a project with a documented need. Langworthy helped secure it and is entitled to say so.

What his announcements omit is that New York's two senators secured the same $5 million for the same building, and said so publicly on the same day, and that the August event re-announced an award already made in February.
