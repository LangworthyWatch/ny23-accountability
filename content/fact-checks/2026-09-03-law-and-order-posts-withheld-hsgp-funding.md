## Why this matters

The State Homeland Security Program is the federal grant that helps pay for
planning, equipment, and training at county sheriffs' offices and police
departments in every county in New York — including all eight counties of
NY-23. For nearly a year, part of that money has been withheld by the Trump
administration, despite a presidential promise to restore it and a federal
court order that forced partial payment. In the last week of August,
Rep. Langworthy posted three times about supporting law enforcement. None of
the posts mentions the withheld money, and no statement from him about it
could be located anywhere.

## The Facts

### The expo

The Department of Homeland Security scheduled a two-day hiring expo for
September 1–2, 2026 at the Buffalo Convention Center. The venue canceled it
on August 27 after Erie County Executive Mark Poloncarz urged the convention
center to drop the event over U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement's
participation. Patrick Kaler, president and CEO of the convention center's
operator, said it had asked DHS to hold the expo **without ICE** — with all
other law enforcement agencies participating — before canceling. DHS moved
the event to U.S. Coast Guard Sector Eastern Great Lakes on Fuhrmann
Boulevard, where it ran September 1–2 and drew thousands of job seekers.

Two details in the Congressman's post don't match that record. The event is a
**DHS-wide career expo** — Coast Guard, CBP, TSA, Secret Service, FEMA and
others — not an "ICE job fair." And the venue's own account was that it
offered to host every other law enforcement agency, which is difficult to
square with a dispute about hating "law and order and the brave men and women
who enforce it." The objection, whatever one thinks of it, was to one agency.

### The money his posts don't mention

While those posts went up, this was the status of the federal grant money
that funds the sheriffs and police he was writing about:

| Date | What happened |
|---|---|
| 2025 (delayed NOFO) | DHS's FY2025 Homeland Security Grant Program funding notice cut New York's allocation by 40 percent, about $87 million |
| Sept 27, 2025 | At award issuance, DHS cut an additional $102.7 million without stated explanation — $187 million total, 86 percent of New York's funding. SHSP fell from $61,229,940 (FY2024) to $5,624,924, a 90.8 percent cut; UASI fell 85.9 percent |
| Sept 29, 2025 | Attorney General James and 11 other state attorneys general sued DHS in the U.S. District Court for the District of Rhode Island over the award-stage cut |
| Oct 3, 2025 | President Trump posted on Truth Social that he had "reversed the cuts made to Homeland Security and Counterterrorism for New York City and State" |
| Dec 2025 | The court granted the states summary judgment and ordered DHS to restore funding to committed levels; DHS appealed, then dropped the appeal |
| Sept 1, 2026 | Gov. Hochul's office: only about $100 million has been delivered — after the court order — leaving nearly $87 million still withheld. The state itemizes the two largest components as $63,278,152 in UASI funding and **$17,687,197 in SHSP funding** |

**In plain language:** the federal government cut New York's counterterrorism
and police-preparedness grants nearly to zero last fall, the President said
he had reversed the cuts, a court order later forced part of the money out,
and almost a year after the promise, roughly $87 million — including
$17.7 million from the program that funds county sheriffs and police
departments statewide — still has not arrived. The New York State
Comptroller's office notes that "all counties in New York State receive SHSP
funding." That includes Allegany, Cattaraugus, Chautauqua, Chemung, Erie,
Schuyler, Steuben, and Tioga.

### What his colleagues did, and what he said

When the cuts landed in the fall of 2025, other New York Republicans went
public. Rep. Malliotakis said she called President Trump directly. Rep.
Lawler said he "made clear that these cuts were unacceptable" from day one.
Rep. Garbarino, chairman of the House Homeland Security Committee, publicly
urged DHS to reverse course. There was no delegation letter — the appeals
were individual public statements.

As of September 3, 2026, no public statement from Rep. Langworthy on the
HSGP cuts, the withheld $87 million, or the partial restoration could be
located in his House press-release archive or on either of his Facebook
pages. His August 26 post says sheriffs "should be supported"; the search
found no instance of him addressing the federal grant money that supports
them.

### The vote that funds the hiring surge

The expo he did post about exists because of a law he voted for. H.R. 1, the
July 2025 reconciliation law, provided ICE roughly $75 billion over four
years, funding the recruitment drive the Buffalo expo served. Langworthy
voted Aye (Roll Call 190). That vote and the funding disparity can both be
true; the point is that the enforcement expansion he supported is fully
funded and publicly celebrated, while the local-police grant money is
withheld and unmentioned.

## The fair reading

- **"This critical... job fair will go on" was accurate.** The expo was
  relocated, not stopped, and it ran September 1–2 as he said it would.
- **The dispute he weighed in on is real.** The state Attorney General's
  guidance limiting local police cooperation with federal immigration
  enforcement is a genuine, contested policy question. Criticizing it is
  ordinary political speech, and this entry takes no position on it.
- **The withheld money is not his doing.** The cuts were made by DHS and
  FEMA, executive agencies; no House vote created them. A member of Congress
  does not control grant disbursement. What this entry documents is a
  messaging asymmetry — three posts on law and order in three days, none
  acknowledging the largest law-enforcement funding story in the state — not
  a vote or an action.
- **There was no letter he declined to sign.** The October 2025 appeals by
  his colleagues were individual. His absence from the public record is
  documented silence, not a refusal of a specific ask.
- **Credit for the partial restoration is contested.** Republican colleagues
  cite their appeals to the President; the state credits its lawsuit and the
  December court order, which preceded the $100 million payment. Both can
  have mattered.

## Update — September 4, 2026: A Fourth Post, and a More Accurate Description of the Expo

On September 2, 2026 at 1:10 PM, while the expo was running, the official
office page posted about it again:

> "Chalk one up for the good guys. The radical left could not stop the
> Department of Homeland Security Job Fair in WNY. Going on right now. Here's
> details if you're looking for noble work that pays well and protects the
> homeland."

The post links to the DHS expo page. Three things follow from it.

**This time he described the event accurately.** Question 3 below asks why the
August 28 official-page post called a DHS-wide career expo an "ICE job fair."
The September 2 post calls it "the Department of Homeland Security Job Fair,"
which matches the record. The question stands as to the earlier post, but the
later description is the correct one and this entry credits it.

**"Going on right now" was accurate.** The expo ran September 1–2 at U.S. Coast
Guard Sector Eastern Great Lakes, and the post went up midday on the second
day.

**The framing and the omission are unchanged.** "The radical left could not
stop" restates the characterization of the August 28 post on the official
office page. The convention center operator's account is also unchanged: it
said it had offered to host the expo with every other law enforcement agency
participating, and objected to one. And as of September 4, 2026, no public
statement from Rep. Langworthy on the withheld $87 million — including the
$17.7 million in SHSP funding that flows to sheriffs and police departments in
all eight NY-23 counties — could be located in his House press archive or on
either Facebook page.
