## Why this matters

On August 27 the New York Teamsters endorsed Rep. Langworthy, one of only four
Republicans among the state's 26 congressional districts. He posted that it was
"about action, not just words," and that the union had "recognized my record of
results."

The vote behind that endorsement is real, and it deserves to be said plainly
before anything else: he broke with his own conference to cast it. Only 20 of
the 212 Republicans voting supported the bill. That is not a free vote.

The context the post leaves out is that the Teamsters described the endorsement
as resting on that single vote, in as many words, and that his other recorded
labor votes went the other way.

---

## The claim

**Source:** Post on the campaign page "Nick Langworthy," August 27, 2026,
sharing WETM's story on the endorsement.

> "So proud to earn the endorsement of the Teamsters in my re-election. These
> are the hardworking men and women who make our country run and that's who I
> fight for every day in Washington. Good paying careers, strengthening the
> middle class, and standing up for workers will always be my top priorities
> but it's about action, not just words, and I'm honored that they have
> recognized my record of results."

## The action, and it is a real one

**H.R. 5408, the Faster Labor Contracts Act.** The bill amends the National
Labor Relations Act to require employers to begin bargaining shortly after a
union wins recognition, with mediation and then binding arbitration if no first
contract is reached. It is the Teamsters' marquee legislative priority.

On **June 9, 2026** the House passed it **230 to 193** (Roll Call 216).
**Langworthy voted Yea.**

| | Yea | Nay |
|---|---|---|
| Republicans | **20** | 192 |
| Democrats | 210 | 0 |

He was one of twenty Republicans out of 212 voting. Whatever else is in this
entry, that vote is the thing he was endorsed for, and he cast it against the
overwhelming weight of his own conference.

**One correction to the record now circulating:** he is a cosponsor of H.R.
5408, but he is **not** an original cosponsor. The govinfo BILLSTATUS record
lists his sponsorship date as **January 20, 2026**, with `isOriginalCosponsor`
recorded as false. September 16, 2025 is the date Rep. Donald Norcross (D-NJ-1)
introduced the bill, not the date Langworthy signed on. He joined roughly four
months after introduction, as one of 110 cosponsors.

---

## What the endorsement was actually for

The Teamsters were explicit. Their announcement stated that **every endorsed
candidate voted for the Faster Labor Contracts Act**, and Teamsters Joint
Council 16 President Thomas Gesualdi framed the whole slate around that one
bill:

> "It's an honor to support this bipartisan group of incumbents, because the
> Faster Labor Contracts Act is a game-changer, and it will empower workers
> more than any federal law that's been passed since the New Deal."

Twenty candidates were endorsed across New York, most of them Democrats,
including the House Minority Leader. Langworthy was one of four Republicans.

**In plain language:** the union named the criterion itself, and the criterion
was a single roll-call vote. "Record of results" describes a body of work. The
endorsers described a test with one question on it.

## The rest of the labor record

| Date | Measure | Langworthy | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| June 9, 2026 | H.R. 5408, Faster Labor Contracts Act (passed 230-193) | **Yea** | Roll Call 216 |
| Dec 11, 2025 | H.R. 2550, Protect America's Workforce Act, restoring collective bargaining for federal workers | **Nay** | Roll Call 332 |
| Jan 12, 2024 | H.J. Res. 98, disapproval of the NLRB joint-employer rule | **Not Voting** | Roll Call 010 |

**A second correction.** Research circulating on this topic records the January
2024 joint-employer measure as an anti-labor floor vote by Langworthy. The
House Clerk's roll call shows him as **Not Voting**. He did not vote on it. His
Rules Committee action on that resolution is a separate matter and is not a
floor vote; this entry does not characterize it as one.

**A third.** That same research states the bill reached the floor only after a
successful discharge petition. The official action list for H.R. 5408 shows it
was "considered under the provisions of rule H. Res. 1140," the ordinary path,
and contains no discharge petition entry. This entry does not repeat the
discharge claim.

## The money

Contributions below are from FEC bulk files for the 2022, 2024 and 2026 cycles,
counting only committee-to-candidate contributions to Langworthy for Congress,
excluding memo entries and refunds.

**The Teamsters have given him nothing.** Across all three cycles, no Teamsters
committee, including the union's national PAC, D.R.I.V.E., appears among his
contributors. The total is **$0**. That figure was confirmed against a control:
nineteen Teamsters-affiliated committees appear in the FEC committee master
file, they gave to 104 recipients in the 2026 cycle alone, and none of those
contributions went to Langworthy.

This cuts in his favor on the obvious question. The endorsement did not follow
money from the endorser.

**Labor money generally has grown, but from other unions.**

| Cycle | Labor PAC total |
|---|---|
| 2022 | $22,500 |
| 2024 | $85,500 |
| 2026 | $93,500 |
| **Total** | **$201,500** |

The donors are concentrated in aviation and the postal service: the Air Line
Pilots Association, Allied Pilots, Southwest Airlines Pilots, NetJets pilots,
the National Air Traffic Controllers Association, the letter carriers and
postal workers unions, the Operating Engineers, and the Carpenters, who also
endorsed him this month. Notably, the Independent Pilots Association, which
represents UPS pilots, gave $2,500 in February 2026.

**On the employer side of the same industries:** United Parcel Service's PAC
gave $8,000 across the three cycles, the American Trucking Associations'
Trucking PAC $3,000, and FedEx's PAC $2,000.

## What this entry does not establish

- **The vote was not bought, and nothing here suggests it was.** The union that
  endorsed him has given him no money at all.
- **His labor record is not uniformly hostile.** Labor PACs have given him
  $201,500 over three cycles and the figure has risen each cycle, which is not
  the profile of a member unions consider unreachable.
- **He does not sit on the labor committees.** He is on Energy and Commerce,
  Oversight, and Rules, not Education and Workforce or Transportation and
  Infrastructure, which limits the number of labor votes and hearings he is
  positioned to act on.
- **Three votes is a thin sample.** This entry documents the recorded floor
  votes it could verify, not a complete labor scorecard. Annual union scorecard
  ratings are cited secondhand elsewhere and are deliberately not used here.

---

## Questions this raises

1. The Teamsters said the endorsement rested on one vote. Does the Congressman
   consider the Faster Labor Contracts Act a template, and will he support the
   Senate version without weakening amendments?
2. He voted Nay on restoring collective bargaining for federal workers in
   December 2025 and Yea for private-sector first-contract arbitration six
   months later. What distinguishes the two for him?
3. He sits on the Rules Committee, which handled the rule that brought H.R.
   5408 to the floor. What was his position there?
4. Thousands of Teamsters freight, package and pension constituents live in and
   near NY-23. Has his office taken any action on the 2023 Yellow Corporation
   closure or on the New York State Teamsters Conference Pension Fund?
