## Why this matters

When a member of Congress stands at a company's ribbon-cutting and says he
"fought to bring home millions" for that company, the natural reading is that
he routed federal money to it. For a publicly traded defense contractor, that
route does not exist. House Appropriations rules bar for-profit companies from
receiving Community Project Funding, and Moog Inc. is publicly traded
(NYSE: MOG.A). Moog's federal money comes from competitive procurement, which
no House member awards.

What Langworthy does have on the defense record is real, is bipartisan, and is
narrower than the words on the post: a $7 million photonics amendment the House
adopted. This entry documents both halves, because the two posts he published
the same day describe them to two different standards.

---

## The claim

**Source:** Two posts on the verified congressional Facebook page
(facebook.com/RepLangworthy), August 14, 2026, from Moog Inc.'s 75th-anniversary
event and the opening of its Advanced Integrated Manufacturing (AIM) facility.

Post 1:

> "Proud to celebrate 75 years of Moog and the opening of its new Advanced
> Integrated Manufacturing facility. The future of aerospace, defense, and
> advanced manufacturing is being built right here in NY23. I've fought to
> bring home millions for Moog and other critical defense projects,
> strengthening our national security and creating good-paying jobs."

Post 2:

> "For 75 years, Moog Inc. has made WNY a hub for American innovation. Proud to
> fight for investments in trusted photonics, next-gen control systems and NASA
> Artemis' program. This helps foster an environment where Moog can grow and
> create skilled jobs in NY23."

The difference between those two sentences is the entire subject of this entry.
Post 2 claims advocacy for **programs**. Post 1 claims money brought home for a
**company**.

---

## What is accurate, and it is not trivial

**1. "Right here in NY23" is correct.** Moog's headquarters campus straddles the
Elma/East Aurora line in Erie County, and that part of Erie County falls inside
NY-23 — his own House biography describes the district as including "parts of
Erie." This site has previously confirmed the location (see
[Defense Suppliers Visit](/fact-checks/2026-03-08-defense-suppliers-visit/)).

**2. The "trusted photonics" advocacy is documented, bipartisan, and it passed
the House.** This is the strongest fact in his favor and it checks out at the
primary source.

On H.R. 4016, the FY2026 Department of Defense Appropriations Act, the House
Rules Committee's amendment table lists, as amendment 29:

> "Langworthy (NY), Kennedy (NY) — Bi-Partisan — Transfers $7 million to the
> Navy, RDTE in FY 26 to close a gap and establish a trusted process for
> Photonic Integrated Circuit production." — **Made in Order**

The amendment text itself (filed July 1, 2025) is a two-line account transfer:
"Page 9, line 19, after the dollar amount, insert '(reduced by $7,000,000)'.
Page 39, line 16, after the dollar amount, insert '(increased by $7,000,000)'."

It was then **adopted**. The Congressional Record Daily Digest for July 16, 2025
lists, under "Agreed to:", Calvert amendment en bloc No. 4, which includes
"Langworthy (No. 172) that transfers $7 million to the Navy, RDTE in FY 26 to
close a gap and establish a trusted process for Photonic Integrated Circuit
production." (It is the same amendment throughout: number 29 in the Rules
Committee's table of submissions, No. 172 as printed for floor consideration in
H. Rept. 119-199.)

The House then passed H.R. 4016 by 221-209, Roll No. 212 — a vote recorded at
12:45 a.m. on July 18, 2025, which the Congressional Record files under the
July 17 legislative day. Langworthy voted Yea.

His co-lead was **Rep. Tim Kennedy (D, NY-26)**, the neighboring Buffalo
Democrat. This was a bipartisan Western New York ask, and it succeeded on the
floor.

**In plain language:** when post 2 says he fought for "trusted photonics," there
is a specific, dated, primary-source-verifiable thing he is pointing at. That
part of the claim is supported.

**3. A second bipartisan defense amendment.** On the same bill he was a
cosponsor of amendment 103 (Trahan, Hudson, Langworthy, Keating, Kennedy),
increasing Army RDT&E by $5.0 million for operational evaluation of tactical
throwable camera systems. Also made in order.

**4. His aviation-safety record is documented and externally recognized.** On
August 4, 2026, the Air Line Pilots Association reported:

> "the 2026 ALPA Pilot Partisan of the Year Award was presented to Rep. Nick
> Langworthy (R-NY), who was recognized for his work championing ADS-B In
> requirements, leading to two letters supporting Rotorcraft Operations
> Transparency and Oversight Reform Act provisions. He also created and leads
> the Aviation Safety Caucus and has supported the Association's pilot-partisan
> agenda since coming to Congress."

His own House biography confirms he chairs the Aviation Safety Caucus and was a
leader on the FAA Reauthorization Act of 2024 following the Colgan Air Flight
3407 tragedy. This is aviation safety, not Moog procurement, and it does not
support the Moog claim. It is included because it is the clearest documented
example of this member doing sustained, effective advocacy, and any honest
assessment of "does he actually fight for things" has to account for it.

## What the record does not show

**1. There is no earmark to Moog, and there could not be.** The House
Appropriations Committee's Community Project Funding guidance for the current
cycle is explicit:

> "**Ban on For-Profit Recipients:** Project funding may not be directed to
> for-profit recipients. Members may request funding for state, local, or tribal
> governmental grantees and certain eligible non-profits, as allowed under
> federal law and subcommittee guidance."

Moog is publicly traded. "Bring home millions for Moog," read as directed
federal money, describes a transaction the rules do not permit. (Eligibility
beyond the for-profit ban varies by subcommittee; the for-profit bar itself does
not.)

**2. His own published request lists contain no defense contractor, no company
photonics line item, and no Artemis line.** Committee guidance requires that
"Members ... post every project funding request online, in a searchable format,
with their final submission to the Committee," which makes these lists
checkable. Across four cycles on langworthy.house.gov:

| Cycle | Published project requests | Defense/aerospace company recipients |
|---|---|---|
| FY24 (Community Project Funding) | 15 | 0 |
| FY25 | 15 | 0 |
| FY26 | 15 | 0 |
| FY27 | 20 | 0 |

Every proposed recipient across all four cycles is a unit of local government, a
public authority or special district, a school, a hospital, a nonprofit, a
tribal government, or the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers. Water lines, sewer
extensions, radios, boilers, snow equipment, an MRI. **None is a defense or
aerospace company.** The closest thing to "photonics" anywhere on the four lists
is the FY27 **Corning Community College Optical Systems Technology Project** — a
community-college workforce project in Steuben County, not a line item for any
company.

**The fair caveat:** these pages cover **project** requests, the category
members are required to publish. His office has separately published a Fiscal
Year 2025 programmatic appropriations request form, and no list of programmatic
requests appears anywhere on his site. A defense programmatic request could
exist and be invisible. That is a limit on what this entry can see, and it is
stated rather than papered over.

**3. Moog's federal money is competitively awarded procurement.** The eight
largest USASpending contract awards to Moog entities with activity since 2023
are, by value: $43.3M, $38.2M, $29.9M, $24.9M, $24.2M, $19.6M, $18.9M
(Department of Defense, mostly MOOG MILITARY AIRCRAFT LLC) and $17.3M (NASA).
Several of these awards began before 2023 and remain active. These are bids won
by a contractor,
evaluated by contracting officers. No House member selects the winner. **Nothing
about this is improper.** A district employer winning defense work on the merits
is ordinary, lawful, and good for NY-23. It simply is not something a
congressman brings home.

**4. The photonics amendment names no recipient.** Its operative text is a
dollar transfer between two accounts. Nothing in the amendment, the Rules table,
or the Daily Digest entry names Moog or any company. Moog does have a defense
fiber-optics business — on July 7, 2025 it announced the acquisition of
COTSWORKS Inc., "an aerospace and defense fiber optics transceiver component
manufacturer, for a purchase price of $63 million," a company founded in 2006
and headquartered in Ohio — but adjacency is not documentation, and this entry
does not assert that the $7 million was for Moog.

**5. Whether the $7 million ever became law is unresolved.** H.R. 4016 was not
the enacted vehicle. FY2026 defense funding was enacted February 3, 2026 as
Division A of the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026 (P.L. 119-75). The word
"photonic" does not appear in the enacted text; program-level detail lives in
the explanatory statement referenced by §4 of that Act, which this entry was
unable to locate. So the honest statement is: **the House adopted his amendment;
this entry cannot confirm the $7 million survived into enacted law.** That is an
open question, flagged as such, not a finding against him.

**6. This entry did not locate any Artemis-specific or "next-gen control
systems"-specific action.** Searches of his House press releases, the House
Rules Committee amendment tables for H.R. 4016, H.R. 3838 and S. 1071 (the
FY2026 NDAA), and general web searches surfaced no letter, amendment, report
language request, or floor statement from Langworthy on NASA Artemis or on
control-systems accounts. **Absence of a located record is not proof of
absence** — much congressional advocacy (letters to appropriators, programmatic
requests, member-to-member asks) never becomes a public document. His office is
in the best position to produce it, and this site will publish it if provided.

Worth noting on leverage: he sits on **Rules, Energy and Commerce, and
Oversight** — not Appropriations, not Armed Services, not Science, Space and
Technology. His available tools on defense and NASA funding are floor
amendments, letters, and requests. He used the floor-amendment tool on
photonics, successfully.

## No federal money is attached to the facility he was celebrating

The AIM facility whose opening prompted post 1 is, in Moog's own description,
"the new 150,000-square-foot manufacturing center, a $150 million investment" —
that is, Moog's own capital.

Moog's East Aurora campus has drawn public subsidy, and all of it is state or
local:

- **Empire State Development:** a $16.4 million Excelsior Jobs Program package
  **in 2023**, which the state ties to "a $42 million expansion and the addition
  of more than 500 high-tech engineering, machining, and aerospace production
  jobs" — a separate, earlier project, not the AIM building
- **New York Power Authority:** 8,000 kilowatts of low-cost power through
  ReCharge NY and Western NY Hydropower
- **Erie County IDA:** nearly $3 million in tax breaks ($2 million sales tax
  abatements, roughly $901,000 in property tax reductions)

The state's announcement does not say that any of these incentives paid for the
AIM facility, and this entry does not claim they did.

What it does establish is an absence. Governor Hochul's August 14, 2026
announcement of the very event Langworthy was posting from contains **no mention
of federal funding and no mention of any member of Congress** — the words
"federal," "Congress," "Langworthy," "Schumer," "Gillibrand," and "Kennedy" do
not appear anywhere in it. The officials quoted are the Governor, Empire State
Development, the New York Power Authority, the Erie County IDA, and Moog's CEO.

**In plain language:** nothing in the public record of that ribbon-cutting is
federal. Placing "I've fought to bring home millions for Moog" in the caption of
that event invites readers to connect his advocacy to the building behind him.
They are not connected.

## The pattern this sits in

Announcing competitively awarded defense work as a local win is a recurring
construction. On July 25, 2025, his office issued a release headlined
"Congressman Langworthy Announces $18.8 Million Defense Contract for Lockheed
Martin Owego," reporting that Lockheed's Owego facility "has been awarded" a
Navy contract for 25 Digital Magnetic Anomaly Detection hardware kits, with
Langworthy quoted calling it "a tremendous win for Lockheed Martin Owego."

That release is careful in one respect — it says the facility *was awarded* the
contract, not that he secured it. The Moog post is less careful: it moves from
announcing to claiming.

**And his own on-the-record quote that day was more modest than the post.** In
Moog's own 75th-anniversary announcement, Langworthy is quoted saying:

> "Moog is one of the great Western New York success stories, who for the last
> 75 years, has been pushing the boundaries of innovation while creating
> excellent paying careers and strengthening our manufacturing base."

That statement makes no funding claim at all. The credit-claiming sentence
appears only in the Facebook caption.

This is the same structural point this site documented on the Chautauqua
children's mental-health grant: the recipient won it by competing, and a member
does not pick the winner (see
[The $1M Chautauqua Mental-Health Grant](/fact-checks/2026-07-17-chautauqua-mental-health-grant-credit/)).

## Questions This Raises

1. What specific federal dollars does "I've fought to bring home millions for
   Moog" refer to? Community Project Funding cannot go to a for-profit, and
   competitive contracts are not member-directed.
2. Did the $7 million Photonic Integrated Circuit transfer survive into the
   enacted FY2026 explanatory statement, or did it end with House passage of a
   bill that was not the final vehicle?
3. Was the photonics amendment connected to Moog, to another Western New York
   entity, or to neither? The amendment text names no recipient.
4. What documented action supports "NASA Artemis" and "next-gen control
   systems"? A letter, a programmatic request, or report language would settle
   it.
5. Has his office submitted programmatic appropriations requests on defense or
   NASA accounts, and would it publish them as it publishes project requests?

---

## Bottom line

Post 2 is the more defensible of the two. One of its three claims is verified at
the primary source: he did fight for trusted photonics, through a bipartisan $7
million Navy RDT&E amendment with Rep. Tim Kennedy — the amendment adopted en
bloc on July 16, 2025, the bill passed 221-209 two days later. The other two — next-gen control systems and NASA
Artemis — are unverified rather than contradicted. And the phrasing "helps
foster an environment where Moog can grow" describes what a member can actually
do.

Post 1 is the problem. "I've fought to bring home millions for Moog," delivered
at Moog's ribbon-cutting, describes something structurally impossible: House
Appropriations guidance bars project funding to for-profit recipients, his four
published request lists contain no defense contractor and no company photonics
or Artemis line, and Moog's federal money is competitively bid work no member
awards. The building being opened was Moog's own $150 million investment, and
the state's announcement of that event mentions no federal funding at all.

The missing context is the difference between advocating for a **program
account** and delivering money to a **company** — a distinction his own second
post gets right, and his first post erases.
