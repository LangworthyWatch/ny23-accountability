## Why this matters

A committee seat is the reason he gives for investigating a project outside
his district. On September 10 he posted that he takes "the 'Oversight' in
serving on the House Oversight Committee seriously," and in the attached
radio interview he explained why he, and not a Buffalo official, is the one
asking a federal inspector general to look at the North Aud Block: "I am a
member of the House Oversight Committee. I have oversight over the entire
federal government in that respect." The committee keeps a public record of
who shows up for its work. This entry reads that record: every hearing
roster the committee has published this Congress and every one of the 161
recorded-vote sheets it has posted. It is the first time this site has
compiled it.

task forces, so the full committee's hearings and markups are the whole of

He voted on all 8 roll calls at the January 14, 2025 organizational meeting.
Of the 7 recorded votes taken during hearings (motions to subpoena Elon Musk,
Kristi Noem, Pamela Bondi and others, or to table those motions), he voted
on 4.

**The April 30, 2025 markup.** This was the committee's vote on its piece of
the budget reconciliation bill that became the One Big Beautiful Bill Act
(H.R. 1, Public Law 119-21). Democrats offered 26 amendments; each was
defeated 20 to 23 or by a similar margin. The final roll call, "Transmitting
Reconciliation Committee Print to the House Budget Committee," carried
**22 to 21**. His row is blank on all 28 sheets. Two months later he voted for
the bill on the House floor (Roll Call 190, July 3, 2025).

**In plain language:** in a Congress and a half, the committee has taken 145
recorded votes in markups. He was not in the room for 69 of them, including
every vote at three full markups, one of which sent the committee's share of
the Republican budget bill forward by a single vote. He attended 7 of the 12
hearings the committee has published transcripts for, and he was at the six
best-attended ones.

for hours; the April 30 markup took 28 roll calls, and nothing in the record
shows whether he returned after the Energy hearing ended.

## The fair reading

- **Members double-book.** Every member serves on more than one committee,
  and same-hour conflicts between Energy and Commerce and Oversight are
  documented above for most of these dates. Choosing the committee where his
  district's energy and health work happens is a defensible choice.
- **His hearing attendance is average-to-good where it counts.** He was
  present at the six best-attended hearings (44 to 46 of 47 members), the
  ones with the sanctuary mayors and governors and the Minnesota fraud
  inquiry. The hearings he missed drew 33 to 42 members, and Member Day
  drew four.
- **A blank row is not a position.** On the amendment votes he missed, his
  party prevailed each time without him; his absence changed no outcome
  except that the 22 to 21 transmittal vote would have been 23 to 21.
- **The claim is about seriousness, not attendance**, and seriousness is not
  measurable. What is measurable is that the committee he invokes as the
  basis for his North Aud request is the one whose recorded votes he has
  missed most often.

## Questions this raises

1. On April 30, 2025, the Oversight Committee voted 22 to 21 to send its
   portion of the budget reconciliation bill to the Budget Committee. He was
   not recorded on that vote or on any of the 27 others that day. Where was
   he after the Energy Subcommittee hearing ended?
2. He told WBEN the tax credits "are for everyone and for every district"
   and that the Southern Tier has "just as big of a housing need." Has he
   asked HUD, the inspector general, or the state about any housing project
   or credit allocation in NY-23?
3. Does he consider the committee's markups, where its bills and subpoenas
   are decided, part of taking Oversight seriously?
