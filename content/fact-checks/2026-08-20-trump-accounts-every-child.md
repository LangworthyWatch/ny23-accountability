## Why this matters

Trump Accounts are real, they went live on July 4, 2026, and the Congressman
voted for the law that created them. A family in NY-23 reading his post would
reasonably conclude two things: that every child can get one, and that every
child gets the "jump start." The first is nearly true. The second is true for
roughly one in five of the district's children by the end of 2028, and for
about one in fourteen today.

The post links to trumpaccounts.gov, whose pitch is the $1,000 federal deposit.
That deposit has a birth-date window, a citizenship test, and a paperwork
requirement the statute spells out. The post mentions none of them.

## What the law says

Trump Accounts were created by Section 70204 of P.L. 119-21 (H.R. 1, the One
Big Beautiful Bill Act), which Langworthy voted for on July 3, 2025 (Roll Call
190). The section creates two separate things.

**The account** (new IRC §530A). Open to any child who has not turned 18 by
the end of the year and who has a work-eligible Social Security number. No
citizenship requirement. Contributions are after-tax and capped at $5,000 a
year; employers may add up to $2,500 inside that cap. Money must sit in
low-fee U.S. stock index funds and cannot be withdrawn before the year the
child turns 18. It is a traditional IRA: earnings, and any government or
employer deposits, are taxed as ordinary income when withdrawn. There is no
capital-gains or tax-free treatment, which distinguishes it from a 529 plan.

**The $1,000 pilot contribution** (new IRC §6434). A one-time Treasury deposit,
available only to a child who is (1) born after December 31, 2024 and before
January 1, 2029, (2) a U.S. citizen, and (3) listed with a work-eligible SSN on
an election (Form 4547) filed by the adult who claims the child as a dependent.

**In plain language:** almost every child can have the account. Only citizen
children born in the four years 2025 through 2028, whose parent files the
form, get the $1,000. A child born in 2024 gets an account and no deposit. A
child born in 2029 will get an account and no deposit.

The window estimate multiplies 2023 births by four; births in these counties

**In plain language:** when all four birth-year cohorts exist at the end of
2028, roughly 18,000 of the district's roughly 100,000 minors will have been
born in the window. On the day of the post, only the 2025 cohort and part of
2026 existed: about 7,000 children, or about 7% of the district's minors.

## "Born into a wealthy family"

The post frames the program as a leveler. Independent analyses of the statute
point the other way on the part that scales:

- The $1,000 is the same for every eligible child. Everything above it depends
  on what a family can deposit, up to $5,000 a year. FactCheck.org's July 2026
  review worked the arithmetic: the $1,000 alone grows to roughly $5,800 at 18
  using the 10.3% historical return trumpaccounts.gov advertises; a family
  that deposits the $5,000 maximum every year reaches roughly $304,000. Using
  a 7% assumption, the $1,000 alone is about $3,400 to $3,500.
- Urban Institute (January 2026) modeled the opt-in design and found that
  about 5% of families with children do not file a tax return, concentrated at
  the bottom of the income distribution; its model left about 7% of
  bottom-quintile children without accounts versus about 1% in the top two
  quintiles.
- Urban's NORC survey (March–April 2026) found 60% of adults had heard of the
  accounts, with awareness significantly higher among higher-income and
  higher-savings households; about 39% of eligible children were enrolled.
- Because earnings and the federal deposit are taxed as ordinary income on
  withdrawal, a low-income family's child pays the same rate structure as any
  other IRA holder, with none of the tax-free growth a 529 offers.

None of this makes the account worthless. Brookings noted it could reach some
poor families the Child Tax Credit does not. It does mean the "wealthy family"
framing is inverted for the part of the program that produces large balances.

## The $250 that is not the federal program

Some NY-23 families will see a $250 deposit for children born before 2025.
That money comes from a private pledge by Michael and Susan Dell, announced
December 2, 2025: $250 each for up to 25 million children aged 10 and under,
born before January 1, 2025, living in ZIP codes with median household income
under $150,000. It is not part of P.L. 119-21, was not voted on by Congress,
and is capped at the first 25 million activated accounts. It happens to cover
the cohort the federal $1,000 excludes.

## Enrollment so far

IRS reported on March 31, 2026 that taxpayers had signed up more than 4
million children, of whom more than 1 million were covered by $1,000 elections.
Treasury said more than 6 million by late June and about 7 million by late
July 2026. Contributions opened July 4, 2026.

## Questions this raises

1. The post says "every American child." The $1,000 reaches citizen children
   born 2025–2028 whose parent files Form 4547. Will the Congressman's office
   tell constituents with children born in 2024 or earlier that they are not
   eligible for the deposit?
2. Urban found enrollment lowest among the families "who stand to gain most."
   Has the office done any outreach in NY-23 counties on the filing
   requirement?
3. The account's earnings are taxed as ordinary income, unlike a 529. Has the
   Congressman explained that tradeoff to constituents choosing between the two?
4. This review found no Langworthy press release or local interview on Trump
   Accounts before this post. Was the program a priority when he voted for H.R.
   1, or is this the first time his office has raised it?
