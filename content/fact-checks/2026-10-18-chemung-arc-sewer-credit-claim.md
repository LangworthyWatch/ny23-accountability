## Why this matters

Chemung County is getting $1.4 million from the Appalachian Regional
Commission (ARC) to rebuild sewers at the Southern Tier Logistics Business
Park in the Village of Horseheads. With $1.4 million in state money, the
$2.8 million project would add about 4,500 feet of sewer main and a new
pump station, helping prepare a former military depot site for
manufacturing, warehousing and logistics, according to his release.

That is good news for the county. The question is the verb. Rep.
Langworthy told constituents he was "proud to secure" the money. ARC
grants in New York are applied for at the state level and reviewed by ARC,
not awarded by members of Congress. Sen. Chuck Schumer, who announced the
same grant the same day, says the money comes from a 2021 law, passed
before Rep. Langworthy took office. His own press release describes no
role for him in winning it.

## How ARC grants are awarded in New York

**ARC grants start with the state, not Congress.** ARC is a federal-state
partnership. Its own guidance says: "All ARC Area Development grant
proposals originate at the state level in consultation with the ARC state
program manager." For Chemung County, the local partner is the Southern
Tier Central Regional Planning and Development Board. Its published
process:

- Applicants work with the board; pre-applications are rated by its review
  committee and approved by its board.
- The New York State Department of State reviews them, and "the Secretary
  of State will approve the projects for additional review by ARC."
- ARC then does its own review and decides funding.

**This grant may have taken a different track.** The board's 2027
presentation also says "The maximum grant level allowed by New York State
is $500,000 through this program," and that "Larger awards are being
allowed under POWER, WORC, INSPIRE, and the ARISE funding 'pools'." At
$1.4 million, this award is nearly three times that cap, so it may have
come from one of those separate ARC programs. We could not confirm which
track or process applied. In every ARC track, ARC itself decides funding.

**Members of Congress can write support letters.** The board's 2027
application presentation says: "Support Letters are good, and nice to have
(particularly from your congressperson and your senators)." That is the
only role for a member that the board's materials describe. His release
does not say he wrote one, and we found no record that he did; support
letters are usually not made public.

**Sen. Schumer says the money comes from a 2021 law.** His same-day
release describes the grant as "$1,400,000 through the Appalachian
Regional Commission (ARC) for Chemung County's Sewer Improvement for
Economic Growth project" and quotes him: "I'm proud to deliver $1.4
million from my Bipartisan Infrastructure & Jobs Law." It adds that "In
2021, Schumer negotiated and secured $1 billion for the Appalachian
Regional Commission (ARC)" in that law. We did not find an ARC record
confirming the funding source. House records show Rep. Langworthy took
his seat in January 2023.

**It is not an earmark.** His Community Project Funding request lists for
fiscal years 2024 through 2027 include no Chemung County sewer, Horseheads
or Southern Tier Logistics project.

**Sen. Schumer announced it too.** His release, also dated October 15,
is headlined "Schumer Delivers $1.4 Million to Improve Wastewater
Infrastructure in Chemung County." Neither release has a timestamp, so we
cannot say who announced first.

What we could not find: an ARC award notice for this grant. ARC's posted
project lists stop at fiscal 2025; that list includes the county's earlier
Chemung Canal Connector grant but not this one. We also could not find the
program track the money came from,
or the source of the $1.4 million state match.

In plain language: the county won a grant that ARC reviewed and funded,
from money Sen. Schumer says Congress approved before Rep. Langworthy was
in office. He announced it. The record shows no part he played in getting
it, which is what "secure" claims.

## The fair reading

- **Announcing grants is normal, and useful.** Members routinely tell
  constituents about federal money coming to their district.
- **He may have helped.** The planning board invites support letters from
  members of Congress. If his office wrote one, that is a real, if small,
  role, and this entry will say so.
- **His release is more careful than his post.** The release says he
  "announced" an ARC award; the post says he secured the money and does not
  mention ARC.
- **Sen. Schumer claims credit too.** "I'm proud to deliver" is the same
  kind of claim, though Schumer can point to ARC funding he says he
  negotiated in the 2021 law.
- **This is a pattern the site has documented before** with ARC grants in
  [Steuben](/fact-checks/2026-02-08-steuben-credit-claiming-pattern/) and
  [Allegany](/fact-checks/2026-02-08-allegany-county-grants-accord/)
  counties and an earlier Chemung County grant (see the
  [earmarks entry](/fact-checks/2026-07-09-earmark-distribution-vs-need/)).
