## Why this matters for NY-23

This is a baseline. It sets down, from public records, what the district has
for health care and food, what it has lost recently, and what independent
analysts say is at risk under the Medicaid reductions in the law
Rep. Langworthy voted for on July 3, 2025. It makes no new claim about him.
The claims it measures against are the ones already documented on this site:
that the district's rural hospitals "aren't going anywhere," and that the
federal rural health fund "delivers on that promise."

One clarifier up front, the same one the county profiles carry: counties
administer these systems; the federal government funds a large share of
them. Losses described here have different causes, and each is dated so a
reader can tell which is which. Two Rite Aid bankruptcies closed pharmacies.
Hospital finances turn on Medicaid. Food insecurity turns on wages, prices,
and benefit rules. Only the forward-looking material, the at-risk analyses,
is explicitly modeled on the federal cuts.

## A. Care: ten hospitals, eight on the at-risk list

The Fiscal Policy Institute's June 27, 2025 report lists every New York
hospital that draws more than 25 percent of its net patient revenue from
Medicaid and government appropriations, the threshold at which it judges
closure risk "in the case of severe cuts to Medicaid." Its headline finding:
"8 hospitals at risk of closure, the most of any district in the state, are
in New York's 23rd district."

| Hospital | City | Employees | Medicaid + government appropriations, share of net patient revenue (2023) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brooks-TLC Hospital System | Dunkirk | 290 | 97% |
| Bertrand Chaffee Hospital | Springville | 280 | 43% |
| Arnot Ogden Medical Center | Elmira | 2,200 | 42% |
| Cuba Memorial Hospital | Cuba | 100 | 42% |
| St. James Hospital | Hornell | 280 | 35% |
| Upper Allegheny Health System (Olean General) | Olean | 710 | 29% |
| Westfield Memorial Hospital | Westfield | 70 | 28% |
| UPMC Chautauqua at WCA | Jamestown | 760 | 28% |
| Jones Memorial Hospital (below threshold) | Wellsville | 360 | 24% |
| Ira Davenport Memorial Hospital (below threshold) | Bath | 190 | 22% |

*Source: FPI, "New York Hospitals will Close Under 'One Big Beautiful Bill
Act'," June 27, 2025, hospital table; retained in research/sources.*

**What has already closed.** The New York State Nurses Association's list of
hospital closures since 2000 contains two in the district: Salamanca Hospital
(2000) and Lakeshore Hospital in Silver Creek (2020). (Our Lady of Victory in
Lackawanna, 1999, is in Erie County but outside the district.)

**What closure would mean.** The Cornell Program on Applied Demographics
defines an "emergency care desert" as living 30 minutes or more from a
hospital that provides emergency care, and finds that if the hospitals on
FPI's at-risk list closed, New York's emergency-care-desert population "would
almost double (+182%; 708,633 people)," with upstate "disproportionately
impacted." Cornell did not publish a district-level figure, and this entry
does not estimate one.

**In plain language:** two of the district's hospitals have closed in this
century. Eight of the ten that remain on FPI's table depend on Medicaid and
state support for more than a quarter of their revenue, one of them for
nearly all of it. An analyst at Cornell has modeled what happens to
emergency access if that list closes; the answer for upstate is measured in
hundreds of thousands of people.

## B. Pharmacies: 102 open, 28 closed since 2023, six places with none left

The New York State Education Department registers every pharmacy in the
state. Its registry, pulled in full on September 8, 2026, records each
establishment's status and, for closed ones, the date its registration
ended. Counting only community pharmacies (excluding hospital, nursing-home,
infusion and clinic pharmacies by name), and only the parts of Erie and
Niagara counties inside the district (by geocoding):

| County | Active community pharmacies | Community pharmacies closed since Jan. 2023 | Of which Rite Aid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Allegany | 6 | 1 | 0 |
| Cattaraugus | 12 | 4 | 4 |
| Chautauqua | 18 | 6 | 6 |
| Chemung | 11 | 4 | 4 |
| Erie (NY-23 portion) | 34 | 11 | 9 |
| Niagara (NY-23 portion) | 4 | 0 | 0 |
| Schuyler | 3 | 0 | 0 |
| Steuben | 11 | 2 | 2 |
| Tioga | 3 | 0 | 0 |
| **District** | **102** | **28** | **25** |

The closures are not evenly spread over time: one in 2023, three in 2024,
and 24 in 2025. Twenty-two of the 2025 closures are Rite Aid registrations
ending between June 6 and October 23, 2025, as the chain closed its stores
under its second Chapter 11 filing. The other two 2025 closures are
independents: The Apothecary at Cottrill's in Orchard Park (February) and
Alfred Pharmacy in Alfred (December).

**Where the last one closed.** In six ZIP codes, a closure since 2023 left no
active community pharmacy registered in that ZIP:

| Place | County | Closure | What remains in the ZIP |
|---|---|---|---|
| Salamanca | Cattaraugus | Rite Aid, June 6, 2025 | Lionel R. John Health Center pharmacy (Seneca Nation clinic) |
| Eden | Erie | Rite Aid, July 29, 2025 | none registered |
| Silver Creek | Chautauqua | Rite Aid, Aug. 26, 2025 | none registered |
| Mayville (county seat) | Chautauqua | Rite Aid, Sept. 4, 2025 | none registered |
| Lancaster | Erie | Rite Aid, Oct. 23, 2025 | none registered (adjacent Depew ZIP has several) |
| Alfred | Allegany | Alfred Pharmacy, Dec. 12, 2025 | none registered |

A ZIP with no pharmacy is a proxy, not a drive-time measurement; Lancaster
in particular borders ZIPs with several. The rural cases are the ones that
matter: Mayville is the Chautauqua County seat, Alfred is a university town,
and Silver Creek lost its hospital in 2020 and its pharmacy in 2025.

**In plain language:** the district lost about one pharmacy in five over
three years, almost all of it in one summer, and almost all of it one
company's bankruptcy. That is a corporate event, not a federal policy event,
and this entry does not attribute it to any vote. What the federal record
bears on is what replaces it: the district's remaining independents,
hospitals, and clinics are the ones whose finances the at-risk analyses
describe.

*Source: NYSED Office of the Professions pharmacy-establishment registry via
its public verification API, pulled Sept. 8, 2026; method, definitions,
every record, and eleven unresolved addresses disclosed in
research/data/pharmacies-2026-09-08/README.md.*

## C. Food: one in seven, in every county

Feeding America's Map the Meal Gap (2025 release, 2023 data) puts the
overall food-insecurity rate in every NY-23 county between one in eight and
one in six:

| County | Food insecurity rate | Average meal cost |
|---|---|---|
| Chautauqua | 15.6% | $3.68 |
| Cattaraugus | 15.4% | $3.54 |
| Allegany | 14.7% | $3.75 |
| Chemung | 14.6% | $3.65 |
| Schuyler | 14.0% | $3.65 |
| Steuben | 13.8% | $3.75 |
| Erie | 13.4% | $3.96 |
| Tioga | 12.7% | $3.79 |

**In plain language:** the spread across the district is under three points.
The map's shading, which puts Cattaraugus and Allegany in a darker band, is
an artifact of a 13.5 percent bin boundary, not a different order of
hardship. The SNAP changes in the same July 2025 law, and what they mean for
these counties, are documented in the [SNAP rural impact summary](/fact-checks/2026-02-09-snap-rural-impact-summary/).

## The representation gap

Everything in this section is already documented in its own entry; this
profile only lines it up against the baseline above.

- **The vote.** Roll Call 190, July 3, 2025: Yea on H.R. 1, which cuts
  federal Medicaid spending in rural areas by roughly $137 billion over ten
  years nationally and creates the $50 billion rural health fund that KFF
  finds offsets about 37 percent of it, with 64 percent of the cuts landing
  after the fund expires. [What Medicaid cuts mean for NY-23 healthcare](/fact-checks/2026-02-09-medicaid-rural-impact-summary/).
- **"Pure fiction."** His October 2025 dismissal of closure warnings, against
  the state's own at-risk count. [Rural hospitals: calling closure warnings 'pure fiction'](/fact-checks/2025-10-rural-hospitals-pure-fiction/).
- **"These hospitals aren't going anywhere."** June 2026 town hall: "every
  rural hospital that I've met is not on the verge of closure no matter what
  people are trying to sell you." Eight of them are on the list above.
  [At his June town hall, he contradicted it himself three minutes later](/fact-checks/2026-07-02-town-hall-hospitals-not-going-anywhere/).
- **The fund, announced three times.** The $212 million first-year award,
  announced December 30, again July 15, and as "$76 million" on September 4,
  with no NY-23 facility named. [The December announcement and what it left out](/fact-checks/2026-06-02-rural-health-transformation-212m/), [the Centralus repeat](/fact-checks/2026-07-16-rural-health-fund-centralus-repeat/).
- **Schuyler's hospital.** On the federal at-risk list; he voted for the bill
  behind it twice. [Schuyler County's hospital is on a federal at-risk list](/fact-checks/2026-06-14-schuyler-county-hospital-at-risk/).
- **Nursing homes.** Three Chautauqua County closures and the staffing floor
  his vote blocked. [Three nursing home closures in Chautauqua County](/fact-checks/2026-06-02-chautauqua-snf-cluster/).
- **Pharmacies.** His stated approach is the CHECK Act's pharmacy-benefit
  disclosure provisions; the bill has no cosponsors and no committee action.
  [A price tag is not a price cut](/fact-checks/2026-08-11-check-act-price-tags-transparency/).

## The fair reading

Rural hospital and pharmacy loss is national and decades old; none of it
began with this Congress, and the two Rite Aid bankruptcies would have
closed those 25 stores under any representative. The Rural Health
Transformation money is real, New York's first-year award is the twelfth
largest in the country, and NY-23 organizations have applied for it. He has
said publicly that he wants the district's hospitals to survive, and there
is no record of him wanting otherwise. The documented gap is narrower than
the losses above and sharper: he voted for the law the at-risk analyses are
modeled on, he has told constituents the hospitals on that list are not at
risk, and he has announced the offset fund's money three times without a
facility to show for it.

## Questions this raises

1. Eight NY-23 hospitals are on FPI's at-risk table, one drawing 97 percent
   of its revenue from Medicaid and public appropriations. Which of them has
   his office asked the state to prioritize for Rural Health Transformation
   funds, and has any been told it will receive money?
2. Mayville, Silver Creek, Eden, and Alfred now have no registered community
   pharmacy. Has his office been contacted by any of those communities, and
   what federal tools does he believe apply?
3. His June statement was that every rural hospital he has met "is not on the
   verge of closure." Does that statement stand for Brooks-TLC, Bertrand
   Chaffee, Cuba Memorial, and Westfield Memorial specifically?
4. Cornell's model says closing the at-risk list would nearly double New
   York's emergency-care-desert population. Has his office requested a
   district-level estimate from CMS, the state, or the authors?
