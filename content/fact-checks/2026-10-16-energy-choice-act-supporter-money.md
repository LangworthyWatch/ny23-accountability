## Why this matters

The Energy Choice Act is a bill Rep. Langworthy has promoted repeatedly
this month. "My Energy Choice Act protects that freedom," his campaign page
posted on October 8. A fuel-dealer trade association, the
National Energy & Fuels Institute (NEFI), says it "helped draft" the bill.

Campaign finance records show that NEFI's political action committee has
given more to Rep. Langworthy this cycle than to any other candidate, and
that six of the national groups his own office named as the bill's
supporters have given his committees $54,000. One set, the home
builders' $10,000, arrived between the bill's two committee votes.

None of this is illegal, and it does not show that any vote was bought.
Voters weighing the bill, and the posts about it, can see who backs it
with money as well as with letters.

---

## The bill

Rep. Langworthy introduced H.R. 3699, the Energy Choice Act, on June 4,
2025. It would bar state and local governments from prohibiting or
limiting connections to an energy service based on the type or source of
energy, which would block bans on new natural gas or propane hookups.
The site's [February entry](/fact-checks/2026-02-25-energy-choice-act/)
covers what it does.

His June 5, 2025 press release introducing it states that the bill "has
received wide support from federal organizations" and lists 17 of them:

> American Exploration and Production Council (AXPC), American Gas
> Association (AGA), American Public Gas Association (APGA), Americans for
> Prosperity (AFP), Consumer Energy Alliance (CEA), Energy Marketers of
> America (EMA), GPA Midstream Association, GPSA Midstream Suppliers,
> Hearth, Patio & Barbecue Association (HPBA), National Association of Home
> Builders (NAHB), National Association of Oil and Energy Service
> Professionals (OESP), National Energy and Fuels Institute (NEFI),
> National Propane Gas Association (NPGA), Plumbing Heating Cooling
> Contractors – National Association (PHCC), Pool & Hot Tub Alliance
> (PHTA), LIBRE Initiative, Concerned Veterans for America (CVA).

NEFI's own account goes further. A NEFI news post dated September 16,
2025 describes the Energy Choice Act as the bill "which NEFI helped draft and has led a
national coalition to support."

## The money

We checked every contribution from every group on that list against the
2025-26 filings of Rep. Langworthy's campaign committee (Langworthy for
Congress) and his leadership PAC (Circle the Wagons PAC). Six of the 17
have political action committees that gave:

| Supporter named in his release | PAC contributions to his committees, 2025-26 |
|---|---|
| National Association of Home Builders (BUILD-PAC) | $20,000 ($10,000 to each committee) |
| National Propane Gas Association PAC | $10,000 |
| NEFI Home Comfort PAC | $8,500 |
| American Gas Association PAC | $7,500 |
| Plumbing-Heating-Cooling Contractors PAC | $5,000 |
| Energy Marketers of America Small Business Committee PAC | $3,000 |
| **Total** | **$54,000** |

We found no contribution from the other 11 groups' PACs in these
filings.

In plain language: six of the bill's named backers have given his
campaign and leadership PAC $54,000 this cycle. That is about 4 percent of
the $1.28 million his two committees received from PACs and party
committees over the same period.

### NEFI's PAC: its top recipient

NEFI Home Comfort PAC's 2025-26 reports list payments to 10 candidate and
party committees, totaling about $34,700. Langworthy for Congress is first,
at $9,263.12. The next largest, Rep. Claudia Tenney's campaign, received
$5,970. Counting contributions alone, he is still first: $8,500, against
$5,000 each to Reps. Richard Neal and Andrew Garbarino.

NEFI's figure for him includes three contributions ($1,000, $2,500 and
$5,000, totaling $8,500, which his campaign reports receiving) and one
more item: on October 31, 2025, NEFI's year-end report lists a payment of
$763.12 to Langworthy for Congress described as "Breakfast expenses," on
its line for other disbursements rather than contributions. We did not
find a matching entry in the campaign's reports. The filings alone do not
say what the payment was. NEFI's PAC uses the same line for other
candidates: it reported two items to Rep. Tenney's campaign there, one
described as "Food and beverage costs" and one as "General election
contribution."

### The wider oil and gas money

Counting four of the supporters above (NPGA, NEFI, AGA and EMA), 17 PACs
from oil and gas producers, refiners, pipelines, gas utilities, and
propane and heating-fuel dealers gave his two committees a combined
$83,500 this cycle; $54,500 of it came from PACs not on his supporter
list. The largest: National Fuel Gas, NPGA, Marathon Petroleum and
ExxonMobil at $10,000 each. The count leaves out electric utilities
(including National Grid USA's PAC, $5,000, whose company also delivers
gas in New York), diversified companies such as Koch Industries ($10,000),
and contractors and home builders. The
site's [December 2025 entry](/fact-checks/2025-12-energy-policy-oil-gas/)
covered $66,466 from the industry in the 2024 cycle, using OpenSecrets'
industry totals, which count differently; the two figures should not be
added or compared directly.

## The fair reading, at full strength

- **Everything here is legal and disclosed.** PACs report their giving,
  and so does his campaign. The site found these records because they are
  public.
- **PACs usually give to members who already agree with them.** The
  propane association's first contribution this cycle came nearly four
  months before the bill was introduced. The records cannot show whether the
  money followed the bill, the bill followed the money, or both followed a
  position he already held.
- **NPGA's giving to him is not unique.** The propane association's PAC
  gave $10,000 to at least four other candidates this cycle, in the same
  batches, including Democratic Rep. Jim Costa. NEFI's PAC also
  gives to Democrats, including the campaigns of Richard Neal, John Larson,
  Jared Golden, Seth Magaziner and Chris Pappas.
- **The amounts are a small share of his money.** The six supporters'
  $54,000 is about 4 percent of the PAC and party money his two committees
  have received this cycle.
- **NEFI is open about its role.** It says publicly that it helped draft
  the bill. Whether the bill serves NY-23 households is a separate question,
  which the site's February entry addresses.

## Questions this raises

1. NEFI says it "helped draft" the Energy Choice Act. Which provisions did
   it write, and did any consumer, ratepayer or local-government group have
   comparable input?
2. What was the $763.12 "Breakfast expenses" payment NEFI's PAC reported
   making to Langworthy for Congress on October 31, 2025, and where does it
   appear in the campaign's reports?
3. His posts this month describe the bill as protecting families' freedom
   to choose how to heat their homes. Will he say, when he promotes it,
   that the group that says it helped write it, and others that back it,
   are also among his donors?
