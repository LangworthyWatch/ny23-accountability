## Why this matters

Members of Congress choose what to spend their oversight authority on. On
September 8, Rep. Langworthy asked the inspector general of the U.S.
Department of Housing and Urban Development to investigate a housing project
at Canalside in downtown Buffalo. The project is in New York's 26th District,
not his. The same week, the record on this site shows what was pending in his
own eight counties: eight hospitals on a federal at-risk list, twenty-eight
pharmacies closed since 2023, and $87 million in federal police and
counterterrorism grants still withheld from New York with no public word from
him. This entry sets the two side by side. It does not say the Buffalo
question is illegitimate. It documents which one got the letter.

## The letter

**Source:** Letter to the HUD Office of Inspector General, September 8, 2026,
as reported by the Buffalo News and WIVB. From the Buffalo News:

> "I am concerned that the project has changed materially since it was
> competitively solicited and that its extraordinarily high public cost may
> undermine the purpose of federal affordable housing resources."

> "Federal affordable housing programs should serve families in need – not
> be used to subsidize unnecessarily expensive development simply because a
> project occupies a prominent or high-profile location."

The letter asks the inspector general to review whether HUD resources are
involved and all requirements met; whether the increase in public subsidy
"raises concerns regarding procurement integrity, internal controls, waste,
fraud or abuse"; whether the per-unit cost is an efficient use of federal
dollars; and whether anything should be referred to the IRS or the Treasury
Inspector General for Tax Administration. To WIVB he said: "I'm not accusing
anyone of anything, but let's examine — were all laws followed."

**The project.** Pennrose, a Philadelphia developer, was selected in 2023
after a May 2022 state request for proposals to build 251 apartments, 187 of
them affordable, on the North Aud Block at Canalside, a $225 million project
now under construction. The RFP cited $10 million in public funding; the
project now carries $30 million in federal low-income housing tax credits,
$80 million in discretionary grants and a 30-year payment in lieu of taxes,
for $152.9 million in government support before the tax break, more than
$609,000 per apartment on a cost of $896,000 per unit. Empire State
Development, which oversees the Erie Canal Harbor Development Corporation,
defends it: "For three decades, the North Aud Block has generated nothing but
blight and zero tax revenue." A group of Buffalo developers including former
Rep. Chris Jacobs wrote to the Governor, Mayor and County Executive in June
asking that the project be rebid; Jacobs said the congressman "raises valid
and serious points."

**The district.** Canalside is in Buffalo, represented by Rep. Tim Kennedy.
Census Bureau geocoding of the site's addresses returns the 26th District.
The Buffalo News put it directly: "Langworthy doesn't represent downtown
Buffalo or Canalside but serves on the House Committee on Oversight and
Government Reform." His senior adviser, Jessica Proud, said that seat "gives
him jurisdiction over HUD 'on a project that affects the region, not just the
footprint of the site.'"

## The fair reading

- **Oversight jurisdiction is real.** The Oversight Committee's reach is not
  limited by district lines, and a member may ask an inspector general to
  look at any federally funded project. His adviser's rationale is quoted
  above in full.
- **The subsidy question is genuinely contested.** The per-unit cost and the
  post-award growth in public support have critics beyond partisan lines; the
  Buffalo News has covered the 30-year tax break as controversial in its own
  right. This entry takes no position on the merits.
- **He has acted on district matters this year**: the grape-purchase
  advocacy, the ARC and Head Start grant announcements, the biosimilars bill,
  the rural health fund announcements. The gap documented here is specific,
  not total: the three largest pending problems in the district's health and
  safety record drew no comparable letter, statement or request.
- **The pharmacy losses are not his to fix** in any direct sense; they are
  listed because they are what the district is facing, not as a charge.

## Questions this raises

1. Has he written to any federal agency about the $17.7 million in SHSP
   funds owed to the law enforcement agencies in his own counties, the way
   he wrote to HUD's inspector general about Canalside?
2. Has he asked CMS or the state to name which NY-23 hospitals will receive
   Rural Health Transformation funds, or asked the Fiscal Policy Institute's
   at-risk finding to be addressed?
3. His adviser says the project "affects the region." By that standard, what
   region does the withheld counterterrorism money affect?
4. Will his office release the HUD letter?
