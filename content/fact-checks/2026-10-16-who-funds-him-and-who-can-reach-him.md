## Why this matters

A town hall is where anyone in a district can ask a representative a
question in person, with neighbors listening. Rep. Langworthy has not held
an in-person public town hall since he took office in January 2023. He
says he stays in touch in other ways.

Campaign records cannot show who has his attention or what influences
his decisions, and this entry does not claim to. They can show who pays
for his campaigns. Through September 30, 2026, most of that money came
from political action committees, and a small share came from donors with
addresses in NY-23. Set next to how constituents can reach him, that is
information voters can weigh for themselves.

## Where the individual money comes from

Itemized donors list an address. We matched each donor's ZIP code to the
congressional district it falls in, using the Census Bureau's file that
maps ZIP code areas to the 119th Congress's districts. Some ZIP codes are
split between NY-23 and a neighboring district; those are counted
separately.

| Donor's ZIP code | Cycle total | Share of all contributions |
|---|---|---|
| Entirely inside NY-23 | $194,775.25 | 8.9% |
| Split between NY-23 and another district | $118,677.00 | 5.4% |
| Elsewhere in New York | $329,178.91 | 15.0% |
| Outside New York | $259,012.80 | 11.8% |
| New York, ZIP code not matched | $22,685.00 | 1.0% |

Unitemized gifts, 3.1 percent of contributions, carry no address. Even if
every one of them came from NY-23, and every split ZIP code counted as
NY-23, people living in the district would account for at most $380,975,
or 17.3 percent of what his campaign raised this cycle. Counting only ZIP
codes entirely inside the district, the figure is 8.9 percent, or 9.5
percent if Elma's 14059, which lies almost entirely in NY-23, is counted
as inside. (Totals are
gross of refunds; the unmatched row includes a $20,000 Manhattan
contribution, $13,000 of which the campaign refunded.) The last
quarter looks the same: 9.1 percent from ZIP codes entirely inside NY-23,
and at most 17.4 percent.

**Lobbyists and government-affairs professionals.** Donors who list their
own occupation or employer as lobbyist, government affairs, government
relations (including abbreviations such as "gov't affairs"), public
affairs or public policy gave his campaign $70,850 this cycle, from 44
donors. Of that, $59,300, or 84 percent, came from Virginia and
Washington, D.C., and $2,300 from New York. Donors at lobbying firms who
list themselves only as "consultant" are not counted.

In plain language: PACs give more than half of his campaign money. Of the
rest, the largest shares come from donors in other parts of New York and
outside the state. Donors with addresses in NY-23 provide between about
9 and 17 cents of every dollar.

## How constituents can reach him

- **No in-person public town hall since January 2023.** WSKG reported in
  April 2025 that he "has not held an in-person town hall since he took
  office in 2023," and the site's earlier reporting found none through May
  2026. A web search of news coverage on October 16, 2026 found no report
  of an in-person town hall since.
- **Monthly telephone town halls.** His office says it dials out to 100,000
  phone numbers each month and advertises a call-in number. He opened his September 1, 2026 call:
  "welcome to this month's telephone town hall meeting." The site has
  documented [same-day notice and postponements](/fact-checks/2026-05-02-tele-town-hall-pattern/)
  for these calls.
- **Other channels he cites:** a weekly newsletter, news interviews,
  community events, and office calls, emails, letters and meetings.

### Update, November 11, 2026: commenting limited on both Facebook pages

On November 11, 2026, posts on both of his Facebook pages carried
Facebook's notice that the page "limited who can comment on this post."
We checked every post where the comment area was in view. All seven
carried the notice: four on the official page ("Congressman Nick
Langworthy") and three on the campaign page ("Nick Langworthy"), dated
from about November 6 through November 11.

**When it started.** The setting was applied on the afternoon of November
10. This site's account watched commenting stop that afternoon, and the
comment timestamps agree. Viewed logged out at about 8:30 a.m. on
November 11, the newest visible comment on any of these posts was about
17 hours old, which places it in mid-to-late afternoon on November 10.
None appears after that. Posts from earlier in the week were limited at
the same time, and the posts published since have been limited from the
start.

**What the setting is.** Facebook lets any page limit who can comment on
a post, to the page's followers or to the profiles the post mentions. The
notice does not say which option was chosen. It applies to everyone the
same way, which makes it different from blocking particular people.
Comments left before the change, and comments from accounts the setting
still allows, stay visible. Viewed logged out on November 11, the November
9 casework post still showed 41 comments. Several of the most visible
asked for in-person town halls.

**What his own posts say.** The casework graphic reads: "please don't
ever hesitate to reach out." A post on the official page from about November 6,
linking his office's services page, reads: "If you need help with any
issue related to the federal government, please, contact us." On election
night, November 4, he told supporters to "take a stroll through to the
Facebook comments and you can see how noisy it's been," as transcribed
from the campaign page's video in [the election-returns entry](/fact-checks/2026-11-04-mandate-claim-election-returns/).

**How constituents can reach him now.** His website lists five offices
and their phone numbers:

| Office | Phone |
|---|---|
| Washington, 422 Cannon House Office Building | (202) 225-3161 |
| Jamestown, 2-6 East Second Street | (716) 488-8111 |
| Olean, 1 Bluebird Square | (585) 543-5033 |
| Elmira, 203 Lake Street | (607) 377-3130 |
| Clarence (Williamsville), 8201 Main Street | (716) 547-6844 |

There is no public email address. Email goes through a web form, and the
site first asks visitors to "enter your address to verify residency
within the district"; the address "must be recognized by the United
States Postal Service." That kind of check is common in the House: Rep.
Nick LaLota's site uses the same address step, and Rep. Paul Tonko's asks
for a ZIP+4 code before its form.

**In plain language:** constituents can still call, write, or email
through the form, and his office reports more than 1,400 casework cases
opened in 2026. What changed is the one public place where they could answer his
posts in front of other constituents. On both pages, commenting is now
limited, and Facebook does not say to whom.

**What cuts his way.** Pages commonly limit comments to cut spam and
abuse, and many officials do it after elections. The setting is available
to every page and treats every commenter alike.

**What we do not know.** We don't know when the setting was applied. We
checked posts from the past week, not older ones, so we don't know whether
earlier posts are also limited. And we don't know whether commenting is
open to followers or only to people the posts tag.

## The fair reading, at full strength

- **A high PAC share is common for incumbents.** Among 26 New York House
  members with 2026 reports in the FEC's summary file, his 54.6 percent PAC
  share ranks seventh. Rep. Andrew Garbarino (R) is higher, and so are four
  Democrats: Reps. Paul Tonko, Gregory Meeks, Yvette Clarke and Joseph
  Morelle. Rep. Claudia Tenney (R) is also higher. (The file's coverage runs through June 30 or September 30
  depending on the member; the ranking counts the 26 incumbents who have
  raised more than $100,000, including Rep. Laura Gillen, whom the file
  still flags as a challenger.)
- **"Outside the district" is often next door.** NY-23 includes only part
  of Erie County. The City of Buffalo's ZIP codes fall in the neighboring
  NY-26 in the Census file, so Buffalo donors count here as "elsewhere in
  New York."
- **We did not compute the in-district share for other members**, so this
  entry does not say his is unusually low.
- **The location data is approximate.** Census ZIP code areas do not match
  postal ZIP codes exactly, and donors list mailing addresses, which can
  differ from where they live.
- **Money is not the same as influence.** The records show who funds his
  campaign. They do not show what any donor asked for or received, and
  this entry makes no such claim.
- **He does offer other ways to reach him**, and says thousands of
  constituents contact his offices every week.
- **He has explained why he skips constituent-organized events.** In the
  same statement he called them a "nationally driven leftwing charade of
  fake town hall meetings" organized under "the umbrella of the Indivisible
  network." The Corning organizers, Citizens for a Better Southern Tier,
  told WSKG they are not part of Indivisible and receive no funding from
  it.

## Questions this raises

1. He says telephone town halls reach "all the constituents of NY-23."
   Will he hold an in-person public town hall before the November 3
   election?
2. Will his office publish a schedule and recordings of every telephone
   town hall, including canceled and postponed ones?
3. With more than half his campaign money coming from PACs, will he
   disclose his meetings with the PACs' sponsoring organizations and their
   lobbyists?
4. **Why limit who can comment on the official page?** The page is his office's, and its own posts ask constituents to reach out. Will public commenting return, and does the office have a written comment policy?
