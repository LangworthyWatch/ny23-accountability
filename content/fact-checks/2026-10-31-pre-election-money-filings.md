## Why this matters

The last full picture of a campaign's money before an election is the
pre-general report, which covers October 1 through 14 and was due
October 22. After that, any contribution of $1,000 or more must be
disclosed within 48 hours. Rep. Langworthy's campaign has now filed both.
Together they show who paid for the final stretch, where the money came
from, and where it went.

This site documented on October 16 that 55 cents of every dollar Rep.
Langworthy raised this cycle came from political action committees and at
most 17 cents from donors with addresses in the district. The October
filings continue that pattern and add three things: a single $551,000
television buy, a $25,000 payment to the Republican committee of a
county 350 miles from the district, and a two-week stretch in which about
two-thirds of his itemized individual money arrived earmarked through
AIPAC's political action committee.

## The pre-general report in numbers

Langworthy for Congress, October 1 through 14, 2026:

| | |
|---|---|
| Raised | $53,220 |
| Spent | $597,785 |
| Cash on hand, October 14 | $1,994,177 |
| From individuals | $26,220 |
| From PACs and committees | $26,000 |
| Cycle contributions through Oct. 14 | $2,301,824 |
| PAC share of cycle contributions | 53.8% |

It was the smallest fundraising period of his cycle; every quarterly
report since January 2025 showed between $296,000 and $427,000 in
receipts. His cash on hand fell from $2,563,742 on September 30 to
$1,994,177 on October 14, the first time this cycle the balance went down.

**In plain language:** in the first two weeks of October the campaign
spent eleven times what it raised and still had two million dollars left.

## Where his October money came from

**Individuals: $26,220.** Of the $25,700 that was itemized, $16,600 came
from 18 donors whose contributions are marked "earmarked (non-directed)
through American Israel Public Affairs Committee PAC," and another $750
through the Republican Jewish Coalition PAC and WinRed. Nearly all of the
AIPAC-earmarked donors list addresses in Scarsdale, New Rochelle, Harrison,
Larchmont, Purchase and other Westchester County towns, or in New York
City. None of them is in the district.

Donors with addresses in NY-23 ZIP codes gave $6,850 in the period, from
six contributions: $3,500 from a Williamsville homemaker (first recorded
to her husband, then reattributed to her), $1,500 from a Williamsville
retiree, $1,000 from an Ellicottville business owner, $500 from Silver
Creek, $250 from Waverly and $100 from Williamsville. That is 26.7 percent
of itemized individual money.

**PACs and committees: $26,000.** $5,000 each from the American Institute
of CPAs, SpaceX and UPS; $2,500 each from BASF and the Barona Band of
Mission Indians; $1,000 each from AbbVie, Vertex Pharmaceuticals, the NRA
Victory Fund, the Meat Institute, Lyondell Chemical and the American Supply
Association.

**Party: $1,000.**

## Where it went

**$551,000 to Strategic Media Placement Inc. of Delaware, Ohio, on
October 9, for "media buy."** This is the campaign's only television
payment of the cycle through October 14. It is 92 percent of the period's
spending.

**$25,000 to the Nassau County Republican Committee of Westbury, Long
Island, on October 9, for "political contribution."** Nassau County is
not in the district or anywhere near it. The payment is the largest his
campaign has made to any county party committee this cycle. For
comparison, the Erie County Republican Committee, his home county's,
received $11,500 in political contributions across the whole cycle plus
$7,000 in event sponsorships. The Nassau County executive is Bruce
Blakeman, the Republican nominee for governor; on October 28, Blakeman
and Langworthy made a five-stop "Fire Hochul" tour of the district from
Owego to Lakewood. The filing states no purpose beyond "political
contribution," and this entry draws no connection between the payment and
the tour. Both are facts in the public record, nineteen days apart.

**$22,900 to IFA Holdings LLC of Arlington, Virginia, on October 2, for
"survey research."** A poll, two days before early voting opened.

**$10,000 to Capelianis Consulting** for finance consulting, and $6,082 to
D&D Printing of Buffalo.

## The 48-hour notices: $79,500 in two weeks

From October 15 through 29 his campaign filed nine notices covering 45
contributions of $1,000 or more.

**$42,000 from 21 committees and firms:** $5,000 each from COLEPAC and the
LPL Financial PAC; $4,000 from the American Chemistry Council; $3,500 from
the Seneca Nation of Indians; $2,500 each from Morgan Stanley, Heidelberg
Materials, the Cement PAC and the Republican Italian American Committee;
$2,000 from the Independent Community Bankers; $1,500 from the law firm
Phillips Lytle; and $1,000 each from the property-casualty insurers' PAC,
the Association for Accessible Medicines, State Farm, FishPAC, Pharmavite,
the egg producers' PAC, the soybean growers' PAC, the general contractors'
PAC, GSK, the law firm Florio Perrucci, and the state Senate campaign
committee of Mike Ranzenhofer.

**$37,500 from 24 individuals.** Three of them list addresses in the
district: $2,500 from a Lakewood retiree, $2,500 from an Elma retiree and
$1,000 from a Hamburg office manager. Nineteen thousand dollars came from
elsewhere in New York, mostly Westchester and Rochester. $12,500 came from
out of state, including $5,500 from three donors who list their occupation
as "lobbyist": $3,000 from Salt Point Strategies, $1,500 from a
self-employed lobbyist in Charleston, South Carolina, and $1,000 from
Mehlman Consulting. The two largest out-of-state gifts, $3,500 from
Birmingham, Alabama, and $1,000 from Naples, Florida, list occupation and
employer as "information requested."

## The fair reading

Everything above is legal, disclosed, and in most respects ordinary for
a House incumbent.

Earmarking through a PAC is how AIPAC's members give to candidates; the
money is the donors' own, and each donor chose him. Conduit giving is
routine on both sides of the aisle, and $152,913 of his own individual
money this cycle came through WinRed the same way. The pre-general period
is two weeks; the AIPAC share of his individual money for the whole cycle
is about 6 percent.

A $551,000 television buy in October is what a two-million-dollar
campaign is for. Polling is routine. Transfers to the NRCC and to other
candidates are what members in safe seats are expected to do, and he has
done it generously. Paying a county party committee is lawful and common,
and the entry does not know why Nassau County's committee received
$25,000 from a Western New York campaign; the filing does not say, and no
inference is drawn.

A two-million-dollar balance in the last two weeks is the ordinary
condition of a House incumbent, and a slow fundraising fortnight while
the money is being spent is ordinary too.

What the filings document is the pattern this site recorded on October
16, continued to the end: more than half the money from PACs, a small
fraction from the district, and in the final stretch, individual money
that came mostly from Westchester and Washington and lobbyists, and a
quarter-million-dollar line that leads to Long Island.

---

## Questions this raises

1. What was the $25,000 payment to the Nassau County Republican Committee
   for?
2. In the two weeks before early voting, eight contributions came from
   addresses in the district and 18 came earmarked through AIPAC's PAC
   from Westchester and New York City. Does the campaign consider that
   representative of its support?
3. Three of the 24 large donors in the 48-hour notices are lobbyists, and
   two of the largest have no occupation on file. Will the campaign
   disclose who they are before the election?
