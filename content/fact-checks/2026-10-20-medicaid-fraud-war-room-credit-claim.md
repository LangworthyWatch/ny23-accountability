## Why this matters

How much Medicaid money is lost to fraud is a fair question for anyone
who pays for the program or relies on it, and New York is part of the
picture: it was one of ten states that got letters from Energy and
Commerce leaders this year asking about Medicaid program integrity. Rep. Langworthy sits on the House Energy and Commerce
Committee, which oversees Medicaid. On October 20 he told constituents
that "we took swift and decisive action against 50 high-risk Medicaid
providers, halting over $203 million in potentially improper payments."

The numbers come from a July 28 announcement by the Centers for Medicare
& Medicaid Services (CMS), and they match it. But the actions were taken
by CMS, the Department of Health and Human Services' Office of Inspector
General (OIG) and state Medicaid agencies, not by his committee. And the
$203 million measures what those 50 providers were paid since January
2025; the announcement does not break it down into amounts stopped.

## What checks out

The figures match a CMS press release dated July 28, 2026, headlined "CMS
Medicaid Fraud War Room Stops More Than $203 Million in Improper Payments
During First 88 Days." It says the War Room, launched April 23, "stopped
more than $203 million in potentially improper Medicaid payments in just
under 90 days" and "coordinated actions against 50 unique, high-risk
Medicaid providers."

| What CMS reported | Actions | Payments tied to the providers, since January 1, 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| OIG "Notices of Intent to Exclude" | 42 | about $160.7 million |
| State enforcement actions on War Room referrals | 15 | about $46.2 million |
| Unique providers (7 faced both) | **50** | **about $203.3 million** |

*Source: CMS, July 28, 2026. CMS counts 7 providers that faced both a federal and a state action, for 50 unique providers.*

The post's "under a 90 day period," "50 high-risk Medicaid providers" and
"over $203 million in potentially improper payments" all match the
release.

## Who took the action

**CMS, OIG and the states did.** CMS says the War Room "was established
in coordination with the White House Task Force to Eliminate Fraud and
brings together CMS, OIG, state Medicaid agencies, and federal law
enforcement partners." The 42 notices were "issued by the U.S. Department
of Health and Human Services Office of Inspector General," and the 15
state actions were "taken against providers based on MFWR referrals." The
release does not mention Congress, the Energy and Commerce Committee or
Rep. Langworthy. CMS's June 2026 "Fast Facts" sheet on the War Room lists
its partners as the White House Task Force to Eliminate Fraud, CMS and
HHS-OIG; it does not list Congress either.

**What his committee did was oversight.** Energy and Commerce has run an
investigation into Medicaid fraud. In a March 5, 2026 release, Chairman
Brett Guthrie and Subcommittee Chairmen John Joyce (Oversight and
Investigations) and Morgan Griffith (Health) announced letters to ten more
states "to request information and documents on the actions each state is
taking to strengthen Medicaid program integrity." The committee's
Oversight and Investigations Subcommittee held three hearings on Medicare
and Medicaid fraud this year: February 3, March 17 (with CMS Deputy
Administrator Kim Brandt, who is quoted in the July release) and June 25.

**He is not on that subcommittee, and he did not take part in those
hearings.** The House Clerk lists Rep. Langworthy on Energy and Commerce's
Energy, Environment and Health subcommittees. Members of the full
committee can join other subcommittees' hearings by "waiving on." The
House Committee Repository's member rosters for all three hearings do not
list him as a member or a waive-on, and his name does not appear in the
published transcripts. New York's Medicaid director, Amir Bassiri,
testified at the June 25 hearing.

**His press releases don't mention it.** None of the 20 press releases
on his House website from July 22 through October 15, 2026 mentions the
War Room, Medicaid fraud or the $203 million, and he had issued none since
as of October 21.

In plain language: the committee asked questions and held hearings. The
enforcement the post describes, the 42 exclusion notices and 15 state
actions, came from the executive branch and the states. The post's
wording, opening "As a member of the Energy and Commerce Committee,"
credits the committee, or him, with enforcement it did not carry out.

## What the $203 million measures

**CMS says "stopped," but defines the figure as payments made.** The
release says the War Room "stopped more than $203 million in potentially
improper Medicaid payments," but the only definition it gives is that the
50 providers represent "approximately $203.3 million in Medicaid payments
since January 1, 2025," a period that began nearly 16 months before the
War Room launched on April 23, 2026. The release does not say how much of
that money was withheld, recovered or prevented from being paid.

**Most of the actions were proposals.** Under federal rules, a notice of
intent to exclude is a proposal: it gives the provider "the basis for the
proposed exclusion," and "within 30 days of receipt of notice... the
individual or entity may submit documentary evidence and written argument
concerning whether the exclusion is warranted" (42 CFR 1001.2001). The
release does not say how many of the 42 notices became final exclusions.

**"Improper" is not the same as fraud.** CMS's Fiscal Year 2025 Improper
Payments Fact Sheet says "improper payment measurement is not a measure of
fraud." It estimated Medicaid improper payments at 6.12%, or $37.39
billion, in fiscal 2025, and said 77.17% of them "were the result of
insufficient documentation, which is generally not indicative of fraud or
abuse." That national estimate covers a different scope and period than
the War Room figure, so the two should not be compared directly.

**The providers are not named.** The release does not identify the
providers or the states involved, so we cannot check whether any are in
New York or NY-23.

In plain language: CMS flagged 50 providers who had been paid about $203
million since January 2025 and started actions against them. That is real
enforcement. But the release does not show $203 million "halted," and when
CMS announced the actions, most were proposed exclusions that providers
had 30 days to contest. CMS has not said how many became final.

## The fair reading

- **The enforcement is real**, and it is the kind of work the post says
  he supports. Stopping fraud protects both taxpayers and patients. Some
  payments were in fact stopped: in one example CMS describes, a lab
  testing provider's state "immediately stopped sending payments." CMS
  has not said how much of the $203 million that covers.
- **"We" can be read broadly**, as Republicans or the government as a
  whole, rather than his committee. But the post opens "As a member of the
  Energy and Commerce Committee," which ties the "we" to the committee.
- **The committee's oversight is real too.** Its leaders' letters and its
  hearings put pressure on CMS and the states, and CMS's Kim Brandt
  testified before the committee in March. Oversight can contribute to
  enforcement without being the enforcement.
- **He has his own anti-fraud record.** In March 2025 he and Rep. Joe
  Morelle (D-N.Y.) introduced the Medicaid Provider Screening
  Accountability Act (H.R. 1875), which would require states to check a
  federal data system so providers "terminated in other states" cannot
  re-enroll; his release said it would "help keep bad actors out of the
  Medicaid system." It was referred to Energy and Commerce, and
  Congress.gov's bill status record (last updated May 30, 2025) shows no
  further action on it. In June 2026 his office said that, on the House
  Oversight Committee, he "participated in hearings examining the massive
  fraud schemes in Minnesota." Neither is the War Room's enforcement.
- **The post's "halting" mirrors CMS's own "stopped."** It is CMS's
  release that defines the figure as payments since January 2025.
- **This is a pattern the site has documented before**, with grant
  announcements credited as his own: see the
  [Chemung County ARC entry](/fact-checks/2026-10-18-chemung-arc-sewer-credit-claim/).
