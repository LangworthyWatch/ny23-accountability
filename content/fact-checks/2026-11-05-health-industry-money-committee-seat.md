## Why this matters

On December 13, 2024, Rep. Langworthy announced his appointment to the
House Energy and Commerce Committee, calling its "broad jurisdiction over
energy, healthcare, telecommunications, and more" an "unparalleled
opportunity to influence the issues that impact families across the
Southern Tier and Western New York." He took a seat on its Health
Subcommittee, which writes Medicaid law and much of federal drug and
device policy. He also sits on the Oversight Committee, whose written
jurisdiction includes federal procurement.

A reader asked this site a plain question: how many of his donors do
business with the federal government? This entry answers it for the
health sector first, because that is where his donor list changed most
after the appointment, and because the companies involved are paid by
the same programs his subcommittee writes.

---

## The statements

**Source:** Press release, December 13, 2024.

> "With the committee's broad jurisdiction over energy, healthcare,
> telecommunications, and more, we have an unparalleled opportunity to
> influence the issues that impact families across the Southern Tier and
> Western New York."

**Source:** Press release, June 3, 2026, introducing the CHECK Act.

> "The CHECK Act shines a light on the hidden costs and middlemen driving
> up healthcare expenses, gives patients clear and understandable
> information about their services, and helps protect families from
> surprise charges."

Neither statement is false. The first is the frame for what follows: the
seat changed who funds him. The second is the standard he set for
others, transparency about who is paid and by whom, and it is the
standard this entry applies to his own filings.

## Part 1: What changed after the appointment

Health-industry political action committees are identified here by one
rule applied to all three cycles: the PAC's name or its sponsoring
organization, as recorded in the FEC's committee master file, is a
pharmaceutical, device, diagnostic, insurance, provider, pharmacy or
health trade group. Veterinary, auto-care and energy PACs that match on
a word are excluded. Receipts are to his campaign committee and, from
2023, his leadership PAC, Circle the Wagons; memo entries are excluded.

| Cycle | All PAC receipts | Health-industry PACs | Share | His committees |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2021-22 | $203,224 | $6,000 (3 contributions) | 3.0% | Candidate; seated January 2023 on Agriculture, Oversight, Rules |
| 2023-24 | $895,436 | $85,250 (55) | 9.5% | Agriculture, Oversight, Rules |
| 2025-26 (through September 30) | $1,276,689 | $193,000 (63) | 15.1% | Energy and Commerce (Health, Energy, Environment), Oversight, Rules |

By type of donor:

| Type | 2021-22 | 2023-24 | 2025-26 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pharmaceutical, device and diagnostic companies | $0 | $5,500 | $62,000 |
| Insurers and federal health-program contractors | $1,000 | $25,250 | $44,000 |
| Provider, pharmacy and trade associations | $5,000 | $54,500 | $87,000 |

Twelve corporate health PACs that had never given to him gave in the
2026 cycle: Amgen ($10,500), Pfizer ($5,000), Biogen, DaVita and
Laboratory Corporation of America ($3,500 each), AstraZeneca, Edwards
Lifesciences and Eli Lilly ($3,000 each), Alnylam, BioMarin and
Bristol-Myers Squibb ($2,500 each) and Genentech ($2,000). Each of them
gives to members of the Health Subcommittee in both parties as a matter
of course.

**In plain language:** his total PAC money grew by about 43 percent from
the last cycle to this one. His health-industry PAC money grew by about
126 percent. Drug and device company money went from $5,500 to $62,000.
The change tracks the seat, not the election calendar.

## Part 2: The Medicaid plans that cover his district

Medicaid managed care in New York is run by private plans under state
contract. The state Department of Health publishes enrollment by county
and plan every month. For September 2026, in the eight counties that
make up or overlap NY-23, mainstream Medicaid managed care enrollment
was 259,558 people. (Erie County is counted whole; only part of it is in
the district.)

| Plan | Parent company | Enrollees, eight counties | Parent's PAC to him, 2023-24 | 2025-26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fidelis Care | Centene Corporation | 123,120 | $1,000 | $1,000 |
| Independent Health Association | Independent Health (Buffalo nonprofit) | 60,653 | $0 | $0 |
| Highmark Western and Northeastern New York | Highmark Inc. | 33,926 | $2,250 | $3,000 |
| Molina Healthcare of New York | Molina Healthcare | 26,428 | $0 | $2,000 |
| UnitedHealthcare | UnitedHealth Group | 9,622 | $10,000 | $6,000 |
| Excellus Health Plan | Excellus (Rochester nonprofit) | 5,809 | $0 | $0 |

Plans owned by companies whose PACs gave to him cover 193,096 of the
259,558 enrollees, 74 percent. Outside Erie County, where Independent
Health and Excellus do not operate, every one of the 87,512 mainstream
Medicaid managed care enrollees in Allegany, Cattaraugus, Chautauqua,
Chemung, Schuyler, Steuben and Tioga counties is in a plan whose parent
company's PAC has given to him. Fidelis Care alone covers 68,579 of
them. Fidelis identifies itself on its own website as a trademark of
Centene Corporation.

The sums are small: $12,000 from the four parent companies this cycle,
$13,250 last cycle. The relationship is not. These are the companies
paid by the state, with federal matching funds, to run the program his
subcommittee rewrote in 2025.

**In plain language:** when he voted for the Medicaid provisions of
H.R. 1, the companies that would administer the result in his district
were already his donors. Their interest and his vote did not point the
same way, which the fair-reading section below takes up.

## Part 4: What he has done with the two seats

### The Oversight Committee's jurisdiction over contracts

House Rule X, clause 1(n)(6), gives the Committee on Oversight and
Government Reform jurisdiction over the "overall economy, efficiency,
and management of government operations and activities, including
Federal procurement." Clause 4(c)(2) adds that the committee "may at any
time conduct investigations of any matter without regard to" the clauses
giving another committee jurisdiction. It is the one House committee
that can examine any federal contract.

He has sat on that committee since January 2023. The Clerk's member
data lists him on the full committee only, with no subcommittee
assignment. [An earlier entry](/fact-checks/2026-09-10-oversight-seriously-attendance-record/)
found him absent for 69 of the committee's 145 recorded votes.

The committee did use that jurisdiction on health contractors while he
was a member. Between 2023 and 2024 it held three hearings on pharmacy
benefit managers and on July 23, 2024 published a report naming CVS
Caremark, Express Scripts and OptumRx, the last of which is a
UnitedHealth subsidiary. The report and the chairman's release are
retained.

### His record on the one issue the committee pursued

On pharmacy benefit managers his votes are consistent and run against
one of his largest health donors:

| Date | Measure | His vote | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| December 11, 2023 | H.R. 5378, Lower Costs, More Transparency Act, with PBM provisions | Yea | Passed 320 to 71 |
| December 20, 2024 | H.R. 10545, American Relief Act, the funding bill from which PBM reform had been dropped | Yea | Passed 366 to 34 |
| February 3, 2026 | H.R. 7148, Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026, which enacted Medicare Part D delinking for PBMs | Yea | Passed 217 to 214 |
| June 3, 2026 | Introduced the CHECK Act, requiring PBM and third-party administrator disclosure | Sponsor | Referred |

After the December 2024 vote he wrote that he "remain[ed] committed to
... pursuing much-needed reforms to hold Pharmacy Benefit Managers
accountable." The February 2026 law delivered part of that. The
Energy and Commerce chairman's release on the signing is retained.

### His record where the donors' interests and his votes diverge

- **Medicaid.** He voted Yea on H.R. 1 on May 22, 2025 (Roll Call 145)
  and July 3, 2025 (Roll Call 190). The law's Medicaid provisions reduce
  enrollment, which reduces the per-member payments the plans in Part 2
  receive. [The committee-record entry](/fact-checks/2026-07-03-committee-record-medicaid-seat-at-the-table/)
  and the [rural impact summary](/fact-checks/2026-02-09-medicaid-rural-impact-summary/)
  document the district effect.
- **Nursing homes.** The same law blocked the federal nursing-home
  staffing rule until 2034. [Two](/fact-checks/2026-05-28-nursing-home-staffing-donations/)
  [entries](/fact-checks/2026-10-31-kentucky-nursing-home-joint-fundraising-committee/)
  document $55,000 from one nursing-home operator and $150,800 from six
  Kentucky nursing-home companies through a joint fundraising committee.
- **Oncology.** [One entry](/fact-checks/2026-08-28-nycbs-oncology-money-biosimilars-bill/)
  documents $55,137 from a Long Island cancer network's doctors and
  PACs, the last check 15 days before he introduced a biosimilars bill
  whose economics favor practices like theirs. This cycle the same
  network's employees gave $11,291 and two oncology PACs gave $20,000.

### What he has said

In 149 press releases from his office between April 2025 and September
2026, retained for an earlier entry, pharmacy benefit managers appear in
three. Medicare Advantage, UnitedHealth, Elevance, Centene, Fidelis,
Maximus and dialysis appear in none. On October 20, 2026 he posted that,
"as a member of the Energy and Commerce Committee," he was "committed to
rooting out waste, fraud, and abuse" in Medicaid.

## The fair reading, at full strength

- **This is how the Health Subcommittee is funded, for everyone on it.**
  Pharmaceutical, device and insurer PACs give to nearly every member of
  the subcommittee in both parties. A member who joins it and does not
  see this money would be the exception. The growth documented in Part
  1 is what the seat does, not something he did.
- **The amounts are modest.** $193,000 is 15 percent of his PAC money
  and about 8 percent of everything he raised this cycle. The four
  companies that run his district's Medicaid plans gave $12,000 between
  them.
- **His Medicaid vote cut against the plans' revenue.** Managed care
  companies are paid per enrollee. A law that reduces enrollment reduces
  their income. Whatever the merits of H.R. 1, it was not a vote for
  these donors' interest.
- **His PBM votes cut against UnitedHealth.** OptumRx is one of the
  three PBMs the Oversight report named. He voted for PBM reform three
  times and introduced a disclosure bill. UnitedHealth's PAC gave to him
  before and after.
- **Everything here is lawful and disclosed.** The contributions are in
  his FEC reports. The contracts are in USAspending. The enrollment
  figures are the state's. No donor gave more than the legal limit and
  no filing shows a contribution tied to any act.
- **A member cannot investigate everything.** The Oversight Committee's
  agenda is set by its chairman. A rank-and-file member without a
  subcommittee seat has limited means to open a contractor inquiry on
  his own.

What the record supports is narrower than a charge and still worth
knowing: the seat he described as an "unparalleled opportunity to
influence" health policy was followed, within one cycle, by a
health-industry donor base he did not have before, and that donor base
includes the companies paid to run Medicaid in every one of his
counties and several of the largest health contractors in the federal
government.

## Questions this raises

1. **What accounts for the change?** Twelve drug and device PACs gave to
   him for the first time within eighteen months of his appointment.
   Did his office solicit them, and does he see the seat as the reason?
2. **Has he used the Oversight seat on any health contractor?** The
   committee's jurisdiction over procurement is explicit. Has he asked
   for or joined any inquiry into a Medicare, Medicaid or TRICARE
   contractor, including the ones on his donor list?
3. **Does the CHECK Act's disclosure standard apply to him?** The bill
   would require PBMs and administrators to disclose "detailed claims,
   reimbursement, pricing, fee, rebate, and payment information." Would
   he publish, in his own announcements, the health-industry
   contributions his committees receive while health bills are pending?
4. **What did the Medicaid plans tell him before the H.R. 1 votes?**
   Fidelis, Highmark, Molina and UnitedHealthcare administer the program
   for every enrollee outside Erie County. Did they contact his office
   about the enrollment provisions, and what did he tell them?
