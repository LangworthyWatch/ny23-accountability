## Why this matters

Rep. Langworthy sits on the two House committees with jurisdiction over
nursing homes: Energy and Commerce, which writes Medicaid law, and
Oversight. In January 2026 he formed a joint fundraising committee with
the chairmen of both, Brett Guthrie and James Comer of Kentucky. The
committee raised money on exactly one day. Every dollar came from six
nursing-home operating companies that share a single address in
Richmond, Kentucky. Eleven days later the money was divided among the
three members, their leadership PACs and the National Republican
Congressional Committee, and the committee dissolved.

This site has documented his nursing-home money before: $55,000 from
Benjamin and Judy Landa, and his vote for the law that blocked the
federal nursing-home staffing rule. The Kentucky filings are a different
set of donors and a different vehicle. They came to light through an
October 30 American Prospect report; what follows is the filings
themselves.

## What the filings show

**The committee.** The Langworthy Leadership & Accountability Fund,
FEC committee C00934109, filed its statement of organization on January
12, 2026, from the same Clarence, N.Y. post office box as his campaign,
with the same treasurer. Its participants:

| Participant | Role |
|---|---|
| Langworthy for Congress | his campaign |
| Guthrie for Congress | Rep. Brett Guthrie (R-Ky.), chairman, Energy and Commerce |
| Comer for Congress | Rep. James Comer (R-Ky.), chairman, Oversight |
| Circle the Wagons PAC | Langworthy's leadership PAC |
| Bringing Republican Excellence to Town PAC | Guthrie's leadership PAC |
| Creating Opportunities, Markets and Enthusiasm in Rural Kentucky PAC | Comer's leadership PAC |
| NRCC | the House Republican campaign committee |

**The money.** The committee reported no receipts in January and none in
February. Its March report, filed April 1 as a termination report, lists
$150,800 in receipts, all dated March 19, 2026, from one address, 300
Provider Court, Richmond, Kentucky:

| Contributor | Amount |
|---|---|
| Woodland Oaks Operations LLC | $75,000 |
| Klondike Nursing and Rehabilitation Center | $50,000 |
| Berea Healthcare LLC | $6,000 |
| Cambridge Operations LLC | $6,000 |
| Cumberland KY OpCo LLC | $6,000 |
| Dover Manor Operations LLC | $6,000 |
| In-kind, a self-employed "nursing home management" donor, Sebring, Fla. | $1,800 |

The two large gifts carry attribution lines dividing them among four
partners each, $18,750 apiece for Woodland Oaks and $12,500 apiece for
Klondike. His own campaign's quarterly report books its share with
memo lines naming those partners, along with Mayer Fischl, a
self-described nursing homes owner, Eli Grinspan, chief operating officer
of Blue Grass Health Partners, and Richard Platschek of Lawrence, N.Y.,
whose occupation is listed as nursing home management.

**Where it went.** On March 30 the committee transferred its proceeds:

| Recipient | Amount |
|---|---|
| NRCC | $51,984 |
| Langworthy for Congress | $21,578 |
| Guthrie for Congress | $21,578 |
| Comer for Congress | $21,578 |
| Circle the Wagons PAC (Langworthy) | $9,808 |
| Guthrie's leadership PAC | $9,808 |
| Comer's leadership PAC | $9,808 |
| Compliance, software, in-kind | $4,655 |

The same day, March 19, the NRCC separately recorded $39,000 from
Woodland Oaks Operations and $14,000 from Klondike, with the same
partner attributions. Counting its share of the committee's proceeds,
the NRCC received about $105,000 from the Richmond address that day.

**In plain language:** a fundraising committee that existed for eleven
weeks, took money on one day from one group of nursing-home companies,
and sent the congressman's campaign and PAC $31,386 of it.

## Who the donors are

The six companies are the operating entities of six Kentucky nursing
homes. CMS's Care Compare data, October 2026 release:

| Nursing home | City | Chain (per CMS) | Overall rating | Staffing rating |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Woodland Oaks | Ashland | Bluegrass Health KY | 4 of 5 | 2 |
| Klondike Nursing and Rehabilitation Center | Louisville | Encore Health Partners | 4 | 2 |
| Cambridge Nursing & Rehabilitation Center | Lexington | Bluegrass Health KY | 3 | 2 |
| Cumberland Valley Nursing & Rehabilitation Center | Burkesville | Bluegrass Health KY | 2 | 3 |
| Berea Health and Rehabilitation | Berea | Bluegrass Health KY | 1 | 1 |
| Dover Nursing & Rehabilitation Center | Georgetown | Bluegrass Health KY | 1 | 1 |

CMS groups five of the six under a 15-facility chain it labels
"Bluegrass Health KY," whose average staffing rating across the chain is
1.7 of 5. Two of the six homes carry CMS's lowest overall rating. None is
on the Special Focus Facility list and none carries CMS's abuse icon.

The Prospect, citing a federal inspector general audit and ProPublica,
reports that Fischl and Grinspan co-own a Bronx nursing home, Pinnacle
Multicare, that the audit found had been overpaid $31.2 million by
Medicare in 2021 and 2022. This site documented that audit in its
[Landa entry](/fact-checks/2026-07-25-landa-donor-public-record/); the
audit names the facility, not its owners, and the ownership link is the
Prospect's and ProPublica's reporting, not the audit's. The Prospect also
reports that Kentucky's attorney general opened an investigation this
summer into a death at a Salyersville facility in Rep. Guthrie's district
that it says is owned by the same investor group. That facility is not
among the six donors, and this site has not independently verified the
ownership.

## The committees that oversee nursing homes

Medicaid is the largest payer for nursing-home care. The House Energy
and Commerce Committee writes Medicaid law; the Oversight Committee
investigates how federal money is spent. Rep. Langworthy serves on both.
Reps. Guthrie and Comer chair them. The three men are the three members
of Congress on the committee that took the Richmond money.

Both committees have spent 2026 investigating Medicaid fraud. On
October 20, Rep. Langworthy posted: "As a member of the Energy and
Commerce Committee, I'm committed to rooting out waste, fraud, and abuse.
In under a 90 day period, we took swift and decisive action against 50
high-risk Medicaid providers, halting over $203 million in potentially
improper payments." This site rated that post
[MISLEADING](/fact-checks/2026-10-20-medicaid-fraud-war-room-credit-claim/),
because the actions were CMS's, the inspector general's and the states'.

For this entry the relevant point is narrower. The documents retained
for that fact-check, the committee's March 5 release expanding its fraud
investigation to ten states, CMS's war-room release and fact sheet, and
his own press-release index, contain no mention of nursing homes, skilled
nursing or long-term care. His office has issued no release on nursing
homes in the 70 releases on its index from August 2025 through October
15, 2026. The one documented nursing-home action of his term is a vote
for H.R. 1, which blocked the federal minimum-staffing rule for ten
years; CMS formally repealed the rule in December 2025.

## The fair reading

Joint fundraising committees are an ordinary tool. Members form them with
colleagues and party committees to hold one event and split the
proceeds, and they are often dissolved afterward. Contributions from
limited-liability companies are lawful when, as here, they are attributed
to the individual owners, and the $75,000 and $50,000 gifts are
attributed to four partners each. The filings were
made on time and in full, which is how this entry was possible.

The amounts, for him, are small: $31,386 to his campaign and PAC against
$2.3 million raised this cycle. Two of the six homes are rated four stars,
and CMS does not flag any of them for abuse. The donors have every right
to give to members whose committees affect their industry, and members of
every party take such money. Neither the chairmen's nor Rep. Langworthy's
Medicaid-fraud work was obliged to focus on nursing homes, and the
investigations they have pursued are real.

What the record establishes is the shape of the thing: the committee's
members were the three men whose committees oversee nursing homes, its
only donors were nursing-home companies, and it existed for the length of
one check-writing day plus the paperwork. The site draws no conclusion
about why. It records that it happened, that the six homes' staffing
ratings average under two stars, and that the congressman who took the
money has, in the same year, described himself as rooting out waste,
fraud and abuse in the program that pays those homes, without a public
word about them.

## Questions this raises

1. Who organized the March 19 event or solicitation, and was there one?
   The committee reports no event expenses.
2. Why were the two nursing-home industry chairmen and the one New York
   member of both committees the participants, and no one else?
3. The Energy and Commerce fraud investigation lists provider types it is
   examining. Are nursing homes among them, and if not, why not, given
   that they are among Medicaid's largest line items?
4. Did his office or the committee receive any communication from the
   Richmond donors or their representatives in 2026?
