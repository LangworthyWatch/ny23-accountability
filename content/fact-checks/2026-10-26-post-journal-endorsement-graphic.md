## Why this matters

An endorsement from the hometown paper is the kind of thing a campaign
puts on a card. This one, from the Jamestown Post-Journal on October 24,
is real, and the paper has endorsed Rep. Langworthy in every race since
2022. The graphic built from it chose one paragraph. The editorial also
says things the graphic does not show: that his opponent held "numerous
town hall meetings" that were "well attended" and "greatly appreciated,"
and that Langworthy "has not been as amenable to that process."

The paragraph the graphic does quote makes three claims about his record.
Two of them describe money that the public record shows he requested
jointly with Senators Schumer and Gillibrand, with the Senate listed as
the originating chamber, and in one case money secured before he took
office.

## What the editorial says that the graphic leaves out

The editorial, "Langworthy has earned two more years in Congress," ran
October 24, 2026 in the Post-Journal and, word for word, in the Dunkirk
OBSERVER as "Our choice for District 23." Both papers are edited by John
D'Agostino. Its first three paragraphs are about Aaron Gies:

> "Facing long odds in the race for Congressional District 23, challenger
> Aaron Gies is to be commended for his tireless efforts on the campaign
> trail over the last four months. He has held numerous town hall meetings
> from Erie to Tioga County during the summer and fall."

> "By all accounts, the gatherings were informative and well attended.
> They were also greatly appreciated by those seeking an alternative to the
> incumbent."

> "For his part, U.S. Rep. Nick Langworthy has not been as amenable to that
> process. Since first taking office in 2023, he has said he favors
> telephone conference calls with constituents, in-person meetings and
> events that bring a focus to significant issues and happenings."

The closing paragraph, from which the graphic takes its last two
sentences:

> "Gies deserves high marks for all of his communication and appearances
> while leading a strong campaign. Langworthy, however, has been in the
> trenches fighting for this region for years. He gets our endorsement."

"Has not been as amenable" is the editorial's phrase. The same editor put
it more plainly in his own column on May 30, 2025: "For the record,
Langworthy has been consistent. Since being elected, he said he was not
going to hold town hall events." And: "Having control of the message is
more important than allowing an unruly crowd to steal the show." WSKG
reported in April 2025 that "Langworthy has not held an in-person town
hall since he took office in 2023," and quoted his written statement that
he "will not participate in this nationally driven leftwing charade of fake
town hall meetings."

## The three record claims in the quoted paragraph

### 1. "He secured federal funding for a comprehensive study by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers on Chautauqua Lake."

The money is real. It is a $500,000 Community Project Funding earmark in
the Corps of Engineers Investigations account, enacted in the Consolidated
Appropriations Act, 2024 (Public Law 118-42). The conference explanatory
statement's disclosure table lists the project this way:

| Agency | Account | Project | Amount | House requestor | Senate requestors | Origination |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Army Corps of Engineers (Civil) | Investigations | Chautauqua Lake Aquatic Ecosystem Restoration, NY | $500,000 | Langworthy | Gillibrand, Schumer | S |

Rep. Langworthy did request it; his March 29, 2023 letter to the
Appropriations Committee is on his office's FY2024 request page. So did
both senators, and the table marks the item as Senate-originated. The
study itself was "initially authorized in 2018," per the Corps Pittsburgh
District, four years before he was elected. The Corps describes the
$500,000 as what "enabled study initiation" of a "comprehensive effort,
expected to total $3 million," with Chautauqua County paying half.

What comes next is not secured. The FY2025 appropriation was a full-year
continuing resolution with no community project funding. The FY2026
Energy and Water explanatory statement contains no Chautauqua Lake line.
His FY2027 request page lists the study again, with a nexus letter dated
March 20, 2026, and Senator Gillibrand's FY2027 disclosure requests
$600,000 for it. The Post-Journal's own August 13, 2026 editorial put it
this way: "So far $1.1 million has been spent, and Rep. Nick Langworthy is
working to bring another $600,000 to the project to complete the study."

**MOSTLY TRUE.** He requested and voted for a $500,000 earmark that
started the study. The senators requested the same money, the item
originated in the Senate, the authorization predates him, and the money to
finish is a pending request.

### 2. "With Lake Erie, his work has improved the Barcelona Harbor with dredging projects."

There have been two dredgings since he took office.

**2023.** The Corps removed 65,000 cubic yards in summer 2023. The money
was $1,150,000 that, in the words of the March 15, 2024 joint release from
the two senators and Rep. Langworthy, "Schumer and Gillibrand secured ...
in the bipartisan omnibus spending package for Fiscal Year (FY) 2022."
That law passed in March 2022, nine months before he took office, and its
disclosure table lists no House requestor for the item. His documented
role in the 2023 dredging: he toured the harbor on February 15,
2023, met with the Corps, and on March 13, 2023 announced the Corps would
start "three months ahead of schedule."

**2025.** The Corps removed about 80,000 cubic yards in May and June 2025.
Its own article says the work "was provided in a fiscal year 2024
congressional earmark." That earmark is in the same FY2024 disclosure
table:

| Agency | Account | Project | Budget request | Additional | Total | House requestor | Senate requestors | Origination |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Army Corps of Engineers (Civil) | Operation and Maintenance | Barcelona Harbor, NY | $204,000 | $7,296,000 | $7,500,000 | Langworthy | Gillibrand, Schumer | S |

He requested it; his March 29, 2023 nexus letter is on file. His February
19, 2024 release says he "successfully included nearly $6 million" in the
House version of the bill. The enacted figure, $7.5 million, is the
Senate's number, and the table marks the item Senate-originated. All
three offices announced it together on March 15, 2024, and his quote in
that release is accurate: "I'm proud to advocate for Barcelona Harbor and
secure this much-needed funding in the House."

**MISSING CONTEXT.** "His work" covers one dredging funded before he was in
office and one funded by an earmark he co-requested with both senators.

### 3. "He's been outspoken and a proponent of the HALT ... Fentanyl Act."

**TRUE.** The govinfo status file lists him as an original cosponsor of
H.R. 27 on January 3, 2025. He voted Yea on passage (Roll Call 33,
February 6, 2025, 312 to 108) and Yea on the Senate version that became
law (Roll Call 166, June 12, 2025; Public Law 119-26, signed July 16,
2025). He also cosponsored and voted for the 2023 version. The "major
victory" quote in the editorial is from his July 2025 Jamestown press
conference as reported by the Post-Journal.

## Would the money have come without him?

The entry cannot say what a different representative would have done. It
can say what the disclosure tables show.

- Both FY2024 items are marked Senate-originated, with Senators Schumer and
  Gillibrand as requestors. The money was in the Senate bill whoever held
  the House seat.
- The harbor's previous federal money did not involve the House seat at
  all. The FY2022 Energy and Water disclosure table lists Barcelona Harbor,
  $1,150,000, with the House requestor column blank, the Senate requestors
  Gillibrand and Schumer, and Senate origination. Tom Reed held the seat.
- Community project funding is open to every member of the House. A
  member asking for a Corps harbor and a Corps lake study in his own
  district is doing the ordinary work of the office. That is the baseline
  the editorial describes as being "in the trenches."

## Who the paper is

The Post-Journal and the OBSERVER are owned by Ogden Newspapers of
Wheeling, West Virginia, whose locations page lists both; the company is
run by the Nutting family, and its CEO, Bob Nutting, also chairs the
Pittsburgh Pirates. One editor runs both papers and the Times Observer in
Warren, Pennsylvania, which is why the endorsement appeared in two
mastheads on the same day.

The paper's endorsement history in this seat:

| Year | Headline | What it said |
|---|---|---|
| 2022 | "Major differences in District 23" (OBSERVER) | "He often goes overboard in his criticism ... With that type of attitude, it is tough to find common ground." Concludes: "Della Pia may be more middle ground. But Langworthy better reflects who this district is." |
| 2024 | "Uneventful race for the 23rd Congressional District" | "A fairly pleasant surprise in his first term ... consistently making time to meet with constituents." |
| 2026 | "Langworthy has earned two more years in Congress" | Quoted above. |

The FEC's bulk operating-expenditure files for the 2024 and 2026 cycles
(the latter through June 30, 2026), his campaign's July-to-September 2026
report, and the office communications spending this site has published
show no payment by his campaign or his office to either paper or to Ogden
Newspapers. Nothing in the record suggests the endorsement was anything
other than the editor's opinion, and this entry does not suggest it.

## Fair reading

- The endorsement is the paper's to give. Its stated reasons, that he has
  "given the region a voice" and worked on lake and harbor issues, are
  reasons; the site does not rate opinions.
- He did request both earmarks, voted for the law that carried them, and
  showed up at the harbor. A member who does that can fairly claim a share
  of the credit.
- The graphic is the campaign's selection, not the paper's. Selection is
  what campaigns do. The omitted paragraphs are the ones a voter weighing
  "who shows up" would most want to see, and they are in the same
  editorial the graphic is built from.

## Questions this raises

1. The editorial says he "favors telephone conference calls." His
   statement to WSKG called in-person town halls a "charade." Which
   description does his office stand behind?
2. The March 15, 2024 release was issued jointly by three offices. Why
   does the editorial, and the graphic, name only one?
3. The study's remaining $600,000 is a pending FY2027 request from both
   him and Senator Gillibrand. If it is not enacted, who completes the
   study?
