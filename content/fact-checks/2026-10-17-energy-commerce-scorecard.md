## Why this matters

Rep. Langworthy joined the House Energy and Commerce Committee in January
2025 and sits on three of its subcommittees: Energy, Environment and
Health. His office said at the time that he would be the "first member of
Congress in modern history to represent the Southern Tier counties" on the
committee.

On October 15 he told constituents that his seat "means I can deliver for
NY23 taxpayers," and listed three energy bills. This entry checks those
three bills, then lays out his full record on the committee: what he
sponsored, how far it got, how he voted, and where he spoke. Some of the
record is strong. None of it, so far, is law.

## The three bills in the post

| Bill | What his post says | Where it stands (govinfo, Oct 16, 2026) | His role |
|---|---|---|---|
| POWER Act | "speeds up pipeline and power plant construction" | A **discussion draft** released by Committee Chairman Brett Guthrie on July 22, 2026, bundling 19 bills. It has **no bill number** and has not been introduced. (Other, unrelated House bills use the name "POWER Act.") | Not the sponsor; the draft folds in both of the other two bills in his post |
| GRID Power Act (H.R. 1047) | "prioritizes reliable power plants" | **Passed the House** September 18, 2025, 216 to 206. In the Senate Energy Committee since September 19, 2025. Not law. | **Cosponsor** (signed on June 17, 2025); voted Yea in committee and on the floor |
| State Energy Accountability Act (H.R. 3157) | "exposes the cost of green mandates" | Reported by the committee, 27 to 20 (H. Rept. 119-301). On the House calendar since September 17, 2025. **No floor vote.** | **Sponsor** |

His descriptions of what each bill would do broadly match the committee's
own summaries. The State Energy Accountability Act would require state
utility regulators to "consider and make a determination" on publishing
the costs and reliability effects of certain clean-energy policies, which
is milder than "exposes."

In plain language: one of the three has passed the House, one is waiting
for a floor vote, and one, a package that would include the other two, has
not been introduced. None has become law.

## The scorecard

### Where he sits

The House elected him to Energy and Commerce on January 9, 2025 (H.Res.
21). The House Clerk lists him on three of its subcommittees: **Energy**,
**Environment** and **Health**. He also serves on Rules, where he chairs
the Subcommittee on Legislative and Budget Process, and on Oversight.

### Bills he wrote

He has sponsored 50 bills and resolutions in this Congress. Twenty were
sent to Energy and Commerce.

| Stage reached | His 20 E&C bills | All 2,193 E&C House bills (H.R.) | Republican-sponsored E&C House bills (1,057) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Full-committee markup | **6 (30%)** | 6.4% | 9.9% |
| Reported or ordered reported | 6 (30%) | | |
| Passed the House | 1 (5%) | 4.9% | 7.7% |
| Became law | 0 | 2 | 1 |

(The markup figures include the committee's September 2, 2026 markup,
which the govinfo bill-status data had not yet recorded as of October 16.)

Only Rep. Latta sponsored more E&C bills that reached a full-committee
markup; Reps. Evans, Harshbarger and Miller-Meeks tied him.

His six:

- **Reliable Federal Infrastructure Act (H.R. 4690):** passed the House
  April 22, 2026, 215 to 202. In the Senate Environment and Public Works
  Committee.
- **State Energy Accountability Act (H.R. 3157):** reported; no floor vote.
- **Energy Choice Act (H.R. 3699):** reported, 24 to 21; no floor vote.
  (See the site's [February](/fact-checks/2026-02-25-energy-choice-act/)
  and [October](/fact-checks/2026-10-16-energy-choice-act-supporter-money/)
  entries.)
- **American Quantum Competitiveness Act (H.R. 10163):** ordered reported
  36 to 0 on September 16, 2026.
- **Recycled Materials Attribution Act (H.R. 7502):** ordered reported on
  a recorded vote on September 16, 2026. (The committee's roll-call sheet
  and its vote summary give different margins.) The committee lists it as
  co-led with Democratic Rep. Gonzalez.
- **[BUSES Act](/fact-checks/2026-07-16-buses-act-idling-bounty-subcommittee/) (H.R. 9317):**
  ordered reported by the full committee September 2, 2026, 26 to 22,
  per the committee's vote summary, which lists him and Democratic Rep.
  Gottheimer as its sponsors. The govinfo record has not caught up.

Fourteen others have had no committee action. None of his 50 measures has passed the Senate or become law.

### Bills he cosponsored

He has cosponsored 87 bills sent to Energy and Commerce. Nineteen passed
the House. Six became law: five Congressional Review Act resolutions
overturning federal rules, and the Hydropower Licensing Transparency Act
(P.L. 119-112), a bill led by Democratic Rep. Kim Schrier.

### How he votes in committee

The full committee posted member-by-member results for 221 recorded votes
between February 2025 and September 2026. He voted Yea 92 times and Nay
107 times, and did not vote 22 times.

His 10 percent missed-vote rate is typical for the committee's
Republicans: the median among its 30 Republicans missed 11.1 percent, and
16 of them missed more votes than he did. Democrats missed fewer (median
3.2 percent); across all 54 members the median was 6.3 percent, and 23
missed more than he did. In the Energy and Environment subcommittees he
missed none of the 40 recorded votes that list members; the Health
Subcommittee posted no member-by-member roll calls. (The full
committee's July 20 and September 2, 2026 markups posted summaries without
member-by-member votes, so his votes there are not in this count.)

### Amendments

We found two amendments he offered, both to his own bills at a November
2025 Energy Subcommittee markup, and both adopted by voice vote. We found
none from him at a full-committee markup, including the May 2025 markup
that wrote the committee's share of the 2025 tax-and-spending law's
Medicaid changes. The site's
[July entry](/fact-checks/2026-07-03-committee-record-medicaid-seat-at-the-table/)
covers that markup.

### Hearings

We found no official attendance record for committee hearings, so we
counted the hearings where he spoke, using the transcripts the Government
Publishing Office has published (through March 26, 2026):

| Subcommittee | Hearing transcripts published | He spoke at |
|---|---|---|
| Energy | 11 | 8 |
| Health | 5 | 3 |
| Environment | 6 | 1 |

Not speaking does not mean a member was absent.

In plain language: he moves his own bills through committee more often
than most members, he shows up for committee votes about as often as the
typical Republican on the panel, and his work there is concentrated on
energy. Twenty-one months in, none of the bills he wrote has become law, and
the three he cited as delivering for NY-23 are still pending or not yet
introduced.

## The fair reading, at full strength

- **Getting bills through committee is hard, and he does it.** Thirty
  percent of his committee bills reached a full markup, about three times
  the rate for Republican-sponsored bills.
- **Some of his work is bipartisan.** His quantum bill was approved 36 to
  0, two of his bills are co-led with Democrats, and he cosponsored a
  Democrat's hydropower bill that became law.
- **The Senate is not his to control.** His infrastructure bill and the
  GRID Power Act both passed the House and stopped in the Senate.
- **Committee seats matter beyond bills.** Members can shape legislation
  in drafting and negotiation, which these records don't capture.
- **His committee attendance is ordinary for a Republican on the panel**, not poor.
