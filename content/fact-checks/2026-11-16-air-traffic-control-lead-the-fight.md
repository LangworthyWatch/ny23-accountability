## Why this matters

On Friday, November 13, 2026, the FAA stopped every flight in the country
for about four hours after radar and radio data from parts of the air
traffic control system became unreliable. Between November 15 and 19, Rep.
Langworthy posted four interview clips about it. In each he spoke as
Chairman of the House Aviation Safety Caucus and said he has long pushed to
modernize and fully fund the system.

Every flight out of the district's airports depends on the same national
system that stopped on Friday. Whether the caucus chairman's record matches
his description of it is a fair question.
The short answer: what he says he called for, he did call for. What he
said he would do about it is a different matter.

## The record

### What he said in May 2025

In May 2025, as the administration prepared its air traffic control plan
and Newark's radar and radio outages were in the news, Rep. Langworthy told
Fox News Digital on May 7:

> "I will absolutely lead the fight for [Transportation Secretary Sean
> Duffy] and the president on their plan to modernize our aircraft."

> "When Secretary Duffy and the president roll this out as a spending
> priority, we have to frontload this process. We have to get them the
> resources that they need to get this done, because the flying public —
> everyone's lives hang in the balance on this."

So the claim in his WBEN interview holds up. He did call for full,
upfront funding as the plan was rolled out, and he said so on the record.

### What is not in the record

- **No bill.** Of the 42 bills he has introduced in this Congress, none
  funds or reforms air traffic control. His two aviation-related bills are
  the Safer Skies Act (TSA screening for scheduled charter flights) and the
  Pay TSA Act (paying TSA screeners during a shutdown). Both were referred
  to the Homeland Security Committee and have had no action since. He is
  not a cosponsor of any bill to fund the full modernization plan.
- **No committee seat on it.** He serves on Rules, Energy and Commerce, and
  Oversight. FAA law is written by the Transportation and Infrastructure
  Committee and FAA funding by the Appropriations Committee. He sits on
  neither.
- **No hearing, and none possible from the caucus.** A congressional
  caucus has no power to hold hearings or call witnesses. The committee
  that does, Transportation and Infrastructure, held at least three
  hearings that took up air traffic control in 2025: March 4, June 4 and
  December 16.
- **One air traffic control release in four years.** His House website
  lists 454 press releases from January 2023 to October 15, 2026. One is about air traffic control: the November 25, 2024 caucus
  letter. One other mentions it: a June 10, 2025 statement on the
  1,500-hour rule opens by applauding "President Trump and Transportation
  Secretary Duffy for their bold vision" to modernize the system, without a
  funding request. His other aviation releases announce FAA airport grants,
  the Safer Skies Act, and his work on the 1,500-hour rule. His one release
  since the ground stop, on November 19, is about New York's natural gas
  ban.

**In plain language:** He called for full funding in May 2025, as he says.
He then voted for H.R. 1's $12.5 billion and the 2026 spending law's $4
billion, and signed one letter asking appropriators to
target the most at-risk facilities. He has not written a bill to provide
the rest of the money, and he does not sit on a committee that decides it.
"Lead the fight" has so far meant voting yes when the money came up.

### Where the money stands now

- The $12.5 billion in H.R. 1 is what Secretary Duffy and the White House
  budget office call a "down-payment" (Roll Call). Duffy said at a November
  14 news conference that the agency has spent about one-third of the
  modernization budget, and told CNN the $12.5 billion is on track to be
  spent by the end of 2028 (CNN).
- On Thursday, November 12, the day before the outage, Duffy and budget
  director Russ Vought asked appropriators for $30.1 billion more. On
  Friday night Duffy and Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin
  raised the request to $50 billion, a figure Roll Call reports the
  administration "didn't fully flesh out."
- House Speaker Mike Johnson said on November 14 that he would add FAA
  funding to an upcoming "legislative package" (CNN). In the WBEN
  interview Rep. Langworthy said he hopes the House will have "a very
  serious discussion on this" when it returns this week.

### The majority question, in his words

Two of the three interviewers asked him why the money had not already been
provided.

On Fox News, the host asked: "Why didn't Republicans modernize the air
traffic control system while they had the majority?" He answered that he
had called for the full funding when Secretary Duffy proposed the plan,
and: "We got a piece of it in the budget reconciliation."

On WBEN, the host said: "We've got a Republican majority in the House.
There's a Republican majority in the Senate. There's a Republican
president... Who's holding it up?" He answered: "We made a $12 billion
down payment on this within the big, beautiful bill... It's something that
Congress is used to with projects like this, doing it in tranches. I don't
believe that we have the luxury on this."

His party has held the House, the Senate and the White House since January
2025. Democrats take the House and Senate majorities in January 2027 (CNN;
Roll Call).

### Then and now, on blame

In May 2025 he said the problem was partly the previous administration's:

> "That was before I came to Congress, but, you know, you had just mistaken
> priorities in that, all this DEI policy, DEI staffing, that all got baked
> into the cake. They could have taken that money and spent it on real
> modernization of what is critical infrastructure in this country."
> (Fox News Digital, May 7, 2025, on the 2021 infrastructure law; the same
> article reports he "clarified that he does not believe DEI policies
> 'necessarily' directly hit ATC.")

That 2021 law, signed by President Biden, did set aside money for air
traffic control. Of its $25 billion for the FAA, $5 billion went to the
agency's Facilities and Equipment account, $1 billion a year for fiscal
2022 through 2026. The law limits that money to listed uses, starting with
"replacing terminal and en route air traffic control facilities," "improving
air traffic control en route radar facilities," and "improving air traffic
control tower and terminal radar approach control facilities." The other
$20 billion went to airport infrastructure grants ($15 billion) and airport
terminals ($5 billion). Whether $5 billion was enough is a fair debate. The
law did not leave air traffic control out.

That money went forward. The FAA announced on April 27, 2022 that it had
"started investing the first $1 billion of $5 billion into the country's
air traffic control system." Federal spending data on USAspending.gov,
through September 30, 2026, show the full $5 billion available, $2.57 billion
obligated and $1.59 billion paid out. Rep. Langworthy took
office in January 2023, so he did not vote on the 2021 law. Once in office,
he voted for the FY2024 spending law, which President Biden signed and which
included $3.19 billion for the same FAA facilities and equipment account
(House roll call 64, March 6, 2024).

In the November 15, 2026 Fox News interview, asked whether he accepted the
FAA's explanation of the outage, he said the issue is "not about blame and,
you know, partisanship." Later in the same interview, asked why his party
had not modernized the system while it held the majority, he did not
mention the previous administration; he pointed to the $12.5 billion
"piece" in H.R. 1.

## The fair reading

He has a real aviation record. He voted for the $12.5 billion in H.R. 1
and for the FY2026 spending law's $4 billion and controller hiring. He
broke with most House Republicans to vote for the ROTOR Act. He joined seven Democrats from
New York and New Jersey on the May 2025 letter, the only Republican to do
so. On November 17 he stood with the families of the Flight 5342 victims
to push for the final aviation safety bill. In July 2023 the House adopted his bipartisan amendment to keep the
1,500-hour pilot training rule in the FAA reauthorization, and his office
says the final 2024 law kept it; he voted for the bill both times. His
caucus is bipartisan, with a Republican and a Democratic co-chair.

The structure of Congress matters too. Appropriators and leadership decide
FAA funding. A member outside those committees has votes, letters, bills
and public pressure. He has used votes, letters and public statements. He
has not used a bill.

At the November 17 event he set out his own standard: aviation safety "is
an issue that deserves constant attention, not just when tragedy strikes or
when an authorization is due."

What is missing from his posts is the gap between the two halves of what he
said in May 2025. He did call for frontloading the money. He also said he
would "absolutely lead the fight" to get it. The public record shows him
supporting the funding when it came to a vote. It does not show him
leading an effort to secure the rest.

**Verdict: MISSING CONTEXT.** "I called for the full funding back then"
holds up. "I have long called for wholesale upgrades" holds up from
November 2024. None of the posts mentions that, eighteen months after vowing to
lead the fight, he has introduced no bill to provide the funding, holds no
seat on the committees that would, and has signed one letter on air traffic
control funding, which another member led.

## Questions This Raises

1. Will he introduce or cosponsor a bill to fund the remaining
   modernization, at the $30.1 billion or $50 billion level, or another
   figure?
2. In the remaining weeks of the current majority, will the package Speaker
   Johnson described include the funding, and will the Aviation Safety
   Caucus take a position on it?
3. What did "lead the fight" involve between May 2025 and November 2026
   that is not in the public record? If there are letters, meetings or
   requests to leadership, will his office publish them?
4. Has the FAA responded to the November 2024 caucus letter on oversight of
   modernization projects?
