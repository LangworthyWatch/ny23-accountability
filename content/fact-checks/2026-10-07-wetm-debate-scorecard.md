## Why this matters

On October 6, WETM in Elmira put Rep. Nick Langworthy and Dr. Aaron Gies
at two podiums for 54 minutes, with questions neither had seen. It is the
only time this cycle the two have made checkable claims about the same
things, side by side, to the same voters. This entry checks what can be
checked from both podiums.

## How this was done

Every factual claim in the transcript was listed with its timestamp and
the speaker confirmed from the video. Opinions, forecasts and slogans
("moon race," "the most pro-worker tool," "common sense") were left
alone. Each remaining claim was taken to a primary source where one
exists: IRS, EIA, CBO, DHS, NYISO, the State Comptroller, the bill text
on govinfo. Where the only source is a news report or an advocacy
estimate, the table says so. Labels are the site's standard set, applied
the same way to both men. The challenger has positions and the
incumbent has a four-year record, so the counts are not meant to be
even and were not padded to look even.

## The fair reading

Each man was right about things the other side would rather he were
not. Langworthy's refund number is exactly what the IRS reports, his
deportation comparison holds, and Texas really has passed New York in
banking jobs. Gies's fuel prices are AAA's, his Canada figure is
Statistics Canada's, and the eight-of-twelve hospital number is the
Fiscal Policy Institute's.

Where each man went wrong, the pattern differs. Langworthy's errors
cluster around numbers he presented as settled: a blizzard death toll
of 69 that is 47, an electric bill that "doubled" when it rose 36%, a
"doubled" child credit that his law raised by $200, and a $2.5 million
health pilot described as "billions." Gies's errors cluster around
borrowed figures stretched past their source: a $1 trillion saving that
belongs to national spending rather than the Treasury, a Lancet
projection of all-cause child deaths restated as HIV deaths that have
already happened, a data-center pipeline that would add most of the
state's load but not double it, and a minimum-wage bill described from
memory of an earlier version.

## Questions this raises

1. For Rep. Langworthy: what is the source for 69 blizzard deaths, for
   the "48 rate increases," and for the claim that the Rural Health
   Transformation Program has plans in place for Arnot?
2. For Dr. Gies: what is the source for 28 pharmacy closures, and for the
   statement that Rep. Langworthy called data centers a local issue?
3. For both: the debate's one shared factual dispute, whether nursing
   homes and pharmacies are closing elsewhere, was settled by the record
   in Gies's favor. Will the Representative correct it?
