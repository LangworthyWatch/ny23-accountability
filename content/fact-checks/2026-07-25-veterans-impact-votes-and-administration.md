## Why This Matters for NY-23

NY-23 is one of the more veteran-dense districts in New York. The federal decisions that determine whether a veteran here gets a disability rating, how long a claim takes, and how much a VA home loan costs were all in play between May and July of 2026, and Rep. Langworthy cast recorded votes on every one of them.

This page documents what those votes would do, what the Department of Veterans Affairs is doing at the same time, and — importantly — the places where the record does **not** support the criticism being made of him.

**On the biggest single question, toxic exposure funding, his votes went the other way from what you may have heard.** We start there, because getting that wrong would make everything after it easy to dismiss.

---

## A. Where the Record Shows Support

*This section exists because an accountability page that only prints the damaging half is not a record, it is an argument.*

**Toxic exposure funding went up, and he voted for it.**

The Cost of War Toxic Exposures Fund is the account that pays for PACT Act care — the burn-pit, Agent Orange, and other exposure-related conditions the 2022 law made presumptive.

| Fiscal year | Toxic Exposures Fund |
|---|---|
| FY2026 enacted | **$52,676,000,000** |
| FY2027 House bill (H.R. 8469) | **$54,593,000,000** |
| Change | **+$1,917,000,000 (+3.6%)** |

FY2026 was funded at **100% of the President's request**. The FY2027 House figure also equals the request exactly, and the bill carries a further $53.7 billion advance for FY2028.

**Langworthy voted YES on H.R. 8469** (Roll Call 175, May 15, 2026, passed 400–15).

He also voted Yes on S. 2393, the VA Major Medical Facility Authorization Act (Roll 180, May 20, 405–5), and is an **original cosponsor of the Major Richard Star Act** (H.R. 2102, since March 14, 2025), which would end the offset between retirement pay and disability compensation for combat-injured retirees.

**In plain language:** anyone telling you Langworthy voted to cut PACT Act funding is wrong. That fund grew, and he voted for the bill that grew it.

*Sources: VA FY2027 Budget in Brief; H.R. 8469 as passed; clerk.house.gov Roll Calls 175 and 180; congress.gov cosponsor roster for H.R. 2102.*

## B. Where the Record Shows Cuts — And Who Pays Them

*The bill at the center of this is H.R. 9237, the Take Care of America's Veterans Act. It expands some benefits and pays for them by reducing others. The veterans who pay are not today's veterans. They are tomorrow's.*

### What Section 108 would change

Two disability ratings are rewritten. Compared against the rating schedule in force today:

| Condition | Current law | Under H.R. 9237 |
|---|---|---|
| **Sleep apnea** | **50%** rating when the veteran requires a CPAP machine | **0%** if treatment controls it. 50% only if treatment is ineffective or unusable *and* there is no organ damage. 100% only with organ damage |
| **Tinnitus** | Flat **10%** for recurrent tinnitus | **No separate compensable rating**, except 10% when paired with service-connected hearing loss that is otherwise noncompensable |

More than **760,000** veterans are currently rated for sleep apnea and more than **3.5 million** for tinnitus.

### Who it applies to

The bill applies "with respect to claims filed after the date of the enactment," and states that existing compensation may not be reduced. Supporters emphasize this. It is accurate as far as it goes.

**But it is contested, and honestly so.** DAV argues the new criteria would also govern "any reassessments or reevaluations of existing claims" — because a veteran already rated who files for an increase is filing a new claim. Both readings fit the text. The supporting organizations' own letter effectively concedes the ambiguity by urging that the changes apply "only to future claims **or future requests for increased ratings**."

**In plain language:** if you are rated today and never file again, the bill does not touch you. If you are a young service member who will file a claim after this becomes law, or a current veteran whose condition worsens and who seeks a higher rating, you would be rated under the new, tighter rules.

### The other offset

**Section 103(b) raises VA home loan fees**, amending the fee table from 0.50 to **1.42** and from 0.50 to **1.0**. That is a direct cost increase on the home loan benefit, paid by veterans using it.

### His seven votes

Langworthy sits on the House Rules Committee, which decides what reaches the floor and whether amendments are allowed. He cast four recorded votes there and three on the floor:

| Date | Body | Question | His vote | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jun. 23 | Rules | Make in order the Takano amendment replacing H.R. 9237 with the **Major Richard Star Act**, offset with unobligated defense funds | **Nay** | Defeated 4–8 |
| Jun. 23 | Rules | Report the rule | **Yea** | Adopted 8–4 |
| Jul. 13 | Rules | Strike the closed rule for H.R. 9237 | **Nay** | Defeated 4–6 |
| Jul. 13 | Rules | Report the replacement rule | **Yea** | Adopted 6–4 |
| Jul. 14 | Floor | Previous question / adopt H.Res. 1423 (closed rule) | **Yea / Aye** | 217–210 / 215–211 |
| **Jul. 16** | **Floor** | **Deluzio motion to recommit: strike Section 108 and the loan fee increase, pay for the bill with unobligated defense funds instead** | **Nay** | **Failed 210–211** |

**That motion failed by one vote.** Three Republicans voted for it. Langworthy was not among them.

Because the rule was **closed**, no member could offer an amendment on the floor. The motion to recommit was the only remaining route to strip the offsets, and it was the vote he cast against.

Immediately afterward the Speaker postponed proceedings and pulled the bill. **H.R. 9237 has not passed the House.**

*Sources: rules.house.gov record votes 369, 373, 385, 389; clerk.house.gov Roll Calls 236, 237, 249; H.R. 9237 as introduced, Sections 103(b) and 108; 38 C.F.R. §4.97 DC 6847 and §4.87 DC 6260; Congressional Record, Jul. 16, 2026, p. H4613.*

### The veterans organizations are split, and we are not going to flatten that

**Opposed to the offset** (June 24 coalition letter): **DAV, VFW, IAVA**, Jewish War Veterans, Marine Corps League, National Organization of Veterans' Advocates, National Veterans Legal Services Program, and the Reserve Organization of America. VFW National Commander Carol Whitmore: "A grateful nation pays its debts to veterans; it does not send them the invoice." DAV called Section 108 a "poison pill."

**Supporting passage anyway** (June 29 coalition letter): **The American Legion, MOAA, Wounded Warrior Project, Vietnam Veterans of America, AMVETS, TAPS**, and others — on the ground that the bill is a net expansion. Their letter states plainly: "Legislating changes to the Veterans Affairs Schedule for Rating Disabilities is not a standard path, and under ordinary circumstances it is not an approach we would support."

Even within the supporting camp there is dissent: the American Legion's **Department of Texas** and three individual posts filed opposition into the Congressional Record, breaking with their national organization.

**On the widely-cited $57 billion:** that figure is **not a Congressional Budget Office score.** No CBO estimate for H.R. 9237 exists in the bill status record, the Rules Committee file, or anywhere else it would normally appear — and the bill received **no committee report and no markup**, going straight from introduction to Rules. The $57 billion is VFW's figure, attributed to VA estimates. The American Legion cites a range of $19–58 billion. Treat it as an advocacy estimate, not an official score.

## C. The Same Appropriations Bill Cuts the Offices That Process Claims

*This is the part that does not appear in the press releases about H.R. 8469.*

The FY2027 MilCon-VA bill raises the Toxic Exposures Fund. It also sets four accounts **below** their FY2026 enacted levels:

| Account | FY2026 enacted | FY2027 House bill | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Information Technology Systems | $5,919,000,000 | $5,454,000,000 | **−$465,000,000** |
| General Operating Expenses, VBA | $3,881,000,000 | $3,744,000,000 | **−$137,000,000** |
| Medical and Prosthetic Research | $945,000,000 | $900,000,000 | **−$45,000,000** |
| Office of Inspector General | $296,000,000 | $290,000,000 | **−$6,000,000** |

General Operating Expenses for the Veterans Benefits Administration is the account that pays the people who **adjudicate disability claims**. Information Technology is the account that runs the systems those claims move through. The Office of Inspector General is the watchdog.

The committee's own report expresses concern that "any recent reduction in full-time positions will not adversely impact" claims processing.

**In plain language:** the fund that pays for toxic-exposure care grew. The offices that decide whether a veteran qualifies for it, and the systems that move the paperwork, shrank.

*Source: H. Rept. 119-622, Apr. 23, 2026; H.R. 8469 as passed.*

## D. What the Administration Is Doing at the Same Time

*Votes are only half of it. The other half is execution, and that is the executive branch.*

### The workforce is shrinking, but not the way critics usually say

VA's own monthly Workforce Dashboard:

| As of | Total VA | VHA (health care) | VBA (benefits) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dec. 31, 2025 | 439,736 | 392,575 | 30,695 |
| May 31, 2026 | **434,894** | **389,288** | **29,682** |
| Change in 2026 | **−4,842** | **−3,287** | **−1,013** |

Fiscal year to date: **18,305 hires against 24,975 losses.** VHA physicians are down 776. **VBA claims examiners are down 1,102.**

**The mechanism matters, and we are going to be precise about it.** VA states — accurately — that its VHA restructuring will eliminate no offices or positions, and that no employees were laid off in its payroll consolidation. We found **no 2026 reduction-in-force, buyout, or deferred-resignation announcement** in any official VA or OPM source.

Both things are true at once: no one is being fired, and the workforce is falling, because people are leaving faster than they are replaced. Anyone describing this as mass firings is wrong. Anyone describing the workforce as stable is also wrong.

### The claims backlog

VBA's Monday Morning Workload Report, July 18, 2026:

- **585,335** claims pending
- **67,849** backlogged beyond 125 days — **11.59%**
- Average **64.95 days** to complete

Set that against VBA losing 1,102 claims examiners in the same period, and an appropriations bill that reduces VBA's operating account by $137 million.

### Staffing shortages

VA's Inspector General found **4,434 severe occupational staffing shortages** across **all 139** VA medical centers, up roughly 50% year over year. **That determination is the FY2025 report, issued August 2025** — the FY2026 version does not yet exist. It is frequently misdated to 2026; we are dating it correctly.

*Sources: VA Workforce Dashboard; VBA Monday Morning Workload Report, Jul. 18, 2026; VA OIG FY2025 Determination of Staffing Shortages, Aug. 12, 2025; digital.va.gov/rise.*

## E. What This Means Locally

Honest answer: **less than a district-impact story would want.**

We searched for NY-23 VA facility closures, service reductions, or staffing cuts in 2026 and found none. The Bath VA Medical Center in Steuben County — the facility most NY-23 veterans use — has **no 2026 cuts story**. The widely circulated report about probationary firings at Bath is from **February 2025**, and multiple search engines misdate it to 2026. The only 2026 Bath item runs the other way: a May 18, 2026 VA Finger Lakes announcement of infrastructure funding for a boiler plant generator, sidewalk replacement, and other upgrades.

We also could not read `stargazette.com`, `the-leader.com`, or `mytwintiers.com`, all of which block automated access and have no archive captures. Local coverage may exist there that we cannot see, so we are not claiming a comprehensive local-media negative.

> "That commitment must be reflected in policy, not just words. Veterans deserve care they can access, benefits delivered on time, and a Department of Veterans Affairs that works for veterans rather than bureaucrats."

In the same window:

- He cast **seven recorded votes** keeping the sleep apnea, tinnitus, and home loan fee offsets in H.R. 9237 and blocking every procedural route to strike them.
- He made **zero floor remarks** during the entire H.R. 9237 debate.
- His office issued **no press release** on veterans, the VA, or any of the five veterans measures he voted on between May and July 2026.

- There was **no Memorial Day press release** in 2026.

## A Note on What the Record Does — and Doesn't — Show

**Documented:**
- Toxic exposure funding rose $1.9 billion in the bill he voted for; PACT Act funding was not cut.
- Seven recorded votes preserved offsets that reduce future disability ratings for sleep apnea and tinnitus and raise VA home loan fees.
- The decisive motion to strike those offsets failed 210–211 with his vote against.
- The same appropriations bill reduces VBA operating expenses, IT, research, and the Inspector General below FY2026 levels.
- VA's workforce fell 4,842 in 2026, including 1,102 claims examiners, through attrition.
- No veterans communication from his office in the May–July 2026 window.

**Not documented, and not claimed:**
- Any cut to PACT Act or toxic exposure funding. There was none.
- Any reduction to benefits veterans currently receive. Section 108 is prospective; the dispute is over whether "prospective" reaches reassessments.
- Any mass firing of VA employees. The decline is attrition.
- Any NY-23 facility closure or service reduction in 2026.
- Any statement from Langworthy explaining these votes, because none exists.

**On fairness:** H.R. 9237 is a real trade-off, not a simple betrayal. It expands benefits for some veterans by reducing future benefits for others, and serious veterans organizations landed on both sides of it. What this page documents is which side of that trade Langworthy chose, at each of seven decision points, and that he has not explained the choice to the district.

---

## Verdict: DOCUMENTED PATTERN

Langworthy voted to increase toxic exposure funding, and that deserves to be said plainly. He also used his Rules Committee seat four times and his floor vote three times to preserve a financing mechanism that pays for expanded benefits by reducing future disability ratings for sleep apnea and tinnitus and by raising VA home loan fees — voting against the one motion that could have stripped it, which failed by a single vote. The same appropriations bill he supported cuts the offices that adjudicate claims, while VA loses claims examiners to attrition and 67,849 claims sit beyond 125 days.

He has issued no public statement on any of it.
