## Why this matters

Volunteer and small-city fire departments depend on federal equipment
grants, and two in Cattaraugus County got them: the City of Olean, for
radios, and Ellicottville, for air packs, according to his posts. In
October, Rep. Langworthy said he was "proud to have helped secure" both.

That wording is closer to the record than the "I secured" he has used in
other posts, and the record supports it: he voted for the spending laws
that funded the program. What the posts leave out is how the winners were
chosen, and that the Ellicottville grant's award period began a year ago.

## Where the money came from

**Both are FEMA Assistance to Firefighters Grants (AFG).**

| | Olean | Ellicottville |
|---|---|---|
| Recipient | City of Olean | Ellicottville Fire District Commission |
| Amount | $70,714 | $212,992.38 |
| Grant round | Fiscal 2025 (per the city's application authorization; not yet in USAspending) | Fiscal 2024 |
| Date | Announced in his release, September 28, 2026 | Award period began September 30, 2025 (USAspending) |
| Equipment | Radios | Air packs (per his post) |

*Sources: his September 28, 2026 release and a City of Olean Finance
Committee agenda (Olean); USAspending.gov award EMW-2024-FG-03322
(Ellicottville).*

The amounts match his release (Olean) and USAspending (Ellicottville);
"$210,000" rounds the Ellicottville award down. We could not check the
posts' equipment counts (15 radios, 18 air packs) against the award
records.

**FEMA chose the winners by score.** FEMA's notice for the fiscal 2024
round says all applications are "electronically pre-scored and ranked,"
and the top-ranked ones are "scored competitively by no less than three
members of a Peer Review Panel" made up of "fire service
representatives." The two scores count equally, and FEMA then reviews
the highest-ranked applications before making awards. FEMA's fiscal 2025
notice also uses pre-scoring and a peer review panel "composed of fire
service personnel." The process FEMA describes gives members of Congress
no role in choosing winners.

**Olean's applications were the city's to make.** A June 23, 2026 Olean
Finance Committee agenda, the day after FEMA's application deadline,
lists referrals "to authorize the Mayor to submit a Grant Application" to
FEMA "under the Fiscal Year 2025 Assistance to Firefighter's Grant
Program," one "for funding associated with the purchase of a radios" and
one for "the purchase of a fire engine." The agenda does not show when
the city submitted either application.

## What "helped" rests on

**He voted to fund the program both years.**

- The fiscal 2024 round, which paid for Ellicottville's grant, was funded
  with $324 million in the Department of Homeland Security Appropriations
  Act, 2024, part of the spending package enacted as P.L. 118-47 (H.R.
  2882). Rep. Langworthy voted yes on the House's final vote on that
  package, on March 22, 2024 (286-134).
- The fiscal 2025 round, which paid for Olean's radios, was funded by the
  Full-Year Continuing Appropriations and Extensions Act, 2025, which
  FEMA's notice cites as its appropriation authority. He voted yes on that
  bill on March 11, 2025 (217-213).

**His office has helped departments apply.** His releases say he hosted
FEMA officials for virtual grant-writing seminars for local departments,
including one in June 2026.

**They were not earmarks.** Neither grant appears in his Community Project
Funding requests for fiscal years 2024 through 2027, which list City of
Olean projects for water and police but no Olean or Ellicottville fire
project. He has requested fire projects elsewhere in the district,
including a Wendelville Fire Company station (fiscal 2026), Tioga County
radios (fiscal 2026) and a Hornell fire truck (fiscal 2027).

In plain language: he voted for the money that funds these grants, and
his office has run sessions to help departments apply. The departments
wrote the applications, and FEMA scored them, by computer and by
fire-service peer review, before making the awards. "Helped secure" is a
fair description of his part; "secured" would not have been.

## What the posts leave out

**The Ellicottville grant's award period began a year ago.** USAspending
shows it beginning September 30, 2025, from the fiscal 2024 round. The
post does not say when the grant was awarded. His office posted no House
press releases between August 29 and November 19, 2025, and none of his
releases announced any fiscal 2024 fire grant, so the absence of an
announcement for this one says little. None of his House press releases
mentions Ellicottville.

**Olean also sought authorization to apply for a fire engine.** The same
June 23 agenda lists that second referral. His September 28 release lists
only the $70,714 for Olean, and we found no announced award for a fire
engine. FEMA's notice said selections would be made by September 30,
2026.

*Context: Olean has also received other FEMA fire grants. They include a
$569,903 staffing (SAFER) grant whose award period began in March 2026
(EMW-2024-FF-00634); a $21,904.76 Assistance to Firefighters grant from
the fiscal 2023 round, which he announced on August 21, 2024; and
Assistance to Firefighters grants in four straight rounds, 2014 through
2017, before he took office.*

---

## The fair reading

- **"Helped secure" is accurate.** He voted for the money, and his office
  has helped departments apply. That is a real, if indirect, role.
- **The wording is closer to the record this time.** Compared with "I
  secured" in other posts (see the
  [2026 credit-claims roundup](/fact-checks/2026-10-20-credit-claims-2026-roundup/)),
  "helped secure" matches what he did.
- **Showing the equipment is not a problem in itself.** The post says the
  grant "delivered" the air packs; highlighting equipment once it is in
  service is common in members' announcements.

## Questions this raises
