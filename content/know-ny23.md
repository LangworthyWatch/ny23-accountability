**Why this matters.** Most entries on this site test one claim against one record. This page is the background for all of them: who lives in NY-23, what they do for work, and how much of the district's income comes from government benefits. When a vote changes Social Security, Medicare, Medicaid, SNAP, veterans' benefits or trade policy, the numbers below show how large a share of the district is in the path of that vote. Each figure comes from the Census Bureau or the Bureau of Economic Analysis, and each is set beside the same figure for New York State and the United States.

*The district here is NY-23 on its current lines, the Legislature's 2024 map, used in the 2024 and 2026 elections. For how those lines were drawn, see [Who Drew NY-23](/fact-checks/2026-11-13-who-drew-ny23-redistricting/).*

---

## At a glance

- **Older than the state and the nation.** Nearly one resident in four (23.8%) is 65 or older, compared with 19.3% statewide. No New York district has a higher share; NY-3 and NY-24 are within the margin of error.
- **Shrinking.** The towns and cities wholly inside the district lost about 12,100 people from 2020 to 2025 (1.6%). Only 29 of those 173 places grew.
- **A manufacturing district.** One working resident in eight (12.2%) works in manufacturing, more than twice the state share.
- **Lower incomes, cheaper homes.** Median household income is $78,436, about $10,800 below the state median. The median home value, $209,900, is less than half the state figure.
- **Heavily reliant on benefit income.** Two households in five (39.7%) receive Social Security. In the five counties wholly inside the district, government benefits and other transfer payments made up 30% of all personal income in 2024, compared with 18% statewide and nationally.

## A. The people

*Age is the single fact that shapes most of the rest of this page: an older district has more retirees, more Medicare enrollees and more Social Security income.*

| Median age | 44.5 (± 0.7) | 39.8 | 39.5 |

| Under 18 | 19.5% (± 0.6) | 19.5% | 21.0% |
| White, not Hispanic | 87.3% (± 0.6) | 50.3% | 55.0% |
| Veterans, share of adults | 7.5% (± 0.5) | 3.3% | 5.9% |

*Source: Census Bureau, 2025 American Community Survey 1-year estimates, tables B01003, B01002, B01001, B03002 and B21001, for the 119th Congress district. Figures in parentheses are the Census Bureau's 90% margins of error; derived shares use the Bureau's approximation formulas.*

**In plain language:** the district is about five years older than the state by median age. The share of residents 65 and older is the highest of New York's 26 districts, though NY-3 and NY-24 are statistically tied with it. Veterans make up more than twice the state share of adults; only NY-21 and NY-24 are comparable.

Farming, forestry, fishing and hunting employ about 1 working resident in 90. Unemployment was below the state rate in 2025.

About 1 household in 19 has no internet access at all.

## D. Benefits and coverage

*This section counts people and households who receive each benefit. It does not measure whether a benefit is adequate, or how any vote changed it.*

| Measure (2025) | NY-23 | New York | United States |
|---|---|---|---|
| Households receiving Social Security | 39.7% (± 1.0) | 33.0% | 32.9% |
| Residents with Medicare | 25.1% (± 0.5) | 19.8% | 19.6% |
| Residents with Medicaid or other means-tested public coverage | 19.7% (± 0.8) | 25.7% | 16.6% |
| Households receiving SNAP | 13.3% (± 1.0) | 15.9% | 11.5% |
| Households receiving Supplemental Security Income | 6.1% (± 0.8) | 6.3% | 5.1% |
| Residents without health insurance | 3.4% (± 0.3) | 4.7% | 8.4% |

*Source: 2025 American Community Survey 1-year estimates, tables B19055, C27006, C27007, B22001, B19056 and B27001. Coverage shares are of the civilian noninstitutionalized population; a person can hold more than one type of coverage.*

**In plain language:** two households in five collect Social Security and one resident in four is on Medicare, both well above the state and national shares, which follows from the district's age. About one resident in five has Medicaid or similar public coverage, fewer than statewide but more than nationally. About 13 households in 100 receive SNAP.

### How much of the district's income comes from benefits

*The Bureau of Economic Analysis publishes personal income by county, not by district. This table uses the five counties wholly inside NY-23 and lists the four partial counties separately.*

| Area | 2004 | 2014 | 2024 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Five whole counties combined | 23.5% | 26.8% | 30.0% |
| Allegany | 24.1% | 28.6% | 31.9% |
| Cattaraugus | 23.9% | 27.4% | 30.5% |
| Chautauqua | 25.2% | 29.0% | 31.8% |
| Chemung | 23.7% | 25.4% | 29.2% |
| Tioga | 18.1% | 21.7% | 25.0% |
| Erie (part in district) | 19.2% | 21.6% | 22.1% |
| Niagara (part in district) | 21.4% | 25.3% | 25.8% |
| Schuyler (part in district) | 22.9% | 26.2% | 30.7% |
| Steuben (part in district) | 22.3% | 23.5% | 26.6% |
| New York State | 17.5% | 17.4% | 18.1% |
| United States | 14.2% | 17.2% | 18.3% |

*Source: Bureau of Economic Analysis, table CAINC4 (personal income by major component), personal current transfer receipts (line 47) divided by personal income (line 10); last updated February 5, 2026. In 2024 the five whole counties had $5.94 billion in transfer receipts out of $19.78 billion in personal income. "Partial" counties are shown whole-county.*

BEA defines transfer receipts as "income payments to persons for which no current services are performed": Social Security, Medicare, Medicaid, veterans' benefits, SNAP, SSI, unemployment insurance and similar payments. In these five counties, 96.7% of the 2022 total was paid by governments; the rest came from businesses and nonprofits. In the most recent county detail BEA publishes (table CAINC35, for 2022), the receipts in the five whole counties broke down this way:

| Share of transfer receipts, 2022 | Five whole counties | New York | United States |
|---|---|---|---|
| Social Security | 33.4% | 25.9% | 30.3% |
| Medicaid and other public medical care | 23.0% | 31.0% | 20.7% |
| Medicare | 20.6% | 21.5% | 23.1% |
| Income maintenance (SNAP, SSI, EITC and other aid) | 12.5% | 12.3% | 11.2% |
| Veterans' benefits | 4.1% | 1.6% | 4.3% |
| All other | 6.4% | 7.7% | 10.4% |

*Source: Bureau of Economic Analysis, table CAINC35 (personal current transfer receipts), 2022; last updated November 16, 2023. "All other" is the remainder of total transfer receipts, including unemployment insurance, education assistance and transfers from businesses and nonprofits.*

**In plain language:** in the counties wholly inside NY-23, about 30 cents of every dollar of personal income in 2024 came from benefits and other transfer payments, up from about 23.5 cents in 2004. Statewide and nationally the figure is about 18 cents. Social Security is the largest piece, followed by Medicaid and Medicare. The share has grown in every county in the district since 2004.
