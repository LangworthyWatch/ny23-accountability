**Why this matters.** Most entries on this site test one claim against one record. This page is the background for all of them: who lives in NY-23, what they do for work, and how much of its income comes from Social Security and other government benefits. When a vote changes Social Security, Medicare, Medicaid, SNAP, veterans' benefits or trade policy, the numbers below show how many residents and households those programs reach here. Each figure comes from the Census Bureau or the Bureau of Economic Analysis, and district figures are set beside the same figure for New York State and the United States.

*The district here is NY-23 on its current lines, the Legislature's 2024 map, used in the 2024 and 2026 elections. For how those lines were drawn, see [Who Drew NY-23](/fact-checks/2026-11-13-who-drew-ny23-redistricting/).*

---

## At a glance

- **Older than the state and the nation.** Nearly one resident in four (23.8%) is 65 or older, compared with 19.3% statewide. That is the highest estimate among New York's 26 districts; NY-3 is statistically tied.
- **Fewer people than in 2020.** The towns, cities and reservation areas wholly inside the district lost about 12,100 people from 2020 to 2025 (1.6%). Only 29 of those 173 places grew.
- **Manufacturing employs one working resident in eight.** That share (12.2%) is more than twice the state's.
- **Lower incomes, cheaper homes.** Median household income is $78,436, about $10,800 below the state median. The median home value, $209,900, is less than half the state figure.
- **Social Security is a larger share of income.** Two households in five (39.7%) receive Social Security. It supplies 10.5% of all household income in the district, against 6.3% statewide and 7.2% nationally.
- **Unemployment and uninsured rates are below the state's.** Unemployment was 3.7% in 2025 (5.6% statewide). 3.4% of residents lack health insurance (4.7% statewide). Three households in four own their home.

*Start with age: the district's share of residents 65 or older is the highest estimate in the state.*

**In plain language:** the district is about five years older than the state by median age. The share of residents 65 and older is the highest estimate among New York's 26 districts. NY-3 is statistically tied; NY-24, NY-19 and NY-1 are within one percentage point. Veterans make up more than twice the state share of adults; only NY-21 and NY-24 are comparable.

The largest county losses since 2010 were in Chemung, Chautauqua and Schuyler.

## D. Benefits and coverage

*This section counts people and households who receive each benefit. It does not measure whether a benefit is adequate, or how any vote changed it.*

| Measure (2025) | NY-23 | New York | United States |
|---|---|---|---|
| Households receiving Social Security | 39.7% (± 1.0) | 33.0% | 32.9% |
| Residents with Medicare | 25.1% (± 0.5) | 19.8% | 19.6% |
| Residents with Medicaid or other means-tested public coverage | 19.7% (± 0.8) | 25.7% | 16.6% |
| Households receiving SNAP | 13.3% (± 1.0) | 15.9% | 11.5% |
| Households receiving Supplemental Security Income | 6.1% (± 0.8) | 6.3% | 5.1% |
| Residents without health insurance | 3.4% (± 0.3) | 4.7% | 8.4% |

*Source: 2025 American Community Survey 1-year estimates, tables B19055, C27006, C27007, B22001, B19056 and B27001. Coverage shares are of the civilian noninstitutionalized population; a person can hold more than one type of coverage.*

**In plain language:** two households in five collect Social Security and one resident in four is on Medicare, both well above the state and national shares, consistent with the district's age. About one resident in five has Medicaid or similar public coverage, fewer than statewide but more than nationally. About 13 households in 100 receive SNAP.

**In plain language:** of every dollar of household income in NY-23, about 73 cents comes from work, against 80 cents statewide. Social Security supplies about 10.5 cents, against 6.3 cents statewide and 7.2 cents nationally.

### A broader measure, for the five whole counties

*The Bureau of Economic Analysis publishes personal income by county, not by district. Its measure is broader than the Census survey's: it counts what Medicare and Medicaid pay for residents' medical care as part of personal income. The table uses the five counties wholly inside NY-23, which hold about half the district's residents (374,184 of the 749,487 people in places wholly inside the district in 2025). The Erie County towns in the district, with 281,317 residents, are not in the five-county figure.*

| Area | 2004 | 2014 | 2024 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Five whole counties combined | 23.5% | 26.8% | 30.0% |
| Allegany | 24.1% | 28.6% | 31.9% |
| Cattaraugus | 23.9% | 27.4% | 30.5% |
| Chautauqua | 25.2% | 29.0% | 31.8% |
| Chemung | 23.7% | 25.4% | 29.2% |
| Tioga | 18.1% | 21.7% | 25.0% |
| Erie (whole county shown) | 19.2% | 21.6% | 22.1% |
| Niagara (whole county shown) | 21.4% | 25.3% | 25.8% |
| Schuyler (whole county shown) | 22.9% | 26.2% | 30.7% |
| Steuben (whole county shown) | 22.3% | 23.5% | 26.6% |
| New York State | 17.5% | 17.4% | 18.1% |
| United States | 14.2% | 17.2% | 18.3% |

*Source: Bureau of Economic Analysis, table CAINC4 (personal income by major component), personal current transfer receipts (line 47) divided by personal income (line 10); last updated February 5, 2026. In 2024 the five whole counties had $5.94 billion in transfer receipts out of $19.78 billion in personal income. For Erie, Niagara, Schuyler and Steuben, only part of the county is in NY-23; the figures are for the whole county.*

BEA defines transfer receipts as "income payments to persons for which no current services are performed and net insurance settlements": Social Security, Medicare, Medicaid, veterans' benefits, SNAP, SSI, unemployment insurance and similar payments. In these five counties, 96.7% of the 2022 total was paid by governments to individuals; the rest was business payments to individuals and transfers received by nonprofit institutions. In the most recent county detail BEA publishes (table CAINC35, for 2022), the receipts in the five whole counties broke down this way:

| Share of transfer receipts, 2022 | Five whole counties | New York | United States |
|---|---|---|---|
| Social Security | 33.4% | 25.9% | 30.3% |
| Medicaid and other public medical care | 23.0% | 31.0% | 20.7% |
| Medicare | 20.6% | 21.5% | 23.1% |
| Income maintenance (SNAP, SSI, EITC and other aid) | 12.5% | 12.3% | 11.2% |
| Veterans' benefits | 4.1% | 1.6% | 4.3% |
| All other | 6.4% | 7.7% | 10.4% |

*Source: Bureau of Economic Analysis, table CAINC35 (personal current transfer receipts), 2022; last updated November 16, 2023. "All other" is the remainder of total transfer receipts, including unemployment insurance, education assistance, business payments to individuals and transfers received by nonprofit institutions.*

Medical benefits (Medicare, Medicaid and military medical insurance) were 43.8% of the five-county total in 2022. BEA's table definitions describe them as payments for "care provided to individuals"; for Medicaid, payments "to the vendors" under a "federally assisted, state-administered" program. They are counted as residents' income, but the money is paid for their care rather than to them.

**In plain language:** in the five counties wholly inside NY-23, home to about half its residents, about 30 cents of every dollar of personal income in 2024 came from government benefits and other transfer payments, counting the medical care that Medicare and Medicaid pay for. That is up from 23.5 cents in 2004. Statewide and nationally the figure is about 18 cents. Social Security is the largest piece, followed by Medicaid and Medicare. The share has grown in every county in the district since 2004.
